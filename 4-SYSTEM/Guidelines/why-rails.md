# Why rails — the Wikipedia analogy in full

Rails are to a classical text what Wikipedia is to general knowledge: a curated, structured, citation-dense distillation of a much larger body of source material, organised so that AI tools can produce reliable, traceable output without sifting through the raw tradition on every call.

It's worth understanding *why* AI developers favour Wikipedia as a source of fact in the first place. Almost every fact on Wikipedia exists somewhere else on the internet — often in more depth and from more authoritative sources. But Wikipedia has been gathered, standardised, cited, and continuously vetted by people who know what they're doing. That combination — high information density, uniform structure, built-in citations, neutral tone, open licensing, clean data, and cross-lingual links — makes Wikipedia cheaper to process, easier to verify against, and safer to build on than the raw web. Every row of the table below is a real, measurable advantage Wikipedia gives an AI pipeline over its alternatives (books, news articles, general web content).

A Railroads vault is built to give an AI tool that same set of advantages for one classical text. The rails for the Abhidhamma stand in the same relation to the commentary tradition as a Wikipedia article stands to the open web — they replicate Wikipedia's structural properties at the level of one verse, one section, or one concept. The table below maps the parallel category by category.

## Wikipedia ↔ Rails

| Category | Wikipedia | Rails (for one classical text) | AI Benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Information density** | Ultra-high. Purely objective, encyclopedic data stripped of narrative filler. | Ultra-high. Each rail distils the relevant commentary positions for one verse, section, or concept — morphology, attested senses, translator decisions — with no devotional framing or narrative apparatus. | Saves massive processing power and compute costs. Translation prompts stay within budget; the model isn't asked to wade through irrelevant material. |
| **Structure & formatting** | Highly standardised. Strict use of infoboxes, clear headers, and uniform layouts. | Highly standardised. Every rail follows the same schema: frontmatter, the disambiguation stack (Source Text → Traditional Interpretation → Morphological → Syntactic → Semantic Gloss → Translation Dynamics), and Obsidian block IDs. | Easy for AI pipelines to scrape, parse, and ingest automatically — no per-file parsing logic. |
| **Context-window fit** | Compact. Full topic summaries fit easily into limited LLM memory limits. | Compact. A verse's complete interpretive context fits in a few kilobytes; the raw commentaries for the same verse span thousands of pages across multiple languages and scripts. | Drastically reduces model confusion and memory overload. One verse loads in a single prompt; large transformations stay feasible. |
| **Fact verification** | Built-in citations. Every fact is explicitly linked to a primary source footnote. | Built-in citations. Every claim in a rail cites a specific block ID in `1-SOURCES/` (e.g. `1-SOURCES/Commentaries/sk-prajnakaramati.md#^1-1`). The citation chain `1-SOURCES → 2-RAILS → 3-TRANSFORMATIONS` is strict and one-way. | Powers accurate Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Every claim in a generated translation, study guide, or lesson plan traces back to a specific commentary passage. |
| **Writing tone** | Neutral (NPOV). Strictly objective, factual, non-emotional language. | Descriptive, not prescriptive. Rails record what each commentator attests; they don't pick a winner. Where commentators diverge, the divergence is flagged and attributed, never collapsed into editorial preference. | Prevents AI models from flattening competing traditions into a single editorial choice; surfaces ambiguity instead of papering over it. |
| **Legal compliance** | Open license. Protected under free Creative Commons frameworks. | Open. The vault sits in a public GitHub repo. Source texts cited are classical works long out of copyright; modern translations and commentaries are cited with full attribution and quoted within fair-use limits. | Safe for downstream tools to build on without legal liability or paywall friction. |
| **Data cleanliness** | Pre-vetted. Human editor communities continuously purge spam, vandalism, and typos. | Specialist-reviewed. Every rail moves through a `draft → partial → complete` lifecycle. Only `complete` rails — reviewed claim-by-claim by a domain specialist — feed downstream transformations. The LLM never marks its own output complete. | Eliminates expensive, time-consuming pre-cleaning. `complete` rails are trustable by construction. |
| **Cross-lingual links** | Interconnected. Matching concepts mapped across 300+ parallel languages. | Cross-lingual by design. Each verse anchors parallel files in Pāli, Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and modern translations through shared block IDs. `Local-Wiki/` pages assign language-neutral sense IDs; `Glossaries/` map term renderings per language pair. | Perfect for translation, alignment, and bilingual lookup; the same anchored structure works for every target language. |

## The bottom line

Anything an AI tool gains from training on or retrieving against Wikipedia — speed, cheapness, verifiability, neutrality, legal safety, cross-lingual coverage — a Railroads vault aims to give that same tool when it's asked to work on the text the vault serves. The vault is the Wikipedia of one classical text, built for AI consumption first and human reading second.
