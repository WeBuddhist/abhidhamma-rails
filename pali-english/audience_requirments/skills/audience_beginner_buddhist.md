# Audience Profile: Beginner Buddhist Readers

## Audience

Readers **new to Buddhism** who want to learn teachings and build vocabulary — newcomers to a centre, intro-course students, or motivated self-study readers.

**English is their second or third language** — not their main language. They are learning Buddhist ideas **through English**, not as native English speakers.

**Assume they:**

* are motivated to learn Buddhist ideas
* may know **Buddha**, **Dhamma**, **Nibbāna**, **Saṅgha** from talks or materials in their own language
* can follow clear explanatory English at an intermediate level
* do **not** yet know Abhidhamma analysis or Pāli grammar

**Do not assume they:** have native English fluency, retreat experience, Pāli study, or fluency in traditional commentarial categories.

---

## Translation Goal

Preserve **core Buddhist concepts** while keeping English **welcoming, teachable, and plain** — accurate enough to build on, accessible to an **L2/L3 reader**, not dumbed down into vagueness.

---

## Terminology

Retain important Buddhist concepts while lowering the barrier to entry. For each doctrinal sense, pick **one** rendering and reuse it **every time** that sense appears.

### Word-choice rules (beginner Buddhist)

| Rule | Do | Don't |
| ---- | -- | ----- |
| English level | Plain sentences; common words around Buddhist terms | Idioms, slang, academic English, long nested clauses |
| Core terms | Keep **Dhamma**, **Nibbāna**, **Saṅgha**, **kamma**, **jhāna** when the concept is central — introduce with clear nearby English | Replace every loanword with a vague synonym, or stack many loanwords without context |
| Abhidhamma | Gentle standard English ("mental factor", "aggregate", "hindrance") with consistency | Dense untranslated lists or scholar-only labels |
| Teaching tone | Wording that **introduces** the idea (still in translation, not commentary) | Assumed prior study of commentaries |
| Distinctions | Separate Sense Tags — beginners need stable vocabulary | Collapsing related but distinct senses |

### Examples (lemma, sense → rendering)

* citta, `mind_heart` → mind
* vedanā, `pleasant_unpleasant` → feeling (pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral)
* saṅkhāra, `mental_formation` → mental formations
* dhamma, `matter_thing` → mental or physical phenomenon
* dhamma, `teaching_discourse` → the Dhamma (teaching)
* kilesa, `defilement_impurity_unwholesome` → defilement / unwholesome mental state

For other doctrinal terms (e.g. saññā → perception), choose one consistent rendering and reuse it.

---

## Style

* **Educational** and **accessible** to an international English reader
* **Friendly** — not academic coldness, not childish simplification
* Clear sentences; prefer **short to medium** length over complex syntax
* **Stable vocabulary** — beginners learning Buddhism and English at once need repeated terms

---

## Explanatory Additions

Wording may **naturally** carry meaning (e.g. "clinging" vs "grasping" where the source distinguishes them).

Do not insert commentary blocks or footnotes in the translation body.

---

## Priority

1. **Understanding** of Buddhist ideas
2. **Accuracy**
3. **Learning value** — stable terms readers can reuse
4. **Readability**
