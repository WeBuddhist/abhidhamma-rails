## Tikamātikā

### Synthesis (original language)

Sabbāpi mātikāpadabhājanīyassa paṭhamabhāgavasena vavatthāpitā dvāvīsati tikā saddhiṃ satta dukasatehi pañcadasaparicchedavasena vavatthitā. Tikamātikā nāma kusalattikādivasena ādimhi ṭhitā, hetudukādayo dukā tato paraṃ. Sabbe tikadukā sappadesa-nippadesa-bhedena duvidhā honti.
(Raw/dhammasangani-atthakatha/1-0a-0.md)
(Raw/dhammasangani-mulatiika/1-0a-0.md)

Kusalattikassa anupubbapadavaṇṇanāyaṃ kusalasaddo ārogya-anavajja-cheka-sukhavipākātīsu atthesu dissati, idha pana ārogya-anavajja-sukhavipākesu pavattati. Dhammasaddo pariyatti-hetu-guṇa-nissattanijjīvatāsu dissati, idha pana nissattanijjīvatāyameva pavattati. Lakkhaṇato kusalā anavajjasukhavipākalakkhaṇā, akusalā sāvajjadukkhavipākalakkhaṇā, abyākatā avipākalakkhaṇā.
(Raw/dhammasangani-atthakatha/1-0a-0.md)
(Raw/dhammasangani-mulatiika/1-0a-0.md)
(Raw/dhammasangani-anutiika/1-0a-0.md)

Akusalasaddo na kusalābhāvamattavacanaṃ, na ca kusalehi tadaññamattavacanaṃ, atha kho kusalapaṭipakkhavacanaṃ — yathā loke ‘‘amittā’’ti, sāsane ‘‘alobho’’ti. Abyākatasaddopi tatiyo rāsi hutvā kusalākusalavinimuttasabhāvaṃ jotayati, na pana kusalākusalānaṃ avipākārahasabhāvaṃ āpādeti.
(Raw/dhammasangani-mulatiika/1-0a-0.md)
(Raw/dhammasangani-anutiika/1-0a-0.md)

Anavajjavacanena kusalānaṃ pavattisukhatā ca attasuddhi ca dīpīyate, sukhavipākavacanena vipākasukhatā ca visuddhavipākatā ca. Sāvajjavacanena akusalānaṃ pavattidukkhatā, dukkhavipākavacanena vipākadukkhatā. Avipākavacanaṃ abyākatānaṃ sabhāvaṃ kathayati.
(Raw/dhammasangani-mulatiika/1-0a-0.md)
(Raw/dhammasangani-anutiika/1-0a-0.md)

Codako tikādīnaṃ chakkacatukkabhāvāpattidosaṃ codetvāpi taṃ paṭisedhitaṃ — māsa-saddo viya eko dhammasaddo bhinnatthe vattumarahati, tasmā kusala-dhamma-saddānaṃ ekatthatā na anuññātā, nānatthatā ca chakkacatukkabhāvaṃ na āpādeti.
(Raw/dhammasangani-atthakatha/1-0a-0.md)
(Raw/dhammasangani-mulatiika/1-0a-0.md)
(Raw/dhammasangani-anutiika/1-0a-0.md)

Vedanāttikādīsu sukha-dukkha-adukkhamasukha-saddānaṃ atthe vibhāgaṃ datvā idha sukhavedanāyameva dukkhavedanāyañca daṭṭhabbo. Sanidassanattike ‘‘sahanidassanena’’ti sanidassanā, ‘‘sahapaṭighena’’ti sappaṭighā vuttā. Evaṃ sabbatikāpadāni anupubbapadavaṇṇanavasena niṭṭhitā.
(Raw/dhammasangani-atthakatha/1-0a-0.md)

### Divergences

- **Kusalassa vacanattho** — Aṭṭhasālinī ‘‘kucchite pāpake dhamme salayanti calayanti kampenti viddhaṃsentī’’ti niddhāretvā paccayehi kusalattaṃ ⚑; Mūlaṭīkā taṃ purimassa pacchimaṃ atthavacananti vā salanassa tīṇi pariyāyavibhāgāni tadaṅgappahānādīhi vā yojetabbānīti vyākhyāti ⚑. Ubhopi kusala-saddo lavanārahā ‘‘kusena ñāṇena lātabbā’’ti pakkhaṃ anumodanti, mūlaṭīkā ñāṇavippayuttānampi kusaluppattiyaṃ ñāṇaṃ upanissayapaccayoti adhikaṃ vadati.
  (Raw/dhammasangani-atthakatha/1-0a-0.md)
  (Raw/dhammasangani-mulatiika/1-0a-0.md)

- **Abyākata-saddassa vyutpatti** — Aṭṭhasālinī ‘‘kusalākusalabhāvena akathitā’’ti niddesamattaṃ karoti ⚑; Mūlaṭīkā vi-saddaṃ virodhavacanaṃ ā-saddañca abhimukhabhāvappakāsanaṃ vibhajitvā byākatā kusalākusalā lakkhaṇavirodhato vināsakavināsitabbatāya ca vutto, abyākatā pana lakkhaṇato kusalākusalā viya viruddhā na hontīti vibhāveti ⚑.
  (Raw/dhammasangani-atthakatha/1-0a-0.md)
  (Raw/dhammasangani-mulatiika/1-0a-0.md)

- **A-kārassa pavatti** — Aṭṭhasālinī akusala-saddaṃ kevalaṃ ‘‘kusalapaṭipakkhā’’ti niddhāreti ⚑; Anuṭīkā a-kārassa anekesu atthesu pavattidassanena (taṃyoganivatti, taṃsambandhibhāvanivatti, taṃsabhāvanivatti, taggayhabhāvanivatti, taṃkiccanivatti, tabbhāvanivatti, tabbhāvamattanivatti, taṃsambhāvanaguṇanivatti, tadappabhāvanivatti, taṃsadisabhāvanivatti, tadapariyosānanivatti) pārisesanayena tappaṭipakkhavacanataṃ niyāmeti ⚑.
  (Raw/dhammasangani-atthakatha/1-0a-0.md)
  (Raw/dhammasangani-anutiika/1-0a-0.md)

### English translation

The Tikamātikā is the first division of the mātikā-list: twenty-two triads followed by the hundred dyads, organised into fifteen sections. The Kusalattika (the triad of *kusala*, *akusala*, *abyākata*) is placed first; the dyads of the Hetugocchaka and the rest follow. Every triad and dyad is further classified as either *sappadesa* (with-remainder, of restricted scope) or *nippadesa* (without-remainder, of unrestricted scope).

In the word-by-word commentary on the Kusalattika, *kusala* is shown to bear four senses in the canon — health (*ārogya*), blamelessness (*anavajja*), skill (*cheka*) and producing pleasant result (*sukha-vipāka*) — of which three are active here (health, blamelessness, pleasant-result). *Dhamma* has multiple senses (scripture, cause, virtue, the no-being-no-soul sense) but is taken here only in the no-being-no-soul sense. As characteristics: *kusala* is what is blameless and produces pleasant result; *akusala* is blameworthy and produces painful result; *abyākata* is what produces no result.

The word *akusala* is neither a mere negation of *kusala* nor a designation of something other than *kusala* — it is the designation of *kusala*'s opposite, just as in common usage *amittā* (non-friends = enemies) or in Buddhist terminology *alobha* (non-greed = its opposite). Likewise *abyākata* stands as a third class that is neither *kusala* nor *akusala*, but does not imply that *kusala* and *akusala* themselves are without result.

The two-part predicate *anavajja-sukhavipāka* shows both *kusala*'s wholesomeness-as-it-arises (purity of its own nature) and its capacity to produce pleasant result; conversely *sāvajja-dukkhavipāka* characterises *akusala*. The simple *avipāka* characterises *abyākata*.

An objector raises the worry that taking the three predicates (*kusala*, *akusala*, *abyākata*) together with *dhamma* would either collapse the triad into one ("triplet of dhammas") or expand it into six. Both horns are answered: just as the single word *māsa* legitimately picks out distinct things (a month, a bean, a measure of gold) without splitting into three words, so a single word *dhamma* can pick out the three differently characterised classes here without forcing either ekattha (synonymy) or chakka-catukka (six- or four-fold) expansion.

In the Vedanāttika, the words *sukha*, *dukkha*, *adukkhamasukha* are restricted to the feeling-sense; in the Sanidassanattika, *sanidassana* (visible) is *sa-nidassana* "with visibility", *sappaṭigha* (reactive) is *sa-paṭigha* "with collision". The remaining triads are commented on in the same style. The word-by-word commentary on the Tikamātikā ends here.
