## Dukamātikā: Combined Summary of Commentaries (1-0b-0)

This summary synthesizes the interpretations of the Atthakatha, Mūlaṭīkā, and Anuṭīkā on the *Dhammasaṅgaṇī*'s Dukamātikā, specifically focusing on the `1-0b-0` section concerning the Hetugocchaka (Root-Group).

### Atthakatha

The Atthakatha defines `hetu dhammā` as phenomena rooted in `mūlaṭṭhena` (in the sense of a root/foundation). `Nahetu` are their negations. `Sahetukā` are those with roots due to association in their arising (`sampayogato pavattena saha hetunā`), while `ahetukā` are without such roots. `Hetusampayuttā` are associated with roots due to co-arising (`ekuppādāditāya hetunā sampayuttā`), and `hetuvippayuttā` are dissociated from them. The text emphasizes that while `sahetukā/ahetukā` and `hetusampayuttā/hetuvippayuttā` may not differ in ultimate meaning (`atthato nānattaṃ natthi`), their distinct presentation (`desanāvilāsena`) caters to diverse individual understandings (`puggalānaṃ ajjhāsayavasena`). The additional 'kho pana' in the phrase 'na hetū kho pana dhammā' is noted to indicate a broader meaning, implying that just as non-root phenomena can be with or without roots, so too can root phenomena, and non-root phenomena can be associated or dissociated from roots.

### Mūlaṭīkā

The Mūlaṭīkā clarifies `mūlaṭṭhena` as a firmly established foundation (`suppatiṭṭhitabhāvasādhanena mūlabhāvena`), distinct from merely being a condition (`na paccayamattaṭṭhena`). It differentiates the terms `saha` (concomitant) and `sampayutta` (associated). `Saha` implies co-existence within the same cluster (`ekapuñje`) from arising to dissolution (`yāva bhaṅgā`) for phenomena that are `sahetukā` and share the same location as their roots (`samānadesagahaṇānaṃ hetuādisabbhāvaṃ dīpeti`). `Sampayutta`, on the other hand, signifies unification with roots through co-arising (`ekuppādādivasena saha hetūhi ekībhāvupagamanaṃ`). This distinction in the meaning of the terms (`padatthanānattenā`) is the basis for the difference between the two pairs of *dukas*, even if there is no difference in the underlying *dhamma* (`dhammanānattābhāvepi`).

### Anuṭīkā

The Anuṭīkā further explicates `samānadesaggahaṇānaṃ` as phenomena that are taken in the same location (`ekasmiṃyeva vatthusmiṃ gahetabbānaṃ`) or have the same object (`ekavatthuvisayānaṃ vā`). It clarifies `ekuppāditoti attho` to mean co-arising. It states that phenomena associated with roots (`hetusahagatā`) are taken together with roots (`hetūhi saha saṅgayhanti`). The state of being `sahetukabhāvo` (with roots) is attributed to these co-arising roots (`sahajātādīhi hetūhi katoti katvā vuttaṃ`). Reaffirming previous commentaries, the Anuṭīkā states that `dhammanānattābhāvepīti` means there is no difference in the inherent nature or essence of the phenomena (`sabhāvatthabhedābhāvepi`), while `padatthanānattenāti` refers to a difference in the expressible meaning of the terms (`nānāpadābhidheyyatābhedena`). The `desanāvilāso` (mode of presentation) is understood as the knowledge of various ways of teaching (`desetabbappakārajānanañhi desanāvilāso tathā desanāñāṇañcāti`), indicating the varied pedagogical approaches to explain the nature of these phenomena.

### Synthesis

The commentaries collectively elucidate the subtle yet precise distinctions within the Dukamātikā's Hetugocchaka. While the ultimate *dhamma* may be the same for seemingly similar pairs like `sahetukā/ahetukā` and `hetusampayuttā/hetuvippayuttā`, their differing terminology and modes of presentation serve to highlight distinct aspects of their relationship with roots. The Atthakatha introduces the core definitions and the pedagogical rationale. The Mūlaṭīkā refines these by distinguishing `saha` and `sampayutta` based on co-existence versus co-arising. The Anuṭīkā provides further granular clarification on shared location, co-arising, and the nuanced difference between the inherent nature of phenomena and the distinct ways they are described, emphasizing the deliberate `desanāvilāso` in the teachings. This layered interpretation is crucial for building accurate interpretive rails for the Abhidhamma.
