## Intro to the Daily Tipitaka on the ITCC website
- ### Welcome to Daily Tipitaka!
    
    In December 2026, thousands of practitioners will gather in Bodhgayā — the very place where the Buddha attained enlightenment — to chant the Abhidhamma Piṭaka together at the International Tipiṭaka Chanting Council. This 200-day journey is our preparation. Day by day, page by page, we will do two things at once: we will let the profound meaning of the Abhidhamma gradually unfold in our minds, and we will train our voices and hearts to carry these sacred Pāli words with confidence and devotion. Every session is both a step toward understanding and an act of offering — a source of blessing for ourselves and all beings. We don't need to master the Abhidhamma before we begin. We just need to begin. 🌱
    
- ### How Each Session Work
    
    Each day follows the same simple structure. Every morning you will receive a notification reminding you to sit down, open the app, and take the next step on the journey. 
    1. 🧭 TODAY'S CHANTING GUIDE — a short note orienting us to the day's passage and how it connects to our path. 
    2. 🪔 HOMAGE — we pay respect to the Buddha with the Vandanā. 
    3. 🌱 INTENTION — we recite the Buddha's own summary of the entire path, to set our purpose for the session. 
    4. 💡 READING FOR MEANING — we read the passage in our own language so the text is alive in our minds before we chant. 
    5. 🔑 PĀLI WORD OF THE DAY — one key word to carry as an anchor into the chanting. 
    6. ☸️ CHANTING IN PĀLI — we chant the passage unhurried and attentive, letting the words settle in the body as well as the mind. 
    7. ✨ ASPIRATION — we close with one concrete intention: how today's passage will help us avoid evil, cultivate good, and purify the mind. 
    8. Seven steps. Every day. For 200 days.

## Sample Day

### 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today we continue the Dukamātikā with five powerful clusters: Knots, Floods, Yokes, Hindrances, and Misapprehensions. Each cluster describes a different way the mind gets bound — knotted, flooded, yoked, blocked, or twisted by wrong view. Notice that the Abhidhamma is not just cataloguing neutral facts about reality. It is mapping the architecture of suffering itself, with surgical precision. The same six-part structure repeats in each cluster: what IS the binding force, what is SUBJECT to it, what is ASSOCIATED with it, and the combinations thereof. As you chant today, let the repetition work on you — each cluster is another mirror held up to the mind. As you read, ask yourself: which of these five — knots, floods, yokes, hindrances, misapprehensions — do I recognise most clearly in my own experience right now?

### 5. 🔑 Pali word of the day

NĪVARAṆĀ Pronounced: nee-VAR-a-naa NĪVARAṆĀ means hindrances — the five mental forces that obstruct meditation and cloud clear seeing: sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, and doubt. The word literally means ""that which covers or blocks."" In today's reading the Nīvaraṇagocchaka asks the same precise question it asks of every phenomenon: is this state itself a hindrance, is it subject to hindrances, or is it associated with them? Of all the clusters in today's reading, the nīvaraṇā are the most immediately recognisable in daily life — they are the reason we struggle to sit still, to focus, to begin. Recognising them by name is the first step to setting them aside.
