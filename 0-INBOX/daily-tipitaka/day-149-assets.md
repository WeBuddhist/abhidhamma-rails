### Tikaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-5|↑↑↑]]

Tividhena ñāṇavatthuniddese yogavihitesu kammāyatanesu sippāyatanesu vijjāṭṭhānesu kammassakataṃ vā saccānulomikaṃ vā parato assutvā sayameva cintento paṭilabhati, ayaṃ cintāmayā paññā, sā pana abhiññātānaṃ mahāsattānameva uppajjati; parato sutvā paṭiladdhā sutamayā; samāpannassa antosamāpattiyaṃ pavattā bhāvanāmayā. Dānacetanāsīlacetanāsampayuttā paññā pubbacetanā muñcacetanā aparacetanāti tividhena uppajjamānā dānamayā sīlamayā nāma. Pañcasīladasasīlaṃ upādāya pātimokkhasaṃvaro adhisīlaṃ, vaṭṭapādikā aṭṭha samāpattiyo upādāya vipassanāpādikā adhicittaṃ, kammassakatapaññaṃ upādāya vipassanāmaggaphalapaññā adhipaññā, yā uppanneyeva tathāgate uppajjanti. Akusalānaṃ anuppattippahānesu kusalānañca uppattiṭṭhitīsu paññā āyakosallaṃ, tabbiparītā apāyakosallaṃ, accāyikakicce vā bhaye vā tatrūpāyā paññā upāyakosallaṃ.

### Catukkaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-6|↑↑↑]]

Catubbidhena ñāṇavatthuniddese ‘idaṃ kammaṃ sakaṃ, idaṃ no saka’nti jānanaṃ kammassakatañāṇaṃ, ṭhapetvā saccānulomikaṃ vipassanāñāṇaṃ avasesā sabbāpi sāsavā kusalā paññā kammassakatañāṇameva; ekameva maggañāṇaṃ catūsu saccesu ekapaṭivedhavasena catūsu ṭhānesu saṅgahitaṃ. Maggaphalapaññā dhamme ñāṇaṃ, tena atītānāgate nayaṃ nayanaṃ anvaye ñāṇaṃ, cittaparicchedañāṇaṃ pariye ñāṇaṃ, avasesā sammutiñāṇaṃ. Kāmāvacarakusalapaññā cutipaṭisandhiṃ ācinateva, lokuttaramaggapaññā apacinateva, rūpārūpāvacarapaññā ācinati ceva vikkhambhanavasena apacinati ca. Paṭhamajjhānapaññā nibbidāya no paṭivedhāya, catutthajjhānapaññā paṭivedhāya no nibbidāya, maggapaññā ubhayāya; paṭhamajjhānapaññāva kāmasahagatasaññāmanasikāravasena hānabhāginī, nikantivasena ṭhitibhāginī, avitakkasahagatavasena visesabhāginī, nibbidāsahagatavasena nibbedhabhāginī; kilesavikkhambhanapaṭipadāya dukkhattā vipassanāparivāsassa dandhattā dukkhapaṭipadā dandhābhiññāti evamādi.

### Tikaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-5|↑↑↑]]

Tikaniddese paññavā ‘mayhaṃ evaṃ manasikaroto anuppannā akusalā dhammā uppajjanti, uppannā pavaḍḍhantī’ti pajānitvā anuppannānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ uppajjituṃ na deti, uppanne pajahati; anuppanne kusale uppādeti, uppanne bhāvanāpāripūriṃ pāpeti. Dānamayā sīlamayā ca paññā ‘dānaṃ dassāmi, sīlaṃ pūressāmī’ti cintentassa, dentassa pūrentassa, datvā pūretvā paccavekkhantassa pubbamuñcaaparacetanāvasena tividhena uppajjati; pañcasīladasasīlaṃ upādāya pātimokkhasaṃvaro adhisīlaṃ, kammassakatapaññaṃ upādāya vipassanāmaggaphalapaññā adhipaññā. Tasmā yoginā "pañcasīlamattena ala"nti abhimānaṃ pahāya, apāyakosallena akusalaṃ uppajjituṃ adatvā, āyakosallena dānasīlapātimokkhasaṃvaraṃ pubbamuñcaaparacetanāhi upasampādetvā, adhicittādhipaññāsannissitāya vipassanāya cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

### Catukkaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-6|↑↑↑]]

Catukkaniddese attano vā parassa vā sabbampi akusalaṃ atthabhañjanato anatthajananato na sakakammaṃ, sabbampi kusalaṃ anatthabhañjanato atthajananato sakakammaṃ; imasmiṃ kammassakatañāṇe ṭhatvā bahuṃ dānaṃ datvā sīlaṃ pūretvā uposathaṃ samādiyitvā nibbānaṃ pattānaṃ gaṇanapatho natthi. Paṭhamajjhānaṃ pana santato paṇītato disvā assādayamānā nikanti uppajjati, tassa vasena paññā neva hāyati na vaḍḍhati, ṭhitibhāginī hoti; nibbidāsahagatā pana virāgūpasañhitā saññāmanasikārā nibbedhabhāginiṃ karonti. Tasmā yoginā "jhānaṃ me santaṃ paṇīta"nti nikantiṃ pahāya, tividhaṃ kāyaduccaritaṃ catubbidhaṃ vacīduccaritaṃ tividhaṃ manoduccaritaṃ na sakakammanti akatvā, dasavidhaṃ sucaritaṃ sakakammanti upasampādetvā, nibbidāsahagatena vipassanāñāṇena virāgaṃ nibbānaṃ sacchikātuṃ cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

##### The Exposition of the Fours ^2-16-11-4-0

(1a) Therein, what is knowledge that action is one's own? "There is what is given, there is what is offered, there is what is sacrificed; there is the fruit and result of well-done and badly-done actions; there is this world, there is the other world; there is mother, there is father; there are spontaneously reborn beings; there are in the world ascetics and brahmins of right conduct and right practice who declare this world and the other world, having realised them for themselves by direct knowledge" — the wisdom of such a kind, the understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view: this is called "knowledge that action is one's own". Setting aside knowledge in conformity with the truths, all wholesome wisdom that is subject to mental taints is knowledge that action is one's own.
(1b) Therein, what is knowledge in conformity with the truths? "Material form is impermanent", or "feeling… perception… formations… consciousness is impermanent" — whatever conforming acceptance, view, preference, understanding, consideration, acceptance upon reflection on things of such a kind: this is called "knowledge in conformity with the truths".
(1c) Wisdom in the four paths is the knowledge of one endowed with the path.
(1d) Wisdom in the four fruits is the knowledge of one endowed with the fruit. ^2-793

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-794]]

(2) The knowledge of one endowed with the path is knowledge of suffering, knowledge of the origin of suffering, knowledge of the cessation of suffering, and knowledge of the way leading to the cessation of suffering.
(a) Therein, what is knowledge of suffering? The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view that arises concerning suffering: this is called "knowledge of suffering".
(b–d) Concerning the origin of suffering… concerning the cessation of suffering… concerning the way leading to the cessation of suffering, the wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view that arises: this is called "knowledge of the way leading to the cessation of suffering". ^2-794

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-795]]

(3) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the sense sphere is wisdom of the sense sphere; wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the fine-material sphere is wisdom of the fine-material sphere; wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the immaterial sphere is wisdom of the immaterial sphere; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom not included in any sphere. ^2-795

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-796]]

(4a) Therein, what is knowledge of the teaching? Wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is knowledge of the teaching.
(4b) By this teaching that is known, seen, reached, understood, and fathomed, he draws the inference concerning past and future: "Whatever ascetics or brahmins in the past directly knew suffering, directly knew the origin of suffering, directly knew the cessation of suffering, directly knew the way leading to the cessation of suffering, it was just this suffering they directly knew, just this origin of suffering they directly knew, just this cessation of suffering they directly knew, just this way leading to the cessation of suffering they directly knew. Whatever ascetics or brahmins in the future will directly know suffering, will directly know the origin of suffering, will directly know the cessation of suffering, will directly know the way leading to the cessation of suffering, it is just this suffering they will directly know, just this origin of suffering they will directly know, just this cessation of suffering they will directly know, just this way leading to the cessation of suffering they will directly know" — the wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein: this is called "inferential knowledge".
(4c) Therein, what is knowledge of encompassing others' minds? Here a monk knows the minds of other beings, of other persons, having encompassed them with his own mind. He knows a mind with lust as "a mind with lust"; he knows a mind without lust as "a mind without lust"; he knows a mind with hate as "a mind with hate"; he knows a mind without hate as "a mind without hate"; he knows a mind with delusion as "a mind with delusion"; he knows a mind without delusion as "a mind without delusion"; he knows a contracted mind as "a contracted mind"; he knows a distracted mind as "a distracted mind"; he knows an expanded mind as "an expanded mind"; he knows an unexpanded mind as "an unexpanded mind"; he knows a surpassable mind as "a surpassable mind"; he knows an unsurpassable mind as "an unsurpassable mind"; he knows a concentrated mind as "a concentrated mind"; he knows an unconcentrated mind as "an unconcentrated mind"; he knows a freed mind as "a freed mind"; he knows an unfreed mind as "an unfreed mind" — the wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein: this is called "knowledge of encompassing others' minds".
(4d) Setting aside knowledge of the teaching, inferential knowledge, and knowledge of encompassing others' minds, the remaining wisdom is conventional knowledge. ^2-796

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-797]]

(5a) Therein, what is wisdom for accumulation and not for dismantling? Wisdom in the wholesome of the sense sphere is for accumulation and not for dismantling.
(5b) Wisdom in the four paths is for dismantling and not for accumulation.
(5c) Wisdom in the wholesome of the fine-material and immaterial spheres is both for accumulation and for dismantling.
(5d) The remaining wisdom is neither for accumulation nor for dismantling. ^2-797

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-798]]

(6a) Therein, what is wisdom for disenchantment and not for penetration? The wisdom by which one becomes dispassionate towards sense pleasures, but does not penetrate the direct knowledges or the truths: this is called "wisdom for disenchantment and not for penetration".
(6b) Being already dispassionate towards sense pleasures by that wisdom, one penetrates the direct knowledges but not the truths: this is called "wisdom for penetration and not for disenchantment".
(6c) Wisdom in the four paths is both for disenchantment and for penetration.
(6d) The remaining wisdom is neither for disenchantment nor for penetration. ^2-798

(7a) Therein, what is wisdom that shares in decline? When perceptions and attentions accompanied by sense pleasure beset one who has gained the first meditative absorption, that is wisdom that shares in decline.
(7b) When mindfulness in conformity with that state stands firm, that is wisdom that shares in stability.
(7c) When perceptions and attentions accompanied by absence of thought beset him, that is wisdom that shares in distinction.
(7d) When perceptions and attentions accompanied by disenchantment, connected with dispassion, beset him, that is wisdom that shares in breaking through. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by thought beset one who has gained the second meditative absorption, that is wisdom that shares in decline. When mindfulness in conformity with that state stands firm, that is wisdom that shares in stability. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by equanimity beset him, that is wisdom that shares in distinction. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by disenchantment, connected with dispassion, beset him, that is wisdom that shares in breaking through. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by joy and happiness beset one who has gained the third meditative absorption, that is wisdom that shares in decline. When mindfulness in conformity with that state stands firm, that is wisdom that shares in stability. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by neither-pain-nor-pleasure beset him, that is wisdom that shares in distinction. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by disenchantment, connected with dispassion, beset him, that is wisdom that shares in breaking through. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by equanimity beset one who has gained the fourth meditative absorption, that is wisdom that shares in decline. When mindfulness in conformity with that state stands firm, that is wisdom that shares in stability. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by the field of infinite space beset him, that is wisdom that shares in distinction. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by disenchantment, connected with dispassion, beset him, that is wisdom that shares in breaking through. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by material form beset one who has gained the field of infinite space, that is wisdom that shares in decline. When mindfulness in conformity with that state stands firm, that is wisdom that shares in stability. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by the field of infinite consciousness beset him, that is wisdom that shares in distinction. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by disenchantment, connected with dispassion, beset him, that is wisdom that shares in breaking through. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by the field of infinite space beset one who has gained the field of infinite consciousness, that is wisdom that shares in decline. When mindfulness in conformity with that state stands firm, that is wisdom that shares in stability. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by the field of nothingness beset him, that is wisdom that shares in distinction. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by disenchantment, connected with dispassion, beset him, that is wisdom that shares in breaking through. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by the field of infinite consciousness beset one who has gained the field of nothingness, that is wisdom that shares in decline. When mindfulness in conformity with that state stands firm, that is wisdom that shares in stability. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception beset him, that is wisdom that shares in distinction. When perceptions and attentions accompanied by disenchantment, connected with dispassion, beset him, that is wisdom that shares in breaking through. ^2-799

(8) Therein, what are the four analytical knowledges? The analytical knowledge of meaning, the analytical knowledge of principles, the analytical knowledge of language, the analytical knowledge of ready understanding. Knowledge with regard to meaning is the analytical knowledge of meaning; knowledge with regard to principles is the analytical knowledge of principles; knowledge with regard to the expression of the language of those principles is the analytical knowledge of language; knowledge with regard to knowledges is the analytical knowledge of ready understanding. These are the four analytical knowledges. ^2-800
