### Pañhāpucchakaṃ (Jhāna)

[[#^toc-12-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchake pāḷianusāreneva jhānānaṃ kusalādibhāvo veditabbo. Ārammaṇattikesu tiṇṇaṃ jhānānaṃ nimittārammaṇattā parittārammaṇādibhāvena navattabbatā; lokuttarā pana maggakāle phalakāle vā appamāṇārammaṇā. Catutthaṃ jhānaṃ pana kusalato terasavidhaṃ — sabbatthapādaka-iddhividha-dibbasota-cetopariya-pubbenivāsa-dibbacakkhu-yathākammūpaga-anāgataṃsañāṇacatutthaṃ, catubbidhaṃ āruppacatutthaṃ, lokuttaracatutthañcāti — tesaṃ ārammaṇavatthuvasena parittamahaggatāappamāṇanavattabbārammaṇatā ceva atītānāgatapaccuppannaajjhattabahiddhārammaṇatā ca pabhedato vibhattā. Evamayaṃ jhānavibhaṅgopi sammāsambuddhena suttantabhidhammapañhāpucchakavasena teparivaṭṭaṃ nīharitvā, sabbattha lokiyalokuttaramissakāni jhānāni dassento, bhājetvā dassito.

[[#^toc-12-2|↑↑↑]]

Therein, what is the first meditative absorption? Here a monk, on the occasion when he develops the meditative absorption that is beyond the world, leading out, leading to dismantling, for the abandoning of views, for reaching the first stage — secluded from sense pleasures… he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, of difficult practice and slow direct knowledge — on that occasion there is contact… there is non-distraction. Therein, what is the first meditative absorption? #### The Section of Questions and Answers ^2-12-3-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-638]]

There are four meditative absorptions. Here a monk, secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome things, enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, which is with thought and with examination, with joy and happiness born of seclusion… with the stilling of thought and examination, he enters and dwells in the second meditative absorption, which has inner confidence and unification of mind, is without thought and without examination, with joy and happiness born of concentration… with the fading away of joy he dwells equanimous, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body — that of which the noble ones declare, "equanimous, mindful, one who dwells happily" — he enters and dwells in the third meditative absorption… with the abandoning of happiness and the abandoning of pain, and with the earlier disappearance of joy and grief, he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant and has purity of mindfulness through equanimity. ^2-638
