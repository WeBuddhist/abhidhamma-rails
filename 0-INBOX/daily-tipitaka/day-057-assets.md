## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-summaries.md`)

### Tikanikkhepaṃ

[[#^toc-4-1|↑↑↑]]

Tikanikkhepakathāyaṃ tikanikkhepaṃ ārabhibbā kusalapadaniddese tīṇīti gaṇanaparicchedo. Kusalāni ca tāni mūlāni ca, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ hetupaccayapabhavajanakasamuṭṭhānanibbattakaṭṭhena mūlānīti kusalamūlāni. Evaṃ atthavasena dassetvā nāmavasena dassetuṃ alobho adoso amohoti āha. Taṃsampayuttoti tehi sampayutto, taṃsamuṭṭhānanti tehi samuṭṭhitaṃ. Tīhi ṭhānesu catubhūmakakusalaṃ pariyādiyitvā dassesi dhammarājā. Akusalepi eseva nayo; dassanena pahātabbahetukesupi eseva desanānayo veditabbo.

#### Hetugocchakaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-4-2-1|↑↑↑]]

Hetugocchakadukānaṃ nikkhepe tehidhammehi ye dhammā sahetukāti tehi hetudhammehi ye aññe hetudhammā vā nahetudhammā vā te sahetukā. Ahetukapadepi eseva nayo. Hetu hetuyeva ca hoti, tiṇṇaṃ vā dvinnaṃ vā ekato uppattiyaṃ sahetuko ca. Vicikicchuddhaccasahagato pana moho hetu ahetuko. Hetusampayuttadukaniddesepi eseva nayoti vuttaṃ.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-practice.md`)

### Tikanikkhepaṃ

[[#^toc-3-1|↑↑↑]]

Tikanikkhepe paṭhamaṃ kusalattikaṃ nikkhittaṃ — "katame dhammā kusalā? Tīṇi kusalamūlāni alobho adoso amoho"ti. Alobho dānassa, adoso mettāsīlassa, amoho paññābhāvanāya mūlaṃ; tabbiparītā lobhadosamohā akusalamūlāni. Aparo tiko "dassanena pahātabbā, bhāvanāya pahātabbā, neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbā"ti pahānabhūmiṃ dasseti. Tasmā yoginā tīṇi akusalamūlāni pahānāya, tīṇi kusalamūlāni upasampadāya ca ussahitvā, dassanena bhāvanāya ca pahātabbe kilese kamena samugghātetuṃ maggo bhāvetabbo; evaṃ cittaṃ pariyodāpeti.

#### Hetugocchakaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-3-2-1|↑↑↑]]

Hetugocchake dhammā sahetukā ahetukā, hetū na hetū cāti vavatthapiyanti. Tayo kusalahetū (alobhādayo), tayo akusalahetū (lobhādayo) ca vaṭṭavivaṭṭassa mūlaṃ. Yathā rukkho mūle chinne patati, evaṃ akusalamūlesu samugghātitesu sabbe akusalā dhammā na pavattanti. Tasmā yoginā "kiṃmūlako ayaṃ dhammo"ti hetuṃ pariggahetvā, akusalahetumūlaṃ paññāya chinditvā, kusalahetumūlaṃ vaḍḍhetvā, cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

Those consciousness and mental factors that arise taking future dhammas as their object. These dhammas have future objects. ^1-1048
Those consciousness and mental factors that arise taking present dhammas as their object. These dhammas have present objects. ^1-1049
Those phenomena of those beings that are internal, personal, fixed, pertaining to the individual, and appropriated: form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness – these phenomena are internal. ^1-1050
Those phenomena of other beings, other individuals, that are internal, personal, fixed, pertaining to the individual, and appropriated: form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness – these phenomena are external. ^1-1051
Both of these – these phenomena are internal and external. ^1-1052
Those consciousness and mental factors that arise taking internal phenomena as object – these phenomena have internal objects. ^1-1053
Those consciousness and mental factors that arise taking external phenomena as object – these phenomena have external objects. ^1-1054
Those consciousness and mental factors that arise taking internal and external phenomena as object – these phenomena have internal and external objects. ^1-1055
The visible object – these phenomena are visible and impinging. ^1-1056
The eye-base, ear-base, nose-base, tongue-base, body-base, sound-base, smell-base, taste-base, tangible-base – these phenomena are invisible and impinging. ^1-1057
The Triad. ^1-1058
Which states are roots? Three wholesome roots, three unwholesome roots, three indeterminate roots, nine sense-sphere roots, six fine-material sphere roots, six immaterial sphere roots, six supramundane roots. ^1-1059
Among these, which are the three wholesome roots? Non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion. ^1-1060
That which is non-greed, not being greedy, the state of not being greedy, non-attachment, not being attached, the state of not being attached, non-covetousness, non-greed, a wholesome root—this is called non-greed. ^1-1061
That which is non-hatred, not being hateful, the state of not being hateful, loving-kindness, being loving, the state of being loving, sympathy, being sympathetic, the state of being sympathetic, wishing for welfare, compassion, non-ill-will, not being ill-willed, non-hatred, a wholesome root—this is called non-hatred. ^1-1062
These are the three wholesome roots. ^1-1063
Among these, which are the three unwholesome roots? Greed, hatred, delusion. ^1-1064
greed as the root of evil—this is what is called greed. ^1-1065
What is hatred? Resentment arises thinking: "He harmed me," "He is harming me," "He will harm me." Or thinking: "He harmed my dear and beloved one," "He is harming my dear and beloved one," "He will harm my dear and beloved one." Or thinking: "He benefited my disliked and unloved one," "He is benefiting my disliked and unloved one," "He will benefit my disliked and unloved one." Or resentment arises without reason. This mental resentment, repugnance, opposition, anger, wrath, ill-will, mental corruption, displeasure of mind, fury, fierceness, lack of satisfaction of mind—this is called hatred. ^1-1066
These are the three unwholesome roots. ^1-1067
Which are the three indeterminate roots? Non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion, whether as results of wholesome states or as functional indeterminate states—these are the three indeterminate roots. ^1-1068
Which are the nine sense-sphere roots? The three wholesome roots, the three unwholesome roots, the three indeterminate roots—these are the nine sense-sphere roots. ^1-1069
Which are the six fine-material-sphere roots? The three wholesome roots, the three indeterminate roots—these are the six fine-material-sphere roots. ^1-1070
Which are the six immaterial-sphere roots? The three wholesome roots, the three indeterminate roots—these are the six immaterial-sphere roots. ^1-1071
Which are the six unincluded roots? The three wholesome roots, the three indeterminate roots—these are the six unincluded roots. ^1-1072
Which are the three wholesome roots? Non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion. ^1-1073
What is non-greed? That non-greed, not being greedy, the state of not being greedy, non-attachment, not being attached, the state of not being attached, non-covetousness, non-greed, the root of wholesome kamma—this is called non-greed. ^1-1074
Therein, what is non-hatred? That which is non-hatred, non-aversion, non-malice, non-ill-will, non-harming, is the wholesome root of non-hatred. This is called non-hatred. ^1-1075
These are the three wholesome roots. ^1-1076
Therein, what are the three indeterminate roots? Non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion, as results of wholesome phenomena – these are the three indeterminate roots. These six are the unclassified roots – these phenomena are roots. ^1-1077
Which phenomena are not roots? Excluding those phenomena, the remaining wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate phenomena of the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and not included in any sphere; the feeling aggregate, the perception aggregate, the formation aggregate, the consciousness aggregate; all matter, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena are not roots. ^1-1078
