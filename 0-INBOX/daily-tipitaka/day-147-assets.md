(14) Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalābyākate paññā nīvaraṇavippayuttā nīvaraṇiyā paññā, catūsu maggesu catūsu phalesu paññā nīvaraṇavippayuttā anīvaraṇiyā paññā.
(15) Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalābyākate paññā parāmaṭṭhā paññā, catūsu maggesu catūsu phalesu paññā aparāmaṭṭhā paññā.

(17) Tīsu bhūmīsu vipāke paññā upādinnā paññā, tīsu bhūmīsu kusale tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākate catūsu maggesu catūsu phalesu paññā anupādinnā paññā.

(19) Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalābyākate paññā upādānavippayuttā upādāniyā paññā, catūsu maggesu catūsu phalesu paññā upādānavippayuttā anupādāniyā paññā.
(20) Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalābyākate paññā saṃkilesikā paññā, catūsu maggesu catūsu phalesu paññā asaṃkilesikā paññā.
(21) Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalābyākate paññā kilesavippayuttā saṃkilesikā paññā, catūsu maggesu catūsu phalesu paññā kilesavippayuttā asaṃkilesikā paññā.
(22) Vitakkasampayuttā paññā savitakkā paññā, vitakkavippayuttā paññā avitakkā paññā.
(23) Vicārasampayuttā paññā savicārā paññā, vicāravippayuttā paññā avicārā paññā.

(25) Pītisampayuttā paññā pītisahagatā paññā, pītivippayuttā paññā na pītisahagatā paññā.
(26) Sukhasampayuttā paññā sukhasahagatā paññā, sukhavippayuttā paññā na sukhasahagatā paññā.
(27) Upekkhāsampayuttā paññā upekkhāsahagatā paññā, upekkhāvippayuttā paññā na upekkhāsahagatā paññā.

(29) Rūpāvacarakusalābyākate paññā rūpāvacarā paññā, kāmāvacarā paññā arūpāvacarā paññā apariyāpannā paññā na rūpāvacarā paññā.
(30) Arūpāvacarakusalābyākate paññā arūpāvacarā paññā, kāmāvacarā paññā rūpāvacarā paññā apariyāpannā paññā na arūpāvacarā paññā.

(34) Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalābyākate paññā sauttarā paññā, catūsu maggesu catūsu phalesu paññā anuttarā paññā.

Evaṃ duvidhena ñāṇavatthu.

### Ekakaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-3|↑↑↑]]

"Pañcaviññāṇā na hetumevā"tiādinā nayena āraddhe niddesavāre na hetumevāti sādhāraṇahetupaṭikkhepaniddeso; aniccamevāti hutvā abhāvaṭṭhena aniccāyeva. Uppannavatthukā uppannārammaṇāti anāgatapaṭikkhepo, purejātavatthukā purejātārammaṇāti sahuppattipaṭikkhepo. Cakkhuviññāṇaṃ cakkhupasādameva vatthuṃ katvā rūpañca ārammaṇaṃ katvā uppajjati – nibaddhavatthu nibaddhadvāraṃ nibaddhārammaṇameva; evamete aññamaññassa gocaravisayaṃ na paccanubhonti. Na kañci dhammaṃ paṭivijānāti aññatra abhinipātamattāti cakkhuviññāṇaṃ dassanamattameva hoti; kusalākusalapaṭivijānanaṃ, iriyāpathakappanaṃ, samādhisamāpajjanaṃ, vipassanā, nibbānārammaṇatā, sabbopi panesa pabhedo manodvārikajavaneyeva labbhati, na pañcadvārikena. Supinaṃ dhātukkhobhato anubhūtapubbato devatopasaṃhārato pubbanimittato vā kapimiddhapareto passati; taṃ sekkhaputhujjanāva passanti appahīnavipallāsattā, asekkhā na passanti. Evaṃ pañcannaṃ viññāṇānaṃ yāthāvaṭṭhaṃ vatthuṃ vibhāvetīti yāthāvakavatthuvibhāvanā paññā, tassā vasena ekavidhena ñāṇavatthu.

### Dukaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-4|↑↑↑]]

Duvidhena ñāṇavatthuniddese catūsu bhūmīsu kusalapaññā attano attano bhūmipariyāpannaṃ vipākasaṅkhātaṃ atthaṃ jāpeti janeti pavattetīti atthajāpikā; arahato abhiññāsamāpattīnaṃ parikammasamaye kāmāvacarakiriyapaññāpi abhiññāsamāpattipabhedaṃ kiriyasaṅkhātaṃ atthaṃ jāpetīti atthajāpikā. Catūsu bhūmīsu vipākapaññā pana sahajātādipaccayavasena vipākatthaṃ jāpetvā ṭhitāti jāpitatthā, uppannābhiññāsamāpattīnaṃ kiriyapaññāpi tatheva; sabbāpi vā esā attano attano kāraṇehi jāpitā janitā pavattitā sayañca atthabhūtātipi jāpitatthā.

### Ekakaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-3|↑↑↑]]

Ekakaniddese pañcaviññāṇāni dassanasavanādimattāneva, kusalākusalapaṭivijānanaṃ samādhivipassanā ca manodvārikajavaneyeva labbhati; supinante ca cetiyavandanadhammassavanādīni karontassa kusalo, pāṇātipātādīni karontassa akusalo, taṃ kammaṃ savipākaṃ; asekkhā pahīnavipallāsattā supinaṃ na passanti. ##### The Exposition of the Twos ^2-16-11-2-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-767]]

(1) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is worldly wisdom; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom beyond the world.
(2) All wisdom can be known by one means and cannot be known by another.
(3) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom subject to mental taints; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom without mental taints.
(4) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from mental taints but subject to them; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from mental taints and without them.
(5) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom that can be bound by fetters; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom that cannot be bound by fetters.
(6) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from fetters but able to be bound by them; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from fetters and not able to be bound by them.
(7) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom that can be bound by ties; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom that cannot be bound by ties.
(8) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from ties but able to be bound by them; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from ties and not able to be bound by them.
(9) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom that can be swept by floods; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom that cannot be swept by floods.
(10) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from floods but able to be swept by them; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from floods and not able to be swept by them.
(11) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom that can be bound by yokes; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom that cannot be bound by yokes.
(12) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from yokes but able to be bound by them; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from yokes and not able to be bound by them.
(13) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom that can be obstructed by hindrances; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom that cannot be obstructed by hindrances.
(14) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from hindrances but able to be obstructed by them; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from hindrances and not able to be obstructed by them.
(15) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom that can be wrongly grasped; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom that cannot be wrongly grasped.
(16) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from wrong grasping but able to be wrongly grasped; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from wrong grasping and not able to be wrongly grasped.
(17) Wisdom in the results of the three planes is wisdom taken up at birth; wisdom in the wholesome of the three planes, in the functional neutral of the three planes, and in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom not taken up at birth.
(18) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom that can be clung to; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom that cannot be clung to.
(19) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from clinging but able to be clung to; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from clinging and not able to be clung to.
(20) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom connected with defilement; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom not connected with defilement.
(21) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom dissociated from defilements but connected with defilement; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom dissociated from defilements and not connected with defilement.
(22) Wisdom associated with thought is wisdom with thought; wisdom dissociated from thought is wisdom without thought.
(23) Wisdom associated with examination is wisdom with examination; wisdom dissociated from examination is wisdom without examination.
(24) Wisdom associated with joy is wisdom with joy; wisdom dissociated from joy is wisdom without joy.
(25) Wisdom associated with joy is wisdom accompanied by joy; wisdom dissociated from joy is wisdom not accompanied by joy.
(26) Wisdom associated with happiness is wisdom accompanied by happiness; wisdom dissociated from happiness is wisdom not accompanied by happiness.
(27) Wisdom associated with equanimity is wisdom accompanied by equanimity; wisdom dissociated from equanimity is wisdom not accompanied by equanimity.
(28) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the sense sphere is wisdom of the sense sphere; wisdom of the fine-material sphere, wisdom of the immaterial sphere, and wisdom not included in any sphere are wisdom not of the sense sphere.
(29) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the fine-material sphere is wisdom of the fine-material sphere; wisdom of the sense sphere, wisdom of the immaterial sphere, and wisdom not included in any sphere are wisdom not of the fine-material sphere.
(30) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the immaterial sphere is wisdom of the immaterial sphere; wisdom of the sense sphere, wisdom of the fine-material sphere, and wisdom not included in any sphere are wisdom not of the immaterial sphere.
(31) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is wisdom included in a sphere; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is wisdom not included in any sphere.
(32) Wisdom in the four paths is wisdom that leads out; wisdom in the wholesome of the three planes, in the results of the four planes, and in the functional neutral of the three planes is wisdom that does not lead out.
(33) Wisdom in the four paths is fixed wisdom; wisdom in the wholesome of the three planes, in the results of the four planes, and in the functional neutral of the three planes is unfixed wisdom.
(34) Wisdom in the wholesome and neutral of the three planes is surpassable wisdom; wisdom in the four paths and the four fruits is unsurpassed wisdom.
(35) Therein, what is wisdom that brings about a goal?
Wisdom in the wholesome of the four planes, and in the functional neutral of an arahant producing a direct knowledge or producing an attainment, is wisdom that brings about a goal; wisdom in the results of the four planes, and in the functional neutral of an arahant when the direct knowledge has been produced and the attainment has been produced, is wisdom whose goal has been brought about.
Thus is the ground of knowledge by way of two.
