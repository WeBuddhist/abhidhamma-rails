### Jhānavibhaṅgo ^2-12-0
#### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ ^2-12-1-0

Idha bhikkhu pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto viharati, ācāragocarasampanno, aṇumattesu vajjesu bhayadassāvī, samādāya sikkhati sikkhāpadesu indriyesu guttadvāro bhojane mattaññū pubbarattāpararattaṃ jāgariyānuyogamanuyutto sātaccaṃ nepakkaṃ bodhipakkhikānaṃ dhammānaṃ bhāvanānuyogamanuyutto. So abhikkante paṭikkante sampajānakārī hoti, ālokite vilokite sampajānakārī hoti, samiñjite [sammiñjite (sī. syā.)] pasārite sampajānakārī hoti, saṅghāṭipattacīvaradhāraṇe sampajānakārī hoti, asite pīte khāyite sāyite sampajānakārī hoti, uccārapassāvakamme sampajānakārī hoti, gate ṭhite nisinne sutte jāgarite bhāsite tuṇhībhāve sampajānakārī hoti. So vivittaṃ senāsanaṃ bhajati araññaṃ rukkhamūlaṃ pabbataṃ kandaraṃ giriguhaṃ susānaṃ vanapatthaṃ abbhokāsaṃ palālapuñjaṃ appasaddaṃ appanigghosaṃ vijanavātaṃ manussarāhasseyyakaṃ [manussarāhaseyyakaṃ (sī. syā.)] paṭisallānasāruppaṃ. So araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā nisīdati pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā ujuṃ kāyaṃ paṇidhāya parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā. So abhijjhaṃ loke pahāya vigatābhijjhena cetasā viharati, abhijjhāya cittaṃ parisodheti. Byāpādapadosaṃ pahāya abyāpannacitto viharati sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī, byāpādapadosā cittaṃ parisodheti. Thinamiddhaṃ pahāya vigatathinamiddho viharati ālokasaññī sato sampajāno, thinamiddhā cittaṃ parisodheti. Uddhaccakukkuccaṃ pahāya anuddhato viharati ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacitto, uddhaccakukkuccā cittaṃ parisodheti. Vicikicchaṃ pahāya tiṇṇavicikiccho viharati akathaṃkathī kusalesu dhammesu, vicikicchāya cittaṃ parisodheti. So ime pañca nīvaraṇe pahāya cetaso upakkilese paññāya dubbalīkaraṇe vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati; vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati; pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca viharati sato ca sampajāno sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeti, yaṃ taṃ ariyā ācikkhanti – "upekkhako satimā sukhavihārī"ti tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati; sukhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā pubbeva somanassadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā adukkhamasukhaṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati; sabbaso rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā "ananto ākāso"ti ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati; sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma "anantaṃ viññāṇa"nti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati; sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma "natthi kiñcī"ti ākiñcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati; sabbaso ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samatikkamma nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati. ^2-508

##### Mātikā ^2-12-1-1-0

"Idhā"ti imissā diṭṭhiyā, imissā khantiyā, imissā ruciyā, imasmiṃ ādāye, imasmiṃ dhamme, imasmiṃ vinaye, imasmiṃ dhammavinaye, imasmiṃ pāvacane, imasmiṃ brahmacariye, imasmiṃ satthusāsane. Tena vuccati "idhā"ti. ^2-509

"Bhikkhū"ti samaññāya bhikkhu, paṭiññāya bhikkhu, bhikkhatīti bhikkhu, bhikkhakoti bhikkhu, bhikkhācariyaṃ ajjhupagatoti bhikkhu, bhinnapaṭadharoti bhikkhu, bhindati pāpake akusale dhammeti bhikkhu, bhinnattā pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ bhikkhu, odhiso kilesānaṃ pahānā bhikkhu, anodhiso kilesānaṃ pahānā bhikkhu, sekkho bhikkhu, asekkho bhikkhu, nevasekkhanāsekkho bhikkhu, aggo bhikkhu, bhadro bhikkhu, maṇḍo bhikkhu, sāro bhikkhu, samaggena saṅghena ñatticatutthena kammena akuppena ṭhānārahena upasampanno bhikkhu. ^2-510

"Pātimokkha"nti sīlaṃ patiṭṭhā ādi caraṇaṃ saṃyamo saṃvaro mokkhaṃ pāmokkhaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ samāpattiyā. Saṃvaroti. Kāyiko avītikkamo, vācasiko avītikkamo, kāyikavācasiko avītikkamo. Saṃvutoti. Iminā pātimokkhasaṃvarena upeto hoti samupeto upāgato samupāgato upapanno sampanno samannāgato. Tena vuccati "pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto"ti. ^2-511

"Viharatī"ti iriyati vattati pāleti yapeti yāpeti carati viharati. Tena vuccati "viharatī"ti. ^2-512

"Ācāragocarasampanno"ti atthi ācāro, atthi anācāro.
Tattha katamo anācāro? Kāyiko vītikkamo, vācasiko vītikkamo, kāyikavācasiko vītikkamo – ayaṃ vuccati "anācāro". Sabbampi dussīlyaṃ anācāro. Idhekacco veḷudānena vā pattadānena vā pupphadānena vā phaladānena vā sinānadānena vā dantakaṭṭhadānena vā cāṭukamyatāya vā muggasūpyatāya [muggasuppatāya (sī.)] vā pāribhaṭayatāya vā jaṅghapesanikena vā aññataraññatarena vā buddhapaṭikuṭṭhena micchāājīvena jīvikaṃ kappeti – ayaṃ vuccati "anācāro".
Tattha katamo ācāro? Kāyiko avītikkamo, vācasiko avītikkamo, kāyikavācasiko avītikkamo – ayaṃ vuccati "ācāro". Sabbopi sīlasaṃvaro ācāro. Idhekacco na veḷudānena na pattadānena na pupphadānena na phaladānena na sinānadānena na dantakaṭṭhadānena na cāṭukamyatāya na muggasūpyatāya na pāribhaṭayatāya na jaṅghapesanikena na aññataraññatarena buddhapaṭikuṭṭhena micchāājīvena jīvikaṃ kappeti – ayaṃ vuccati "ācāro". ^2-513

"Gocaro"ti atthi gocaro, atthi agocaro.
Tattha katamo agocaro? Idhekacco vesiyāgocaro vā hoti vidhavāgocaro vā thullakumārigocaro vā paṇḍakagocaro vā bhikkhunigocaro vā pānāgāragocaro vā, saṃsaṭṭho viharati rājūhi rājamahāmattehi titthiyehi titthiyasāvakehi ananulomikena saṃsaggena; yāni vā pana tāni kulāni assaddhāni appasannāni anopānabhūtāni akkosakaparibhāsakāni anatthakāmāni ahitakāmāni aphāsukakāmāni ayogakkhemakāmāni bhikkhūnaṃ bhikkhunīnaṃ upāsakānaṃ upāsikānaṃ, tathārūpāni kulāni sevati bhajati payirupāsati – ayaṃ vuccati "agocaro".
Tattha katamo gocaro? Idhekacco na vesiyāgocaro hoti na vidhavāgocaro na thullakumārigocaro na paṇḍakagocaro na bhikkhunigocaro na pānāgāragocaro, asaṃsaṭṭho viharati rājūhi rājamahāmattehi titthiyehi titthiyasāvakehi ananulomikena saṃsaggena; yāni vā pana tāni kulāni saddhāni pasannāni opānabhūtāni kāsāvapajjotāni isivātapaṭivātāni atthakāmāni hitakāmāni phāsukakāmāni yogakkhemakāmāni bhikkhūnaṃ bhikkhunīnaṃ upāsakānaṃ upāsikānaṃ, tathārūpāni kulāni sevati bhajati payirupāsati – ayaṃ vuccati "gocaro". Iti iminā ca ācārena iminā ca gocarena upeto hoti…pe… samannāgato. Tena vuccati "ācāragocarasampanno"ti. ^2-514

"Aṇumattesu vajjesu bhayadassāvī"ti tattha katame aṇumattā vajjā? Yāni tāni vajjāni appamattakāni oramattakāni lahusāni lahusammatāni saṃyamakaraṇīyāni saṃvarakaraṇīyāni cittuppādakaraṇīyāni manasikārapaṭibaddhāni – ime vuccanti "aṇumattā vajjā". Iti imesu aṇumattesu vajjesu vajjadassāvī ca hoti bhayadassāvī ca ādīnavadassāvī ca nissaraṇadassāvī ca. Tena vuccati "aṇumattesu vajjesu bhayadassāvī"ti. ^2-515

"Samādāya sikkhati sikkhāpadesū"ti tattha katamā sikkhā? Catasso sikkhā – bhikkhūnaṃ bhikkhusikkhā, bhikkhunīnaṃ bhikkhunisikkhā, upāsakānaṃ upāsakasikkhā, upāsikānaṃ upāsikasikkhā. Imā vuccanti "sikkhāyo". Iti imāsu sikkhāsu sabbena sabbaṃ sabbathā sabbaṃ asesaṃ nissesaṃ samādāya vattati. Tena vuccati "samādāya sikkhati sikkhāpadesū"ti. ^2-516

"Indriyesu guttadvāro"ti atthi indriyesu guttadvāratā, atthi aguttadvāratā.
Tattha katamā indriyesu aguttadvāratā? Idhekacco cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā nimittaggāhī hoti anubyañjanaggāhī. Yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ cakkhundriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya na paṭipajjati, na rakkhati cakkhundriyaṃ, cakkhundriye na saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Sotena saddaṃ sutvā…pe… ghānena gandhaṃ ghāyitvā…pe… jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā…pe… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā…pe… manasā dhammaṃ viññāya nimittaggāhī hoti anubyañjanaggāhī. Yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ manindriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya na paṭipajjati, na rakkhati manindriyaṃ, manindriye na saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Yā imesaṃ channaṃ indriyānaṃ agutti agopanā anārakkho asaṃvaro – ayaṃ vuccati "indriyesu aguttadvāratā".
Tattha katamā indriyesu guttadvāratā? Idhekacco cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī. Yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ cakkhundriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya paṭipajjati, rakkhati cakkhundriyaṃ, cakkhundriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Sotena saddaṃ sutvā…pe… ghānena gandhaṃ ghāyitvā…pe… jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā…pe… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā…pe… manasā dhammaṃ viññāya na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī. Ñatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ manindriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya paṭipajjati, rakkhati manindriyaṃ, manindriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Yā imesaṃ channaṃ indriyānaṃ gutti gopanā ārakkho saṃvaro – ayaṃ vuccati "indriyesu guttadvāratā". Imāya indriyesu guttadvāratāya upeto hoti samupeto…pe… samannāgato. Tena vuccati "indriyesu guttadvāro"ti. ^2-517

"Bhojane mattaññū"ti atthi bhojane mattaññutā, atthi bhojane amattaññutā.
Tattha katamā bhojane amattaññutā? Idhekacco appaṭisaṅkhā ayoniso āhāraṃ āhāreti davāya madāya maṇḍanāya vibhūsanāya. Yā tattha asantuṭṭhitā amattaññutā appaṭisaṅkhā bhojane – ayaṃ vuccati "bhojane amattaññutā"ti.
Tattha katamā bhojane mattaññutā? Idhekacco paṭisaṅkhā yoniso āhāraṃ āhāreti – "neva davāya na madāya na maṇḍanāya na vibhūsanāya, yāvadeva imassa kāyassa ṭhitiyā yāpanāya vihiṃsūparatiyā brahmacariyānuggahāya, iti purāṇañca vedanaṃ paṭihaṅkhāmi navañca vedanaṃ na uppādessāmi yātrā ca me bhavissati anavajjatā ca phāsu vihāro cā"ti. Yā tattha santuṭṭhitā mattaññutā paṭisaṅkhā bhojane – ayaṃ vuccati "bhojane mattaññutā". Imāya bhojane mattaññutāya upeto hoti…pe… samannāgato. Tena vuccati "bhojane mattaññū"ti. ^2-518

## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ (Jhāna)

[[#^toc-12-1|↑↑↑]]

Mātikaṃ paṭipāṭiyā bhājento "imissā diṭṭhiyā"tiādīhi padehi sikkhattayasaṅkhātaṃ sabbaññubuddhasāsanameva dasseti. Tattha pātimokkhanti sikkhāpadasīlaṃ; yo naṃ pāti rakkhati, taṃ āpāyikādīhi dukkhehi mokkheti mocayatīti pātimokkhaṃ; tañca sīlaṃ sabbesaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ patiṭṭhā ādi ca. Yathā hi nagaravaḍḍhakī paṭhamaṃ nagaraṭṭhānaṃ sodheti, evameva yogāvacaro āditova sīlaṃ visodhetvā aparabhāge samathavipassanāmaggaphalanibbānāni sacchikaroti. So ājīvaṃ parisodhetvā anācārāgocare pahāya, aṇumattānipi vajjāni aṭṭhasaṭṭhiyojanasatasahassubbedhasinerupabbatasadisāni katvā bhayato passanto, indriyesu guttadvāro bhojane mattaññū pubbarattāpararattaṃ jāgariyānuyogamanuyutto satisampajaññena sabbakiccakārī, vivittaṃ appasaddaṃ appanigghosaṃ paṭisallānasāruppaṃ senāsanaṃ bhajitvā, pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā ujuṃ kāyaṃ paṇidhāya parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā, abhijjhaṃ byāpādaṃ thinamiddhaṃ uddhaccakukkuccaṃ vicikicchañcāti ime pañca nīvaraṇe cetaso upakkilese paññāya dubbalīkaraṇe pahāya cittaṃ parisodheti. Evaṃ pahīnanīvaraṇo vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ, tato sampasādapītisukhacittekaggatāsaṅkhātāni aṅgāni gahetvā dutiyatatiyacatutthajjhānāni upasampajja viharati; "vivicceva kāmehī"tiādīsu pana yaṃ vattabbaṃ siyā, taṃ heṭṭhā cittuppādakaṇḍe rūpāvacaraniddese vuttanayeneva veditabbaṃ.

### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ (Jhāna)

[[#^toc-12-1|↑↑↑]]

Suttantabhājanīye jhānassa pubbabhāgo vitthārena dassito — sīlavisuddhi, ājīvapārisuddhi, indriyasaṃvaro, jāgariyānuyogo, satisampajaññaṃ, vivittasenāsanapariggaho, nīvaraṇappahānañca. Tasmā yoginā aṇumattānipi vajjāni aṭṭhasaṭṭhiyojanasatasahassubbedhasinerupabbatasadisāni katvā bhayato passantena, anācārāgocaraṃ pahāya, ājīvaṃ sodhetvā, "idaṃ me pāpaṃ na karaṇīya"nti sabbaṃ dussīlyaṃ pariccajitvā, indriyesu guttadvārena bhojane mattaññunā satisampajaññena ca kusalā dhammā upasampādetabbā; gatapaccāgatavattaṃ pūrento kammaṭṭhānaṃ avissajjetvā vivittaṃ appasaddaṃ appanigghosaṃ senāsanaṃ bhajitvā, pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā ujuṃ kāyaṃ paṇidhāya parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā, abhijjhaṃ byāpādaṃ thinamiddhaṃ uddhaccakukkuccaṃ vicikicchañcāti pañca nīvaraṇe pahāya, tehi cetaso upakkilesehi cittaṃ parisodhetvā, vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānādīni upasampajja cittaṃ ekaggaṃ katvā pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

## English translation (from `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-Contemporary-English-Abhidhamma/pi-2-english-plain-zeroshot.md`)

**Format note.** Track-folder copy of the one-shot pass (promoted 2026-09-01). It carries an `![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-N]]` transclusion line above each verse and does **not** print the verse number; the `**v. N.**` label in the day file's §4 is supplied by the composer.

Here a monk dwells restrained by the restraint of the monastic code, endowed with proper conduct and proper resort, seeing danger in the slightest faults; he trains himself, having undertaken the rules of training; he guards the doors of the faculties, knows moderation in food, and is devoted to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night, devoted with perseverance and care to the development of the things that side with awakening. He acts with clear comprehension when going forward and returning; he acts with clear comprehension when looking ahead and looking away; he acts with clear comprehension when bending and stretching; he acts with clear comprehension when carrying the outer robe, the bowl, and the robes; he acts with clear comprehension when eating, drinking, chewing, and tasting; he acts with clear comprehension when defecating and urinating; he acts with clear comprehension when walking, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, speaking, and keeping silent. He resorts to a secluded dwelling place — a forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a glen, a mountain cave, a charnel ground, a remote forest, the open air, a heap of straw — with little sound, little noise, without the breath of crowds, hidden from people, suitable for retreat. Gone to the forest, or to the foot of a tree, or to an empty hut, he sits down, having folded his legs crosswise, set his body straight, and established mindfulness in front of him. Having abandoned covetousness with regard to the world, he dwells with a mind free of covetousness; he purifies his mind of covetousness. Having abandoned ill will and hatred, he dwells with a mind free of ill will, compassionate for the welfare of all living beings; he purifies his mind of ill will and hatred. Having abandoned dullness and drowsiness, he dwells free of dullness and drowsiness, percipient of light, mindful and clearly comprehending; he purifies his mind of dullness and drowsiness. Having abandoned restlessness and remorse, he dwells unagitated, with a mind inwardly stilled; he purifies his mind of restlessness and remorse. Having abandoned doubt, he dwells having crossed over doubt, without perplexity about wholesome things; he purifies his mind of doubt. Having abandoned these five hindrances, corruptions of the mind that weaken wisdom — secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome things, he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, which is with thought and with examination, with joy and happiness born of seclusion; with the stilling of thought and examination, he enters and dwells in the second meditative absorption, which has inner confidence and unification of mind, is without thought and without examination, with joy and happiness born of concentration; with the fading away of joy he dwells equanimous, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body — that of which the noble ones declare, "equanimous, mindful, one who dwells happily" — he enters and dwells in the third meditative absorption; with the abandoning of happiness and the abandoning of pain, and with the earlier disappearance of joy and grief, he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant and has purity of mindfulness through equanimity; completely passing beyond perceptions of material form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, not attending to perceptions of diversity, aware that "space is infinite", he enters and dwells in the field of infinite space; completely passing beyond the field of infinite space, aware that "consciousness is infinite", he enters and dwells in the field of infinite consciousness; completely passing beyond the field of infinite consciousness, aware that "there is nothing", he enters and dwells in the field of nothingness; completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, he enters and dwells in the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. ^2-508

"Here": in this view, in this acceptance, in this preference, in this taking-up, in this teaching, in this discipline, in this teaching-and-discipline, in this doctrine, in this holy life, in this teacher's dispensation. Therefore it is said, "here". ^2-509

"Monk": a monk by designation, a monk by acknowledgement, a monk because he begs, a monk because he is a beggar, a monk because he has taken up the life of begging, a monk because he wears the patched cloth, a monk because he breaks up evil, unwholesome things, a monk because he has broken up evil, unwholesome things, a monk through the partial abandoning of defilements, a monk through the complete abandoning of defilements, a monk in training, a monk beyond training, a monk neither in training nor beyond training, a foremost monk, an excellent monk, a monk in the prime, a monk of substance, a monk fully ordained by a united Order through an act with a motion and three announcements, unshakeable, fit to stand. ^2-510

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-511]]

"The monastic code": virtue, the foundation, the beginning, conduct, self-control, restraint, release, the foremost thing for the attainment of wholesome things. "Restraint": non-transgression in body, non-transgression in speech, non-transgression in body and speech. "Restrained": he is endowed with this restraint of the monastic code, fully endowed, having reached it, fully reached it, possessed of it, complete in it, furnished with it. Therefore it is said, "restrained by the restraint of the monastic code". ^2-511

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-512]]

"Dwells": he moves, goes on, keeps going, maintains himself, carries on, conducts himself, dwells. Therefore it is said, "dwells". ^2-512

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-513]]

"Endowed with proper conduct and proper resort": there is proper conduct, there is improper conduct.
Therein, what is improper conduct? Transgression in body, transgression in speech, transgression in body and speech — this is called "improper conduct". All bad conduct is improper conduct. Here someone makes his living by giving bamboo, or giving leaves, or giving flowers, or giving fruits, or giving bathing supplies, or giving tooth-sticks, or by flattery, or by half-truthful talk, or by fondling children, or by carrying messages on foot, or by some other wrong means of livelihood condemned by the Buddhas — this is called "improper conduct".
Therein, what is proper conduct? Non-transgression in body, non-transgression in speech, non-transgression in body and speech — this is called "proper conduct". All restraint through virtue is proper conduct. Here someone does not make his living by giving bamboo, nor by giving leaves, nor by giving flowers, nor by giving fruits, nor by giving bathing supplies, nor by giving tooth-sticks, nor by flattery, nor by half-truthful talk, nor by fondling children, nor by carrying messages on foot, nor by any other wrong means of livelihood condemned by the Buddhas — this is called "proper conduct". ^2-513

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-514]]

"Resort": there is proper resort, there is improper resort.
Therein, what is improper resort? Here someone frequents prostitutes, or frequents widows, or frequents grown-up unmarried women, or frequents eunuchs, or frequents nuns, or frequents taverns; he dwells associating with kings, king's ministers, teachers of other sects, and disciples of other sects, in unsuitable association; or he resorts to, frequents, and attends upon families that are without confidence and without trust, that are not like a well, that abuse and revile, that wish harm, wish ill, wish discomfort, and wish no security from bondage for monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen — this is called "improper resort".
Therein, what is proper resort? Here someone does not frequent prostitutes, does not frequent widows, does not frequent grown-up unmarried women, does not frequent eunuchs, does not frequent nuns, does not frequent taverns; he dwells not associating with kings, king's ministers, teachers of other sects, and disciples of other sects, in unsuitable association; and he resorts to, frequents, and attends upon families that have confidence and trust, that are like a well, that shine with the ochre robe, that are fanned by the breeze of sages, that wish good, wish welfare, wish comfort, and wish security from bondage for monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen — this is called "proper resort". So he is endowed with this proper conduct and this proper resort… furnished with them. Therefore it is said, "endowed with proper conduct and proper resort". ^2-514

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-515]]

"Seeing danger in the slightest faults": therein, what are the slightest faults? Those faults that are slight, minor, light, considered light, to be dealt with by self-control, to be dealt with by restraint, to be dealt with by the arising of a right state of mind, bound up with attentiveness — these are called "the slightest faults". So in these slightest faults he is one who sees the fault, sees the danger, sees the drawback, and sees the escape. Therefore it is said, "seeing danger in the slightest faults". ^2-515

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-516]]

"He trains himself, having undertaken the rules of training": therein, what is training? There are four trainings — for monks, the monks' training; for nuns, the nuns' training; for laymen, the laymen's training; for laywomen, the laywomen's training. These are called "the trainings". Having undertaken these trainings completely, in every respect, in every way, fully, without remainder, he proceeds in them. Therefore it is said, "he trains himself, having undertaken the rules of training". ^2-516

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-517]]

"He guards the doors of the faculties": there is guarding the doors of the faculties, and there is not guarding the doors.
Therein, what is not guarding the doors of the faculties? Here someone, having seen a sight with the eye, grasps at its signs and grasps at its details. Since, if he dwelt with the eye faculty unrestrained, covetousness and grief, evil, unwholesome things, might flow in upon him, he does not practise for its restraint, he does not guard the eye faculty, he does not undertake restraint of the eye faculty. Having heard a sound with the ear… having smelled a smell with the nose… having tasted a taste with the tongue… having touched a tangible thing with the body… having known a mental object with the mind, he grasps at its signs and grasps at its details. Since, if he dwelt with the mind faculty unrestrained, covetousness and grief, evil, unwholesome things, might flow in upon him, he does not practise for its restraint, he does not guard the mind faculty, he does not undertake restraint of the mind faculty. The lack of guarding, lack of protection, lack of watching over, lack of restraint of these six faculties — this is called "not guarding the doors of the faculties".
Therein, what is guarding the doors of the faculties? Here someone, having seen a sight with the eye, does not grasp at its signs and does not grasp at its details. Since, if he dwelt with the eye faculty unrestrained, covetousness and grief, evil, unwholesome things, might flow in upon him, he practises for its restraint, he guards the eye faculty, he undertakes restraint of the eye faculty. Having heard a sound with the ear… having smelled a smell with the nose… having tasted a taste with the tongue… having touched a tangible thing with the body… having known a mental object with the mind, he does not grasp at its signs and does not grasp at its details. Since, if he dwelt with the mind faculty unrestrained, covetousness and grief, evil, unwholesome things, might flow in upon him, he practises for its restraint, he guards the mind faculty, he undertakes restraint of the mind faculty. The guarding, protection, watching over, and restraint of these six faculties — this is called "guarding the doors of the faculties". He is endowed with this guarding of the doors of the faculties, fully endowed… furnished with it. Therefore it is said, "he guards the doors of the faculties". ^2-517

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-518]]

"Knows moderation in food": there is knowing moderation in food, and there is not knowing moderation in food.
Therein, what is not knowing moderation in food? Here someone takes food without reflection and unwisely — for amusement, for intoxication, for adornment, for beautification. The lack of contentment there, the lack of moderation, the lack of reflection with regard to food — this is called "not knowing moderation in food".
Therein, what is knowing moderation in food? Here someone takes food with reflection and wisely — "not for amusement, not for intoxication, not for adornment, not for beautification, but only for the persistence and maintenance of this body, for ending discomfort, for supporting the holy life, thinking: thus I shall put an end to the old feeling and not give rise to a new feeling, and I shall keep going, be blameless, and dwell in comfort." The contentment there, the moderation, the reflection with regard to food — this is called "knowing moderation in food". He is endowed with this knowing of moderation in food… furnished with it. Therefore it is said, "knows moderation in food". ^2-518

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-519]]

And how is a monk devoted to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night? Here a monk during the day purifies his mind of obstructive things by walking and sitting; in the first watch of the night he purifies his mind of obstructive things by walking and sitting; in the middle watch of the night he lies down on his right side in the lion's posture, placing one foot on the other, mindful and clearly comprehending, having attended to the idea of rising; in the last watch of the night, having risen, he purifies his mind of obstructive things by walking and sitting. Thus a monk is devoted to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night. ^2-519

"Perseverance": the mental initiation of energy… right effort. ^2-520

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-521]]

"Care": the wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view. ^2-521

"Devoted to the development of the things that side with awakening": therein, what are the things that side with awakening? The seven awakening factors — the awakening factor of mindfulness, the awakening factor of investigation of things, the awakening factor of energy, the awakening factor of joy, the awakening factor of tranquillity, the awakening factor of concentration, the awakening factor of equanimity. These are called "the things that side with awakening". So he practises those things that side with awakening, develops them, cultivates them. Therefore it is said, "devoted to the development of the things that side with awakening". ^2-522
