## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-summaries.md`)

#### Piṭṭhidukaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-4-2-13|↑↑↑]]

Piṭṭhidukanikkhepakathāyaṃ micchattattike ānantarikānīti anantarāyena phaladāyakāni; mātughātakakammādīnametaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Tesu ekasmimpi kamme kate taṃ paṭibāhitvā aññaṃ kammaṃ attano vipākassa okāsaṃ kātuṃ na sakkoti. Yā ca micchādiṭṭhi niyatāti ahetukavādaakiriyavādanatthikavādesu aññatarā – taṃ gahetvā ṭhitaṃ puggalaṃ buddhasatampi buddhasahassampi bodhetuṃ na sakkonti.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-practice.md`)

#### Piṭṭhidukaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-3-2-13|↑↑↑]]

Piṭṭhiduke dassanena bhāvanāya ca pahātabbā, tesañca hetū, micchattaniyatā sammattaniyatā aniyatā cāti piṭṭhibhūtā dukā nikkhittā. Tattha tīṇi saṃyojanāni — sakkāyadiṭṭhi vicikicchā sīlabbataparāmāso — tadekaṭṭhā ca kilesā dassanena pahātabbā, sesā rāgadosamohādayo bhāvanāya. Ānantarikāni pana kammāni anantarāyena phaladāyakattā aññena kusalakammena appaṭibāhiyāni, niyatā ca micchādiṭṭhi ahetuka-akiriya-natthikavādabhedā buddhasatenapi abodhanīyā. Tasmā yoginā ānantarikakammā ceva niyatamicchādiṭṭhito ca accantaṃ viramitvā, sammādiṭṭhiṃ purakkhatvā, sotāpattimaggena tīṇi saṃyojanāni uparimaggehi ca sesakilese anukkamena samucchindantena cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

## English translation (from `en-dhammasangani-ai.md`)

Among these, what is clinging to rites and rituals? The view among recluses and brahmins outside [this dispensation] that purity comes from morality, purity comes from observances, purity comes from morality and observances – such a view, wrong view... mistaken apprehension – this is called clinging to rites and rituals. These three fetters, and defilements rooted in them, the feeling aggregate... consciousness aggregate associated with them, bodily action, vocal action, mental action originating from them – these states have causes to be abandoned by seeing. The three fetters – identity view, doubt, clinging to rites and rituals – these states are to be abandoned by seeing. Greed, hatred, and delusion rooted in them – these states are causes to be abandoned by seeing. And defilements rooted in them, the feeling aggregate... consciousness aggregate associated with them, bodily action, vocal action, mental action originating from them – these states have causes to be abandoned by seeing. ^1-1271
Which states do not have causes to be abandoned by seeing? Excluding those states, the remaining wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate states in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and unincluded; the feeling aggregate... consciousness aggregate; all matter, and the unconditioned element – these states do not have causes to be abandoned by seeing. ^1-1272
Which phenomena have causes to be abandoned by development? The remaining greed, hatred, delusion – these phenomena are causes to be abandoned by development. And defilements associated with them, the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate associated with them, and bodily, verbal, and mental kamma originated from them – these phenomena have causes to be abandoned by development. ^1-1273
Which phenomena do not have causes to be abandoned by development? Excluding those phenomena, the remaining wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate phenomena in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and supramundane; the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate; and all material phenomena, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena do not have causes to be abandoned by development. ^1-1274
Which phenomena are accompanied by initial application? In the sphere of initial application, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, and supramundane, excluding initial application, the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate associated with it – these phenomena are accompanied by initial application. ^1-1275
Which phenomena are not accompanied by initial application? In the sphere without initial application, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and supramundane; the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate; and initial application, and all material phenomena, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena are not accompanied by initial application. ^1-1276
Which phenomena are accompanied by sustained application? In the sphere of sustained application, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, and supramundane, excluding sustained application, the feeling aggregate, the perception aggregate, the mental formations aggregate, the consciousness aggregate associated with it – these phenomena are accompanied by sustained application. ^1-1277
Which phenomena are not accompanied by sustained application? In the sphere without sustained application, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and supramundane; the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate; and sustained application, and all material phenomena, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena are not accompanied by sustained application. ^1-1278
Which phenomena are accompanied by rapture? In the sphere of rapture, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, and supramundane, excluding rapture, the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate associated with it – these phenomena are accompanied by rapture. ^1-1279
Which phenomena are not accompanied by rapture? In the sphere without rapture, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and supramundane; the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate; and rapture, and all material phenomena, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena are not accompanied by rapture. ^1-1280
Which phenomena are concomitant with rapture? In the sphere of rapture, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, and supramundane, excluding rapture, the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate associated with it – these phenomena are concomitant with rapture. ^1-1281
Which phenomena are not concomitant with rapture? In the sphere without rapture, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and supramundane; the feeling aggregate...pe...the consciousness aggregate; and rapture, and all material phenomena, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena are not concomitant with rapture. ^1-1282
Which phenomena are accompanied by pleasure?
In the plane of pleasure, in the sense-sphere, form-sphere, and the unincluded, excluding pleasure itself, the associated aggregate of perception, aggregate of formations, and aggregate of consciousness – these phenomena are accompanied by pleasure. ^1-1283
Which phenomena are not accompanied by pleasure?
Not in the plane of pleasure, in the sense-sphere, form-sphere, formless-sphere, or unincluded; the aggregate of feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness; and pleasure itself, all matter, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena are not accompanied by pleasure. ^1-1284
Which phenomena are accompanied by equanimity?
In the plane of equanimity, in the sense-sphere, form-sphere, formless-sphere, and the unincluded, excluding equanimity itself, the associated aggregate of perception, aggregate of formations, and aggregate of consciousness – these phenomena are accompanied by equanimity. ^1-1285
Which phenomena are not accompanied by equanimity?
Not in the plane of equanimity, in the sense-sphere, form-sphere, or unincluded; the aggregate of feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness; and equanimity itself, all matter, and the unconditioned element – these phenomena are not accompanied by equanimity. ^1-1286
Which phenomena belong to the sense-sphere?
Taking Avīci hell as the lower boundary, and the Paranimmita-vasavattī devas as the upper boundary, whatever aggregates, elements, and sense-bases are included within this range, namely matter, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness – these phenomena belong to the sense-sphere. ^1-1287
Which phenomena do not belong to the sense-sphere?
Phenomena of the form-sphere, formless-sphere, and the unincluded – these phenomena do not belong to the sense-sphere. ^1-1288
Which phenomena belong to the form-sphere?
Taking the Brahma-world as the lower boundary, and the Akanittha devas as the upper boundary, whatever mental states and mental factors are included within this range, belonging to one who has attained, or one who has been reborn, or one who dwells in happiness in this very life – these phenomena belong to the form-sphere. ^1-1289
Which phenomena do not belong to the form-sphere?
Phenomena of the sense-sphere, formless-sphere, and the unincluded – these phenomena do not belong to the form-sphere. ^1-1290
Which phenomena belong to the formless-sphere?
Taking the devas of the base of infinite space as the lower boundary, and the devas of the base of neither perception nor non-perception as the upper boundary, whatever mental states and mental factors are included within this range, belonging to one who has attained, or one who has been reborn, or one who dwells in happiness in this very life – these phenomena belong to the formless-sphere. ^1-1291
Which phenomena do not belong to the formless-sphere?
Phenomena of the sense-sphere, form-sphere, and the unincluded – these phenomena do not belong to the formless-sphere. ^1-1292
Which phenomena are included?
Phenomena subject to taints, wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate, in the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere; the form aggregate... up to... the consciousness aggregate—these phenomena are included. ^1-1293
Which phenomena are not included?
The paths, the path fruits, and the unconditioned element—these phenomena are not included. ^1-1294
Which phenomena lead to liberation?
The four not-included paths—these phenomena lead to liberation. ^1-1295
