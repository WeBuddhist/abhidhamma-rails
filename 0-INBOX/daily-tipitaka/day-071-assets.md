## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-summaries.md`)

### Tikaatthuddhāro

[[#^toc-5-1|↑↑↑]]

Tikaatthuddhāravaṇṇanāyaṃ catūsūti kāmāvacararūpāvacarārūpāvacaraapariyāpannāsu. Kusalanti phassādibhedaṃ kusalaṃ – ime dhammā kusalā nāma. Akusalānaṃ pana bhūmivasena bhedābhāvato dvādasa akusalacittuppādāti āha. Tattha 'rājā āgato'ti vutte amaccādīnampi āgamanaṃ vuttameva hoti – evaṃ 'cittuppādā'ti vutte tehi sampayuttadhammāpi vuttāva hontīti; cittuppādaggahaṇena sasampayuttadhammaṃ cittaṃ gahitanti veditabbaṃ. Ito paresu pana catūsu bhūmīsu vipākotiādīnaṃ sabbesampi tikadukabhājanīyapadānaṃ attho visesamattameva dassetvā veditabbo.

### Dukaatthuddhāro

[[#^toc-5-2|↑↑↑]]

Dukaatthuddhāravaṇṇanāyaṃ parittārammaṇattike tāva sabbo kāmāvacarassa vipākoti ettha dvipañcaviññāṇāni cakkhupasādādayo nissāya niyameneva iṭṭhāniṭṭhādibhede rūpasaddagandharasaphoṭṭhabbadhamme ārabbha pavattantīti parittārammaṇāni. Maggārammaṇattike ādimhi vuttā aṭṭha ñāṇasampayuttacittuppādā sekkhāsekkhānaṃ attanā paṭividdhamaggānaṃ paccavekkhaṇakāle maggārammaṇā. Atītārammaṇattike viññāṇañcāyatananevasaññānāsaññāyatanadhammā heṭṭhā atītasamāpattiṃ ārabbha pavattitā ekantena atītārammaṇāva. Ajjhattārammaṇattike anindriyabaddharūpañca nibbānañca bahiddhāti niyakajjhattapariyāyassa abhāvena bahiddhāti vuttaṃ.

#### Hetugocchakaṃ (Atthuddhāra)

[[#^toc-5-2-1|↑↑↑]]

Hetugocchakānaṃ atthuddhāraniddese tayo kusalahetūtiādinā nayena hetū dassetvā puna teyeva uppattiṭṭhānato dassetuṃ catūsu bhūmīsu kusalesu uppajjantītiādi vuttaṃ. Iminā upāyena sesagocchakesupi desanānayo veditabbo – hetudhammavasena, bhūmivasena, kammadvāravasena ca atthuddhārā kātā.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-practice.md`)

### Tikaatthuddhāro

[[#^toc-4-1|↑↑↑]]

Tikaatthuddhāre kusalādayo dhammā tikavasena uddhaṭā — catūsu bhūmīsu (kāmāvacararūpāvacaraarūpāvacaraapariyāpannāsu) kusalaṃ, bhūmibhedābhāvato dvādasa akusalacittuppādā, vipākakiriyarūpanibbānavasena abyākatañca. "Cittuppādā"ti vutte tehi sampayuttā cetasikāpi gahitāva honti, yathā "rājā āgato"ti vutte amaccādayopi āgatā honti. Tasmā yoginā ekekaṃ cittuppādaṃ "kusalo ayaṃ, akusalo ayaṃ, abyākato aya"nti sasampayuttadhammaṃ sallakkhetvā, dvādasa akusalacittuppāde pahānāya, catubhūmakaṃ kusalaṃ upasampadāya ussāhaṃ katvā, sukhadukkhādivedanāsampayoge ca satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā, cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-dhammasangani-atthakatha.md#^1-1885]]
[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-dhammasangani-atthakatha.md#^1-1888]]

### Dukaatthuddhāro

[[#^toc-4-2|↑↑↑]]

Dukaatthuddhāre dhammā dukavasena ārammaṇādito uddhaṭā — parittamahaggatārammaṇā, maggārammaṇā, atītānāgatapaccuppannārammaṇā, ajjhattabahiddhārammaṇā ca. Tattha sekhāsekhānaṃ attano paṭividdhamaggaṃ paccavekkhaṇakāle aṭṭha ñāṇasampayuttacittuppādā maggārammaṇā honti; anindriyabaddharūpaṃ nibbānañca bahiddhāti vuttaṃ. Tasmā yoginā "kiṃ ārammaṇaṃ me citta"nti ārammaṇaṃ pariggahetvā, kāmaparittārammaṇesu rāgaṃ pahāya, maggaṃ nibbānañca ārammaṇaṃ katvā, ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-dhammasangani-atthakatha.md#^1-1889]]
[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-dhammasangani-atthakatha.md#^1-1929]]

#### Hetugocchakaṃ (Atthuddhāra)

[[#^toc-4-2-1|↑↑↑]]

Hetuatthuddhāre tayo kusalahetū (alobho adoso amoho) tayo akusalahetū (lobho doso moho) ca uppattiṭṭhānato uddhaṭā — "catūsu bhūmīsu kusalesu uppajjantī"tiādinā. Yathā mūle sati rukkho patiṭṭhāti, mūle chinne patati, evaṃ hetumhi sati phalaṃ pavattati. Tasmā yoginā "kiṃmūlako ayaṃ dhammo"ti hetuṃ pariggahetvā, akusalahetumūlaṃ "idha uppajjatī"ti ñatvā paññāya samucchinditvā, kusalahetumūlaṃ tesu tesu ṭhānesu vaḍḍhetvā, cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

## English translation (from `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-Contemporary-English-Abhidhamma/en-dhammasangani-ai.md`)

Which states are perfected? The highest fruit of Arahantship – these states are perfected. ^1-1415

Which states are neither learners nor perfected? Wholesome states in the three planes, unwholesome states, resultant states in the three planes, functional indeterminate states in the three planes, and matter, and Nibbāna – these states are neither learners nor perfected. ^1-1416

Which states are limited? Wholesome states of the sense sphere, unwholesome states, all resultant states of the sense sphere, functional indeterminate states of the sense sphere, and all matter – these states are limited. ^1-1417

What dhammas are exalted? Form-sphere, formless-sphere, wholesome-indeterminate – these dhammas are exalted. ^1-1418

What dhammas are immeasurable? The four paths not included (in the realms), the four fruitions of recluseship, and Nibbāna – these dhammas are immeasurable. ^1-1419

What dhammas have limited objects? All resultant consciousness of the sense-sphere, functional mind-element, functional rootless mind-consciousness element accompanied by joy – these dhammas have limited objects. ^1-1420

What dhammas have exalted objects? The base of infinite consciousness, the base of neither perception nor non-perception – these dhammas have exalted objects. ^1-1421

What dhammas have immeasurable objects? The four paths not included (in the realms), the four fruitions of recluseship – these dhammas have immeasurable objects. From wholesome sense-sphere consciousness, the four consciousnesses dissociated from knowledge; from functional consciousness, the four consciousnesses dissociated from knowledge; all unwholesome (consciousness) – these dhammas may have limited objects, may have exalted objects, not immeasurable objects, and may not be describable as having limited objects or as having exalted objects. From wholesome sense-sphere consciousness, the four consciousnesses associated with knowledge; from functional consciousness, the four consciousnesses associated with knowledge; the fourth rūpa-jhāna, both wholesome and functional; functional rootless mind-consciousness element accompanied by equanimity – these dhammas may have limited objects, may have exalted objects, may have immeasurable objects, and may not be describable as having limited objects, or as having exalted objects, or as having immeasurable objects. The rūpa-jhānas of the triple and quadruple systems, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the resultant of the fourth jhāna; the base of infinite space; the base of nothingness – these dhammas are not describable as having limited objects, or as having exalted objects, or as having immeasurable objects. Matter and Nibbāna are without object. ^1-1422

What dhammas are inferior? The twelve unwholesome consciousnesses – these dhammas are inferior. ^1-1423

What dhammas are middling? Wholesome (consciousness) in the three planes, resultant (consciousness) in the three planes, functional indeterminate (consciousness) in the three planes, and all matter – these dhammas are middling. ^1-1424

What dhammas are superior? The four paths not included (in the realms), the four fruitions of recluseship, and Nibbāna – these dhammas are superior. ^1-1425

What dhammas are fixed in wrongness? The four consciousnesses associated with wrong view, the two consciousnesses accompanied by displeasure – these dhammas may be fixed in wrongness, may be unfixed. ^1-1426

What dhammas are fixed in rightness? The four paths not included (in the realms) – these dhammas are fixed in rightness. ^1-1427

Which phenomena are indeterminate? The four consciousnesses accompanied by greed dissociated from wrong view, consciousness accompanied by doubt, consciousness accompanied by restlessness, wholesome (consciousness) in the three planes, resultant (consciousness) in the four planes, functional indeterminate (consciousness) in the three planes, matter, and Nibbāna – these phenomena are indeterminate. ^1-1428

Which phenomena are path-objects? The four wisdom-associated wholesome consciousnesses of the sense sphere, and the four wisdom-associated functional consciousnesses – these phenomena may be path-objects, are not path-causes; they may be path-dominant, or cannot be described as path-objects or path-dominant. The four Noble Paths are not path-objects, are path-causes; they may be path-dominant, or cannot be described as path-dominant. The fourth fine-material jhána, wholesome and functional, and the functional rootless mind-consciousness element accompanied by equanimity – these phenomena may be path-objects; are not path-causes, are not path-dominant; or cannot be described as path-objects. The four wisdom-dissociated wholesome consciousnesses of the sense sphere, all unwholesome (consciousness), all resultant (consciousness) of the sense sphere, the six functional consciousnesses, the three and fourfold fine-material jhānas, wholesome, resultant, and functional, the resultant of the fourth jhána, the four immaterial (attainments), wholesome, resultant, and functional, and the four fruits of recluseship – these phenomena cannot be described as path-objects, path-causes, or path-dominant. Matter and Nibbāna are objectless. ^1-1429

Which phenomena are arisen? Resultant (consciousness) in the four planes and matter born of kamma – these phenomena may be arisen, may be arising; cannot be described as unarisen. Wholesome (consciousness) and unwholesome (consciousness) in the four planes, functional indeterminate (consciousness) in the three planes, and matter not born of kamma – these phenomena may be arisen, may be unarisen; cannot be described as arising. Nibbāna cannot be described as arisen, unarisen, or arising. ^1-1430

Except Nibbāna, all phenomena may be past, may be future, may be present. Nibbāna cannot be described as past, future, or present. ^1-1431

Which phenomena have past objects? The base of infinite consciousness and the base of neither perception nor non-perception – these phenomena have past objects. ^1-1432

Necessarily, there are no future objects. ^1-1433

Which phenomena have present objects? The two sets of fivefold sense consciousnesses and the three mind-elements – these phenomena have present objects. The ten resultant consciousnesses of the sense sphere, the resultant mind-consciousness element accompanied by equanimity of the unwholesome, the functional rootless mind-consciousness element accompanied by joy – these phenomena may have past objects, may have future objects, may have present objects. Wholesome (consciousness) of the sense sphere, unwholesome (consciousness), the nine functional consciousnesses, the fourth fine-material jhána, wholesome and functional – these phenomena may have past objects, may have future objects, may have present objects; or cannot be described as having past, future, or present objects. The three and fourfold fine-material jhānas, wholesome, resultant, and functional, the resultant of the fourth jhána, the base of infinite space, the base of nothingness, the four supramundane paths, and the four fruits of recluseship – these phenomena cannot be described as having past, future, or present objects. Matter and Nibbāna are objectless. ^1-1434

Except matter not bound to faculties and Nibbāna, all phenomena may be internal, may be external, may be internal and external. Matter not bound to faculties and Nibbāna are external. ^1-1435

Which phenomena have internal objects? The base of infinite consciousness and the base of neither perception nor non-perception – these phenomena have internal objects. ^1-1436

Which phenomena have external objects? The three and fourfold fine-material jhānas, wholesome, resultant, and functional, the resultant of the fourth jhána, the base of infinite space, the four supramundane paths, and the four fruits of recluseship – these phenomena have external objects. Except matter, all wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate phenomena of the sense sphere, and the fourth fine-material jhána, wholesome and functional – these phenomena may have internal objects, may have external objects, may have internal and external objects. The base of nothingness cannot be described as having internal, external, or internal and external objects. Matter and Nibbāna are objectless. ^1-1437

What are the phenomena that are visible and with resistance? The visible object – these phenomena are visible and with resistance. ^1-1438

What are the phenomena that are invisible but with resistance? The eye-base... the tangible object – these phenomena are invisible but with resistance. ^1-1439

What are the phenomena that are invisible and without resistance? Wholesome in the four planes, unwholesome, resultant in the four planes, functional in the three planes, and that matter which is invisible and without resistance and is included in the dharma-base, and Nibbāna – these phenomena are invisible and without resistance.

#### Elucidation of Dyads ^1-4-2-0

##### Chapter on Roots ^1-4-2-1-0

What are the phenomena that are roots? The three wholesome roots, the three unwholesome roots, the three indeterminate roots. Non-greed is a wholesome root, non-hatred is a wholesome root, they arise in wholesome states in the four planes. Non-delusion is a wholesome root, it arises in wholesome states in the four planes, excluding the four consciousnesses dissociated from knowledge in sensuous wholesome states.
Greed arises in the eight consciousnesses accompanied by greed. Hatred arises in the two consciousnesses accompanied by displeasure. Delusion arises in all unwholesome states.
Non-greed is a resultant root, non-hatred is a resultant root, they arise in resultant states in the four planes, excluding the rootless consciousnesses of the sensuous resultant. Non-delusion is a resultant root, it arises in resultant states in the four planes, excluding the rootless consciousnesses of the sensuous resultant, and excluding the four consciousnesses dissociated from knowledge.
Non-greed is a functional root, non-hatred is a functional root, they arise in functional states in the three planes, excluding the rootless consciousnesses of the sensuous functional. Non-delusion is a functional root, it arises in functional states in the three planes, excluding the rootless consciousnesses of the sensuous functional, and excluding the four consciousnesses dissociated from knowledge – these phenomena are roots. ^1-1441
