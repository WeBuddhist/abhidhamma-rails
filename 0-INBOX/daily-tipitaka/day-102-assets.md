## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Sacca)

[[#^toc-4-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye 'ariyasaccānī'ti avatvā nippadesato paccayasaṅkhātaṃ samudayaṃ dassetuṃ 'cattāri saccānī'ti vuttaṃ. Ariyasaccānīti hi vutte avasesā ca kilesā, avasesā ca akusalā dhammā, tīṇi ca kusalamūlāni sāsavāni, avasesā ca sāsavā kusalā dhammā na saṅgayhanti; na ca kevalaṃ taṇhāva dukkhaṃ samudāneti, imepi avasesā kilesādayo paccayā samudānentiyeva. Niddesavāre ca nesaṃ paṭhamaṃ dukkhaṃ aniddisitvā tasseva dukkhassa sukhaniddesatthaṃ dukkhasamudayo niddiṭṭho; nirodhasaccaṃ yathāvuttassa samudayassa pahānavasena pañcahākārehi niddiṭṭhaṃ; maggasaccaṃ paṭhamajjhānikasotāpattimaggavasena dhammasaṅgaṇiyaṃ vibhattassa desanānayassa mukhamattameva dassentena niddiṭṭhaṃ. Yasmā pana na kevalaṃ aṭṭhaṅgiko maggova paṭipadā, puggalajjhāsayavasena pañcaṅgikopi maggo paṭipadā evāti tassa nayassa dassanatthaṃ pañcaṅgikavāropi niddiṭṭho; yasmā ca sampayuttakā atirekapaññāsadhammāpi paṭipadā eva, tasmā tatiyo sabbasaṅgāhikavāropi niddiṭṭho. Evamidaṃ tividhamahāvāraṃ pañcadasakoṭṭhāsaṃ saṭṭhinayasahassapaṭimaṇḍitaṃ abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ nāma niddiṭṭhanti veditabbaṃ; pāḷi pana saṅkhepena āgatā.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-practice.md`)

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Sacca)

[[#^toc-4-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye "ariyasaccānī"ti avatvā "cattāri saccānī"ti vuttaṃ, nippadesato paccayasaṅkhātaṃ samudayaṃ dassetuṃ. Na hi kevalaṃ taṇhāva dukkhaṃ samudāneti; avasesā ca kilesā, avasesā ca akusalā dhammā, tīṇi ca kusalamūlāni sāsavāni, avasesā ca sāsavā kusalā dhammāti imepi paccayā dukkhaṃ samudānentiyeva. Tenevettha nirodhasaccaṃ "taṇhāya ca avasesānañca kilesānaṃ pahāna"nti yathāvuttassa samudayassa pahānavasena pañcahākārehi niddiṭṭhaṃ, maggasaccañca aṭṭhaṅgikapañcaṅgikasabbasaṅgāhikavārehi tividhamahāvāraṃ saṭṭhinayasahassapaṭimaṇḍitaṃ dassitaṃ. Tasmā yoginā "na kevalaṃ taṇhāva dukkhassa paccayo"ti ñatvā, avasesepi kilese akusaladhamme sāsave ca kusaladhamme dukkhasamudayabhāvena pariggaṇhitvā, te sabbe pahāya, nānānayehi ariyamaggaṃ bhāvetvā, nippadesena samudayanirodhena cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

207. Therein, what is the origin of suffering? Craving and the remaining defilements – this is called "the origin of suffering".
Therein, what is suffering? The remaining unwholesome states, the three wholesome roots that are subject to the mental taints, the remaining wholesome states subject to the taints, the results of wholesome and unwholesome states subject to the taints, the states that are mere functioning – neither wholesome nor unwholesome nor the results of action – and all material form – this is called "suffering".
Therein, what is the ending of suffering? The abandoning of craving and of the remaining defilements – this is called "the ending of suffering".
Therein, what is the way leading to the ending of suffering? Here, on an occasion when a monk develops the meditative absorption that is beyond the world, leading out of the round, leading to dismantling, for the abandoning of wrong views and for reaching the first stage – secluded from sense pleasures… he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, the way being difficult and direct knowledge slow – on that occasion there is the eightfold path: right view… right concentration. This is called "the way leading to the ending of suffering". The remaining states are associated with the way leading to the ending of suffering. ^2-207
