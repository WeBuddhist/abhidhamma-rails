## Pāli source verses

### Aṭṭhakathākaṇḍaṃ ^1-4-0
#### Tikaatthuddhāro ^1-4-1-0

1384. Katame dhammā kusalā? Catūsu bhūmīsu kusalaṃ – ime dhammā kusalā. ^1-1384

1385. Katame dhammā akusalā? Dvādasa akusalacittuppādā – ime dhammā akusalā. ^1-1385

1386. Katame dhammā abyākatā? Catūsu bhūmīsu vipāko, tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākataṃ, rūpañca, nibbānañca – ime dhammā abyākatā. ^1-1386

1387. Katame dhammā sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā? Kāmāvacarakusalato cattāro somanassasahagatacittuppādā, akusalato cattāro kāmāvacarakusalassa vipākato ca kiriyato ca pañca, rūpāvacaratikacatukkajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, lokuttaratikacatukkajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca, etthuppannaṃ sukhaṃ vedanaṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā. ^1-1387

1388. Katame dhammā dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttā? Dve domanassasahagatacittuppādā, dukkhasahagataṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ, etthuppannaṃ dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttā. ^1-1388

1389. Katame dhammā adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā? Kāmāvacarakusalato cattāro upekkhāsahagatacittuppādā, akusalato cha, kāmāvacarakusalassa vipākato dasa, akusalassa vipākato cha, kiriyato cha, rūpāvacaraṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, cattāro arūpāvacarā kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, lokuttaraṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca, etthuppannaṃ adukkhamasukhaṃ vedanaṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā. Tisso ca vedanā, rūpañca, nibbānañca – ime dhammā na vattabbā sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttātipi, dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttātipi, ya vedanāya sampayuttātipi. ^1-1389

1390. Katame dhammā vipākā? Catūsu bhūmīsu vipāko – ime dhammā vipākā. ^1-1390

1391. Katame dhammā vipākadhammadhammā? Catūsu bhūmīsu kusalaṃ akusalaṃ – ime dhammā vipākadhammadhammā. ^1-1391

1392. Katame dhammā nevavipākanavipākadhammadhammā? Tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākataṃ, rūpañca, nibbānañca – ime dhammā nevavipākanavipākadhammadhammā. ^1-1392

1393. Katame dhammā upādiṇṇupādāniyā? Tīsu bhūmīsu vipāko, yañca rūpaṃ kammassa katattā – ime dhammā upādiṇṇupādāniyā. ^1-1393

1394. Katame dhammā anupādiṇṇupādāniyā? Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalaṃ, akusalaṃ, tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākataṃ , yañca rūpaṃ na kammassa katattā – ime dhammā anupādiṇṇupādāniyā. ^1-1394

1395. Katame dhammā anupādiṇṇaanupādāniyā? Cattāro maggā apariyāpannā, cattāri ca sāmaññaphalāni, nibbānañca – ime dhammā anupādiṇṇaanupādāniyā. ^1-1395

1396. Katame dhammā saṃkiliṭṭhasaṃkilesikā? Dvādasākusalacittuppādā – ime dhammā saṃkiliṭṭhasaṃkilesikā. ^1-1396

1397. Katame dhammā asaṃkiliṭṭhasaṃkilesikā? Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalaṃ, tīsu bhūmīsu vipāko, tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākataṃ, sabbañca rūpaṃ – ime dhammā asaṃkiliṭṭhasaṃkilesikā. ^1-1397

1398. Katame dhammā asaṃkiliṭṭhaasaṃkilesikā? Cattāro maggā apariyāpannā, cattāri ca sāmaññaphalāni, nibbānañca – ime dhammā asaṃkiliṭṭhaasaṃkilesikā. ^1-1398

1399. Katame dhammā savitakkasavicārā? Kāmāvacaraṃ kusalaṃ, akusalaṃ, kāmāvacarakusalassa vipākato ekādasa cittuppādā, akusalassa vipākato dve, kiriyato ekādasa, rūpāvacaraṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, lokuttaraṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca, etthuppanne vitakkavicāre ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā savitakkasavicārā. ^1-1399

1400. Katame dhammā avitakkavicāramattā? Rūpāvacarapañcakanaye dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, lokuttarapañcakanaye dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca, etthuppannaṃ vicāraṃ ṭhapetvā, vitakko ca – ime dhammā avitakkavicāramattā. ^1-1400

1401. Katame dhammā avitakkaavicārā? Dvepañcaviññāṇāni, rūpāvacaratikatikajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, cattāro āruppā kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca lokuttaratikatikajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca pañcakanaye dutiye jhāne [dutiyajjhāne (sī.)], uppanno ca vicāro rūpañca nibbānañca – ime dhammā avitakkaavicārā. Vitakkasahajāto vicāro na vattabbo savitakkasavicārotipi, avitakkavicāramattotipi, avitakkaavicārotipi. ^1-1401

1402. Katame dhammā pītisahagatā? Kāmāvacarakusalato cattāro somanassasahagatacittuppādā , akusalato cattāro, kāmāvacarakusalassa vipākato pañca, kiriyato pañca, rūpāvacaradukatikajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, lokuttaradukatikajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca, etthuppannaṃ pītiṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā pītisahagatā. ^1-1402

1403. Katame dhammā sukhasahagatā? Kāmāvacarakusalato cattāro somanassasahagatacittuppādā, akusalato cattāro, kāmāvacarakusalassa vipākato cha, kiriyato pañca, rūpāvacaratikacatukkajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, lokuttaratikacatukkajjhānā kusalato ca vipākato ca, etthuppannaṃ sukhaṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā sukhasahagatā. ^1-1403

1404. Katame dhammā upekkhāsahagatā? Kāmāvacarakusalato cattāro upekkhāsahagatacittuppādā, akusalato cha, kāmāvacarakusalassa vipākato dasa, akusalassa vipākato cha, kiriyato cha, rūpāvacaraṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, cattāro āruppā kusalato ca vipākato ca kiriyato ca, lokuttaraṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ kusalato ca vipākato ca, etthuppannaṃ upekkhaṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā upekkhāsahagatā. Pīti na pītisahagatā, sukhasahagatā, na upekkhāsahagatā. Sukhaṃ na sukhasahagataṃ, siyā pītisahagataṃ, na upekkhāsahagataṃ, siyā na vattabbaṃ pītisahagatanti. Dve domanassasahagatacittuppādā, dukkhasahagatakāyaviññāṇaṃ, yā ca vedanā upekkhā , rūpañca nibbānañca – ime dhammā na vattabbā pītisahagatātipi, sukhasahagatātipi, upekkhāsahagatātipi. ^1-1404

1405. Katame dhammā dassanena pahātabbā? Cattāro diṭṭhigatasampayuttacittuppādā, vicikicchāsahagato cittuppādo – ime dhammā dassanena pahātabbā. ^1-1405

1406. Katame dhammā bhāvanāya pahātabbā? Uddhaccasahagato cittuppādo – ime dhammā bhāvanāya pahātabbā. Cattāro diṭṭhigatavippayuttā lobhasahagatacittuppādā, dve domanassasahagatacittuppādā – ime dhammā siyā dassanena pahātabbā siyā bhāvanāya pahātabbā. ^1-1406

1407. Katame dhammā neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbā? Catūsu bhūmīsu kusalaṃ, catūsu bhūmīsu vipāko, tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākataṃ, rūpañca, nibbānañca – ime dhammā neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbā. ^1-1407

1408. Katame dhammā dassanena pahātabbahetukā? Cattāro diṭṭhigatasampayuttacittuppādā, vicikicchāsahagato cittuppādo, etthuppannaṃ mohaṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā dassanena pahātabbahetukā. ^1-1408

1409. Katame dhammā bhāvanāya pahātabbahetukā? Uddhaccasahagato cittuppādo, etthuppannaṃ mohaṃ ṭhapetvā – ime dhammā bhāvanāya pahātabbahetukā. Cattāro diṭṭhigatavippayuttā lobhasahagatacittuppādā, dve domanassasahagatacittuppādā – ime dhammā siyā dassanena pahātabbahetukā, siyā bhāvanāya pahātabbahetukā. ^1-1409

1410. Katame dhammā neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbahetukā? Vicikicchāsahagato moho, uddhaccasahagato moho, catūsu bhūmīsu kusalaṃ, catūsu bhūmīsu vipāko, tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākataṃ, rūpañca, nibbānañca – ime dhammā neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbahetukā. ^1-1410

1411. Katame dhammā ācayagāmino? Tīsu bhūmīsu kusalaṃ, akusalaṃ – ime dhammā ācayagāmino. ^1-1411

1412. Katame dhammā apacayagāmino? Cattāro maggā apariyāpannā – ime dhammā apacayagāmino. ^1-1412

1413. Katame dhammā nevācayagāmināpacayagāmino? Catūsu bhūmīsu vipāko, tīsu bhūmīsu kiriyābyākataṃ, rūpañca, nibbānañca – ime dhammā nevācayagāmināpacayagāmino. ^1-1413

1414. Katame dhammā sekkhā? Cattāro maggā apariyāpannā, heṭṭhimāni ca tīṇi sāmaññaphalāni – ime dhammā sekkhā. ^1-1414

## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-summaries.md`)

### Tikaatthuddhāro

[[#^toc-5-1|↑↑↑]]

Tikaatthuddhāravaṇṇanāyaṃ catūsūti kāmāvacararūpāvacarārūpāvacaraapariyāpannāsu. Kusalanti phassādibhedaṃ kusalaṃ – ime dhammā kusalā nāma. Akusalānaṃ pana bhūmivasena bhedābhāvato dvādasa akusalacittuppādāti āha. Tattha 'rājā āgato'ti vutte amaccādīnampi āgamanaṃ vuttameva hoti – evaṃ 'cittuppādā'ti vutte tehi sampayuttadhammāpi vuttāva hontīti; cittuppādaggahaṇena sasampayuttadhammaṃ cittaṃ gahitanti veditabbaṃ. Ito paresu pana catūsu bhūmīsu vipākotiādīnaṃ sabbesampi tikadukabhājanīyapadānaṃ attho visesamattameva dassetvā veditabbo.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-practice.md`)

### Tikaatthuddhāro

[[#^toc-4-1|↑↑↑]]

Tikaatthuddhāre kusalādayo dhammā tikavasena uddhaṭā — catūsu bhūmīsu (kāmāvacararūpāvacaraarūpāvacaraapariyāpannāsu) kusalaṃ, bhūmibhedābhāvato dvādasa akusalacittuppādā, vipākakiriyarūpanibbānavasena abyākatañca. "Cittuppādā"ti vutte tehi sampayuttā cetasikāpi gahitāva honti, yathā "rājā āgato"ti vutte amaccādayopi āgatā honti. Tasmā yoginā ekekaṃ cittuppādaṃ "kusalo ayaṃ, akusalo ayaṃ, abyākato aya"nti sasampayuttadhammaṃ sallakkhetvā, dvādasa akusalacittuppāde pahānāya, catubhūmakaṃ kusalaṃ upasampadāya ussāhaṃ katvā, sukhadukkhādivedanāsampayoge ca satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā, cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

## English translation (from `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-Contemporary-English-Abhidhamma/en-dhammasangani-ai.md`)

Which phenomena are wholesome? Wholesome phenomena are the wholesome in the four planes. ^1-1384

Which phenomena are unwholesome? Unwholesome phenomena are the twelve unwholesome states of consciousness. ^1-1385

Which phenomena are indeterminate? Indeterminate phenomena are resultant in the four planes, functional indeterminate in the three planes, and matter, and Nibbāna. ^1-1386

Which phenomena are associated with pleasant feeling? These phenomena are the four wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses accompanied by joy; the four unwholesome (consciousnesses); the five resultant and functional from wholesome sense-sphere; the wholesome, resultant, and functional fine-material sphere 3rd and 4th jhānas; the wholesome and resultant supramundane 3rd and 4th jhānas; excluding the pleasant feeling that arises therein. ^1-1387

What phenomena are associated with painful feeling? The two consciousnesses accompanied by displeasure, and the body-consciousness accompanied by pain, excluding the painful feeling arising therein 

– these phenomena are associated with painful feeling. ^1-1388

What phenomena are associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? The four wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses accompanied by equanimity, the six unwholesome, the ten resultant wholesome sense-sphere, the six resultant unwholesome, the six functional, the fine-material sphere fourth jhána (wholesome, resultant, and functional), the four immaterial sphere (wholesome, resultant, and functional), and the supramundane fourth jhána (wholesome and resultant), excluding the neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling arising therein 

– these phenomena are associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. The three feelings, and matter, and Nibbána 

– these phenomena are not said to be associated with pleasant feeling, nor with painful feeling, nor with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. ^1-1389

What phenomena are resultants? Resultant phenomena in the four planes 

– these phenomena are resultants. ^1-1390

What phenomena are of the nature to produce resultants? Wholesome and unwholesome phenomena in the four planes 

– these phenomena are of the nature to produce resultants. ^1-1391

What phenomena are neither resultant nor of the nature to produce resultants? Functional indeterminate phenomena in the three planes, and matter, and Nibbána 

– these phenomena are neither resultant nor of the nature to produce resultants. ^1-1392

What phenomena are clung to and graspable? Resultant phenomena in the three planes, and that matter which is produced by kamma 

– these phenomena are clung to and graspable. ^1-1393

What phenomena are not clung to but graspable? Wholesome phenomena in the three planes, unwholesome phenomena in the three planes, functional indeterminate phenomena in the three planes, and that matter which is not produced by kamma 

– these phenomena are not clung to but graspable. ^1-1394

What phenomena are not clung to and not graspable? The four supramundane paths, the four fruitions of recluseship, and Nibbána 

– these phenomena are not clung to and not graspable. ^1-1395

What phenomena are defiled and defiling? The twelve unwholesome consciousnesses 

– these phenomena are defiled and defiling. ^1-1396

What phenomena are undefiled but defiling? Wholesome phenomena in the three planes, resultant phenomena in the three planes, functional indeterminate phenomena in the three planes, and all matter 

– these phenomena are undefiled but defiling. ^1-1397

What phenomena are undefiled and non-defiling? The four supramundane paths, the four fruits of recluseship, and Nibbána – these phenomena are undefiled and non-defiling. ^1-1398

What phenomena are accompanied by initial application and sustained application? Wholesome and unwholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses; eleven resultant wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses; two resultant unwholesome consciousnesses; eleven functional consciousnesses; the first fine-material jhána, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the first supramundane jhána, wholesome and resultant – excluding the initial application and sustained application arising therein – these phenomena are accompanied by initial application and sustained application. ^1-1399

What phenomena are without initial application but with sustained application only? The second fine-material jhána in the five-fold system, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the second supramundane jhána in the five-fold system, wholesome and resultant – excluding the sustained application arising therein – and initial application – these phenomena are without initial application but with sustained application only. ^1-1400

What phenomena are without initial application and without sustained application? The two sets of fivefold sense consciousnesses; the fine-material jhána triads, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the four immaterial jhána consciousnesses, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the supramundane jhána triads, wholesome and resultant, and the second jhána in the five-fold system; and sustained application arising therein; and matter and Nibbána – these phenomena are without initial application and without sustained application. Sustained application co-arising with initial application should not be said to be accompanied by initial application and sustained application, nor only by sustained application, nor without initial application and without sustained application. ^1-1401

What phenomena are accompanied by rapture? Four wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses accompanied by joy; four unwholesome consciousnesses; five resultant wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses; five functional consciousnesses; the fine-material jhána dyads and triads, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the supramundane jhána dyads and triads, wholesome and resultant – excluding the rapture arising therein – these phenomena are accompanied by rapture. ^1-1402

What phenomena are accompanied by happiness? Four wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses accompanied by joy; four unwholesome consciousnesses; six resultant wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses; five functional consciousnesses; the fine-material jhána triads and tetrads, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the supramundane jhána triads and tetrads, wholesome and resultant – excluding the happiness arising therein – these phenomena are accompanied by happiness. ^1-1403

What phenomena are accompanied by equanimity? Four wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses accompanied by equanimity; six unwholesome consciousnesses; ten resultant wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses; six resultant unwholesome consciousnesses; six functional consciousnesses; the fourth fine-material jhána, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the four immaterial jhána consciousnesses, wholesome, resultant, and functional; the fourth supramundane jhána, wholesome and resultant – excluding the equanimity arising therein – these phenomena are accompanied by equanimity. Rapture is not accompanied by rapture, is accompanied by happiness, is not accompanied by equanimity. Happiness is not accompanied by happiness, may be accompanied by rapture, is not accompanied by equanimity, and may not be said to be accompanied by rapture. The two consciousnesses accompanied by displeasure, body-consciousness accompanied by pain, the feeling of equanimity, and matter and Nibbána – these phenomena should not be said to be accompanied by rapture, nor by happiness, nor by equanimity. ^1-1404

What phenomena are to be abandoned by insight? The four consciousnesses associated with wrong view, and the consciousness accompanied by doubt – these phenomena are to be abandoned by insight. ^1-1405

What phenomena are to be abandoned by development? The consciousness accompanied by restlessness – these phenomena are to be abandoned by development. The four greed-rooted consciousnesses dissociated from wrong view, and the two consciousnesses accompanied by displeasure – these phenomena may be abandoned by insight or by development. ^1-1406

What phenomena are to be abandoned neither by insight nor by development? Wholesome consciousnesses in the four planes; resultant consciousnesses in the four planes; functional indeterminate consciousnesses in the three planes; and matter and Nibbána – these phenomena are to be abandoned neither by insight nor by development. ^1-1407

Which states have roots to be abandoned by seeing? The four consciousnesses associated with wrong view, the consciousness accompanied by doubt, excluding the delusion arising therein – these states have roots to be abandoned by seeing. ^1-1408

Which states have roots to be abandoned by development? The consciousness accompanied by restlessness, excluding the delusion arising therein – these states have roots to be abandoned by development. The four consciousnesses accompanied by greed, dissociated from wrong view, and the two consciousnesses accompanied by displeasure – these states may have roots to be abandoned by seeing, or may have roots to be abandoned by development. ^1-1409

Which states have roots to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by development? Delusion accompanied by doubt, delusion accompanied by restlessness, wholesome states in the four planes, resultant states in the four planes, functional indeterminate states in the three planes, and matter, and Nibbāna – these states have roots to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by development. ^1-1410

Which states lead to accumulation? Wholesome states in the three planes, unwholesome states – these states lead to accumulation. ^1-1411

Which states lead to reduction? The four supramundane paths – these states lead to reduction. ^1-1412

Which states lead neither to accumulation nor to reduction? Resultant states in the four planes, functional indeterminate states in the three planes, and matter, and Nibbāna – these states lead neither to accumulation nor to reduction. ^1-1413

Which states are learners? The four supramundane paths, and the three lower fruits of recluseship – these states are learners. ^1-1414
