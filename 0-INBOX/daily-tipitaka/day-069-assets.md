## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-summaries.md`)

### Suttantikadukanikkhepaṃ

[[#^toc-4-3|↑↑↑]]

Suttantikadukanikkhepakathāyaṃ mātikākathāyaṃ atthato vivecitattā yāni nesaṃ niddesapadāni tesampi heṭṭhā vuttanayeneva suviññeyyattā yebhuyyena uttānatthāneva. Tattha vijjūpamaduke cakkhumā puriso meghandhakāre rattiṃ maggaṃ paṭipajji; vijjuyā niccharitvā andhakāraviddhaṃsanakālo viya sotāpattimaggobhāsena uppajjitvā saccacchādakatamassa vinoditakālo; dutiya- tatiyagamanābhinīhāro viya sakadāgāmi- anāgāmimaggattāya vipassanārambho. Vajirassa pana pāsāṇo vā maṇi vā abhejjo nāma natthi – evameva arahattamaggassa avajjhakileso nāma natthi, sabbakilese vinivijjhati vajiraṃ viyāti adhivacanadukādinaṃ niddesapadāni nānābhedehi vibhattāni.

#### Suttantikadukanikkhepaṃ

[[#^toc-3-3|↑↑↑]]

Suttantikadukesu vijjābhāgi-avijjābhāgi-bāla-paṇḍita-kaṇha-sukka-tapanīya-atapanīyādayo dvācattālīsa dukā nikkhittā. Tattha ahirikānottappāni bālabhāvassa mūlāni, kāyaduccaritādīni ca katattā pacchā tapanīyāni; hiri-ottappā pana paṇḍitakarā, kāyasucaritādīni ca akatattā atapanīyāni. Tasmā yoginā ahirikānottappe pahāya hiri-ottappe patiṭṭhāya, kaṇhe tapanīye ca dhamme vajjetvā sukke atapanīye ca dhamme sampādetabbā. Vijjābhāgiyā ca vipassanā bhāvetabbā — yathā meghandhakāre vijju niccharitvā maggaṃ pakāseti, evameva sotāpatti-sakadāgāmi-anāgāmimaggobhāsā saccacchādakatamaṃ vinodenti; yathā ca vajiraṃ sabbaṃ vinivijjhati, evaṃ arahattamaggo asesato sabbakilese samucchindati, tena ca pahīnā kilesā puna na paccudāvattanti. Iti anukkamena vaḍḍhentena cittaṃ accantaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

## English translation (from `en-dhammasangani-ai.md`)

Here, some person, without reflection, unskillfully takes food for sport, for intoxication, for adornment, for beautification. That dissatisfaction, immoderation, lack of reflection in food—this is called immoderation in food. ^1-1353
What is guardedness of the sense doors?
Here, perceiving forms with the eye, sounds with the ear, odors with the nose, flavors with the tongue, tangible objects with the body, or mental objects with the mind, one does not grasp at their general appearance nor details. Whereby, while the respective faculty dwells unguarded, evil unwholesome states of covetousness and displeasure might flow in; one practices for its restraint, protects that faculty, undertakes restraint in that faculty. The guarding, protection, restraint of these six faculties—this is called guardedness of the sense doors. ^1-1354
What is moderation in food?
Here, some person, with reflection, skillfully takes food—not for sport, not for intoxication, not for adornment, not for beautification, but solely for the maintenance and continuance of this body, for the cessation of discomfort, for the support of the holy life. "Thus I will end old feelings and not produce new feelings, and there will be sustenance for me, blamelessness, and comfortable living." That satisfaction, moderation, reflection in food—this is called moderation in food. ^1-1355
What is forgetfulness?
That which is absence of mindfulness, non-recollection, non-remembrance, inability to hold in mind, non-holding, letting slip, forgetting—this is called forgetfulness. ^1-1356
What is lack of clear comprehension?
That which is ignorance, non-seeing, delusion, the root of unwholesome states—this is called lack of clear comprehension. ^1-1357
What is mindfulness?
That which is mindfulness, recollection, remembrance, calling to mind, holding in mind, not letting slip, not forgetting; mindfulness, the faculty of mindfulness, the power of mindfulness, right mindfulness—this is called mindfulness. ^1-1358
What is clear comprehension?
That which is wisdom, understanding, non-delusion, investigation of phenomena, right view—this is called clear comprehension. ^1-1359
What is the power of reflection?
That which is wisdom, understanding, non-delusion, investigation of phenomena, right view—this is called the power of reflection. ^1-1360
What is the power of development? The repetition, development, and cultivation of wholesome states. This is called the power of development. The seven factors of enlightenment are also the power of development. ^1-1361
What is tranquility? The stability of mind...pe...right concentration. This is called tranquility. ^1-1362
What is insight? Wisdom, understanding...pe...non-delusion, investigation of phenomena, right view. This is called insight. ^1-1363
What is the sign of tranquility? The stability of mind...pe...right concentration. This is called the sign of tranquility. ^1-1364
What is the sign of exertion? Mental effort...pe...right effort. This is called the sign of exertion. ^1-1365
What is exertion? Mental effort...pe...right effort. This is called exertion. ^1-1366
What is non-distraction? The stability of mind...pe...right concentration. This is called non-distraction. ^1-1367
What is moral transgression? Bodily transgression, verbal transgression, or both bodily and verbal transgression. This is called moral transgression. All immorality is moral transgression. ^1-1368
What is wrong view?
There is no giving, no sacrifice, no offering; there is no fruit or result of good or evil deeds; there is no this world, nor the next; there is no mother, nor father; there are no spontaneously arisen beings; there are no recluses or brahmins in the world who have gone rightly, practiced rightly, who, having directly known and realized this world and the next by themselves, proclaim it. Such a view, wrong view...pe...perverted grasp. This is called wrong view. All wrong views are wrong view. ^1-1369
What is moral accomplishment? Bodily non-transgression, verbal non-transgression, or both bodily and verbal non-transgression. This is called moral accomplishment. All restraint in morality is moral accomplishment. ^1-1370
What is accomplishment in view? There is what is given, offered, sacrificed; there is fruit and result of good and bad actions; there is this world, there is the other world; there is mother, there is father; there are spontaneously arisen beings; there are in the world ascetics and brahmins who are rightly gone and rightly practiced, who proclaim this world and the other world, having directly known and realized them themselves. Such wisdom, knowing... non-delusion, investigation of phenomena, right view 
– this is called accomplishment in view. All right view is accomplishment in view. ^1-1371
What is purity of virtue? Bodily non-transgression, verbal non-transgression, bodily and verbal non-transgression 
– this is called purity of virtue. All virtuous restraint is purity of virtue. ^1-1372
What is purity of view? The knowledge that beings are owners of their actions, knowledge conforming to the Noble Truths, the wisdom of one endowed with the path, the wisdom of one endowed with the fruit. ^1-1373
Purity of view is that wisdom, knowing... non-delusion, investigation of phenomena, right view. ^1-1374
Exertion for one with such a view is mental arousal of energy... right effort. ^1-1375
Spiritual urgency is the fear of birth, the fear of old age, the fear of sickness, the fear of death. Grounds for spiritual urgency are birth, old age, sickness, death. ^1-1376
Proper exertion for one who has a sense of urgency: Here, a practitioner arouses desire, strives, exerts energy, grasps the mind, and strives for the non-arising of unarisen evil, unwholesome states; for the abandoning of arisen evil, unwholesome states; for the arising of unarisen wholesome states; for the maintenance, non-forgetting, increase, development, cultivation, and perfection of arisen wholesome states. ^1-1377
Non-contentment with wholesome states is the desire for more for one not content with the cultivation of wholesome states. ^1-1378
Non-recoiling from exertion is diligent performance, continuous performance, unwavering performance, unflagging conduct, unabandoned desire, unabandoned burden in the repetition, development, and cultivation of wholesome states. ^1-1379
