##### Pañcakaniddeso ^2-16-11-5-0

(1) Tattha katamo pañcaṅgiko sammāsamādhi? Pītipharaṇatā, sukhapharaṇatā, cetopharaṇatā, ālokapharaṇatā, paccavekkhaṇānimittaṃ. Dvīsu jhānesu paññā pītipharaṇatā. Tīsu jhānesu paññā sukhapharaṇatā. Paracitte ñāṇaṃ cetopharaṇatā. Dibbacakkhu ālokapharaṇatā. Tamhā tamhā samādhimhā vuṭṭhitassa paccavekkhaṇāñāṇaṃ paccavekkhaṇānimittaṃ. Ayaṃ vuccati pañcaṅgiko sammāsamādhi.
(2) Tattha katamo pañcañāṇiko sammāsamādhi? "Ayaṃ samādhi paccuppannasukho ceva āyatiñca sukhavipāko"ti paccattaññeva ñāṇaṃ uppajjati. "Ayaṃ samādhi ariyo nirāmiso"ti paccattaññeva ñāṇaṃ uppajjati. "Ayaṃ samādhi akāpurisasevito"ti paccattaññeva ñāṇaṃ uppajjati. "Ayaṃ samādhi santo paṇīto paṭippassaddhaladdho ekodibhāvādhigato na sasaṅkhāraniggayhavāritagato"ti paccattaññeva ñāṇaṃ uppajjati. So kho panāhaṃ imaṃ samādhiṃ sato samāpajjāmi sato vuṭṭhahāmī"ti paccattaññeva ñāṇaṃ uppajjati. Ayaṃ pañcañāṇiko sammāsamādhi. Evaṃ pañcavidhena ñāṇavatthu.
Pañcakaṃ. ^2-804

### Dasakaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-10|↑↑↑]]

Dasavidhena ñāṇavatthuniddese aṭṭhānanti hetupaṭikkhepo, anavakāsoti paccayapaṭikkhepo; kāraṇañhi tadāyattavuttitāya attano phalassa ṭhānanti ca avakāsoti ca vuccati. Yaṃ yaṃ hi puthujjano niccaṃ sukhaṃ attāti gaṇhāti, taṃ taṃ ariyasāvako aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ anattāti gaṇhanto gāhaṃ viniveṭheti; mātughātādīni pañca ānantariyakammāni puthujjano karoti, na ariyasāvako; ekissā lokadhātuyā dve sammāsambuddhā apubbaṃ acarimaṃ nuppajjanti anacchariyattā desanāya visesābhāvato vivādabhāvato ca; yathā nimbabījādīni amadhurameva phalaṃ nibbattenti, evaṃ kāyaduccaritādīni amadhurameva vipākaṃ nibbattenti. Dutiyabalaniddese gatiupadhikālapayogasampattīhi paṭibāhitaṃ pāpakammaṃ vipākaṃ adatvā catasso vipattiyo āgamma deti, catūhi vipattīhi paṭibāhitaṃ kalyāṇakammaṃ catasso sampattiyo āgamma deti; idaṃ tathāgatassa kammantaravipākantarajānanañāṇaṃ akampiyaṭṭhena dutiyabalaṃ. Tatiyabalaniddese maggoti vā paṭipadāti vā kammassevetaṃ nāmaṃ; sakalagāmavāsikesu ekato ekaṃ migaṃ jīvitā voropentesupi taṃ kammaṃ āyūhanakkhaṇeyeva nānā hoti, taṃ tathāgato ‘esa niraye nibbattissati, esa tiracchānayoniyaṃ, esa pettivisaye’ti nirayagāminiṃ…pe… nibbānagāminiṃ paṭipadaṃ pajānāti. Catutthabalaniddese khandhāyatanadhātunānattaṃ, anekadhātunānādhātulokanānattañca na kevalaṃ upādinnakasaṅkhāralokassa, anupādinnakasaṅkhāralokassāpi pajānāti; sabbaññubuddhānaṃyeva hi etaṃ balaṃ, na aññesaṃ. Pañcamabalaniddese hīnādhimuttikā hīnādhimuttike, paṇītādhimuttikā paṇītādhimuttike sevanti bhajanti payirupāsanti; taṃ ajjhāsayadhātu niyāmeti. Chaṭṭhabalaniddese sattānaṃ āsayaṃ anusayaṃ caritaṃ adhimuttaṃ, apparajakkhamahārajakkhatikkhindriyamudindriyasvākāradvākārasuviññāpayaduviññāpayabhabbābhabbabhāvañca pajānāti; indriyaparopariyattiñāṇañca āsayānusayañāṇañca ekato hutvā ekaṃ balañāṇaṃ nāma jātaṃ. Sattamabalaniddese cattāro jhāyī, cattāri jhānāni, vimokkhā, savitakkasavicārādayo samādhī, samāpattiyo, hānabhāgiyo visesabhāgiyo ca dhammo, vodānampi vuṭṭhānaṃ, tamhā tamhā samādhimhā vuṭṭhānampi vuṭṭhānanti vibhattaṃ. Aṭṭhamabalaniddese anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ, navamabalaniddese dibbena cakkhunāti sabbaṃ visuddhimagge vitthāritameva; dasamabalaniddese cetovimuttinti phalasamādhiṃ, paññāvimuttinti phalañāṇaṃ. Paravādī pana "dasabalañāṇaṃ nāma pāṭiyekkaṃ natthi, sabbaññutañāṇassevāyaṃ pabhedo"ti āha; taṃ na tathā daṭṭhabbaṃ. Dasabalañāṇañhi sakasakakiccameva jānāti – paṭhamaṃ kāraṇākāraṇameva, dutiyaṃ kammantaravipākantarameva, tatiyaṃ kammaparicchedameva, catutthaṃ dhātunānattakaraṇameva, pañcamaṃ sattānaṃ ajjhāsayādhimuttimeva, chaṭṭhaṃ indriyānaṃ tikkhamudubhāvameva, sattamaṃ jhānādīhi saddhiṃ tesaṃ saṃkilesādimeva, aṭṭhamaṃ pubbenivutthakhandhasantatimeva, navamaṃ sattānaṃ cutipaṭisandhimeva, dasamaṃ saccaparicchedameva; sabbaññutañāṇaṃ pana etehi jānitabbañca tato uttaritarañca pajānāti, etesaṃ pana kiccaṃ na sabbaṃ karoti – jhānaṃ hutvā appetuṃ, iddhi hutvā vikubbituṃ, maggo hutvā kilese khepetuṃ na sakkoti. Paṭipāṭiyā satta ñāṇāni kāmāvacarāni, tato dve rūpāvacarāni, avasāne ekaṃ lokuttaraṃ; sabbaññutañāṇaṃ pana savitakkasavicārameva kāmāvacarameva lokiyameva. Iti aññadeva dasabalañāṇaṃ, aññaṃ sabbaññutañāṇaṃ.

(b–d) Concerning the origin of aging and death… concerning the cessation of aging and death… concerning the way leading to the cessation of aging and death, the wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view that arises: this is called "knowledge of the way leading to the cessation of aging and death". ^2-802

##### The Exposition of the Fives ^2-16-11-5-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-804]]

(1) Therein, what is five-factored right concentration? The pervasion with joy, the pervasion with happiness, the pervasion with mind, the pervasion with light, and the sign for reviewing. Wisdom in two meditative absorptions is the pervasion with joy. Wisdom in three meditative absorptions is the pervasion with happiness. Knowledge of others' minds is the pervasion with mind. The divine eye is the pervasion with light. The reviewing knowledge of one who has emerged from this or that concentration is the sign for reviewing. This is called five-factored right concentration.
(2) Therein, what is five-knowledged right concentration? The knowledge arises in oneself: "This concentration is both pleasant now and pleasant in its result in the future." The knowledge arises in oneself: "This concentration is noble and unworldly." The knowledge arises in oneself: "This concentration is not practised by base people." The knowledge arises in oneself: "This concentration is peaceful, refined, obtained by tranquillisation, attained through unification, and not maintained by forceful suppression with effort." The knowledge arises in oneself: "Mindful I enter this concentration, and mindful I emerge from it." This is five-knowledged right concentration. Thus is the ground of knowledge by way of five.
The Fives. ^2-804

##### The Exposition of the Sevens ^2-16-11-7-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-806]]

Therein, what are the seventy-seven grounds of knowledge? Knowledge that with birth as condition there is aging and death; knowledge that when there is no birth there is no aging and death; knowledge that in the past too, with birth as condition, there was aging and death; knowledge that when there was no birth there was no aging and death; knowledge that in the future too, with birth as condition, there will be aging and death; knowledge that when there is no birth there will be no aging and death. And the knowledge that this knowledge of the fixity of things is itself subject to destruction, subject to vanishing, subject to fading away, subject to cessation. Knowledge that with existence as condition there is birth… knowledge that with clinging as condition there is existence… knowledge that with craving as condition there is clinging… knowledge that with feeling as condition there is craving… knowledge that with contact as condition there is feeling… knowledge that with the six sense-fields as condition there is contact… knowledge that with mentality and material form as condition there are the six sense-fields… knowledge that with consciousness as condition there is mentality and material form… knowledge that with formations as condition there is consciousness… knowledge that with ignorance as condition there are formations; knowledge that when there is no ignorance there are no formations; knowledge that in the past too, with ignorance as condition, there were formations; knowledge that when there was no ignorance there were no formations; knowledge that in the future too, with ignorance as condition, there will be formations; knowledge that when there is no ignorance there will be no formations. And the knowledge that this knowledge of the fixity of things is itself subject to destruction, subject to vanishing, subject to fading away, subject to cessation. These are the seventy-seven grounds of knowledge. Thus is the ground of knowledge by way of seven.
The Sevens. ^2-806

##### The Exposition of the Tens ^2-16-11-10-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-809]]

(1) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the possible as possible and the impossible as impossible? Here the Tathāgata knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any formation as permanent — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some formation as permanent — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any formation as pleasant — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some formation as pleasant — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any thing as self — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some thing as self — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should deprive his mother of life — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should deprive his mother of life — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should deprive his father of life… should deprive an arahant of life… should with a mind of hatred shed the blood of a Tathāgata… should split the Order… should acknowledge another teacher… should produce an eighth existence — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should produce an eighth existence — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that two arahants who are fully awakened Buddhas should arise in one world system neither before nor after each other — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one arahant who is a fully awakened Buddha should arise in one world system — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that two wheel-turning kings should arise in one world system neither before nor after each other — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one wheel-turning king should arise in one world system — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a woman should be an arahant who is a fully awakened Buddha — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a man should be an arahant who is a fully awakened Buddha — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a woman should be a wheel-turning king — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a man should be a wheel-turning king — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a woman should exercise the rulership of Sakka, the rulership of Māra, the rulership of Brahmā — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a man should exercise the rulership of Sakka, the rulership of Māra, the rulership of Brahmā — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from bodily misconduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from bodily misconduct — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from verbal misconduct… from mental misconduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from verbal misconduct… from mental misconduct — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from good bodily conduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from good bodily conduct — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from good verbal conduct… from good mental conduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from good verbal conduct… from good mental conduct — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to bodily misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to bodily misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to verbal misconduct… one given to mental misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to verbal misconduct… one given to mental misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to good bodily conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to good bodily conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to good verbal conduct… one given to good mental conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to good verbal conduct… one given to good mental conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is possible." "Whatever things are causes and conditions of whatever things — that, accordingly, is the possible; whatever things are not causes and not conditions of whatever things — that, accordingly, is the impossible." The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the possible as possible and the impossible as impossible. ^2-809
