Katamo ca sattānaṃ anusayo? Sattānusayā – kāmarāgānusayo, paṭighānusayo, mānānusayo, diṭṭhānusayo, vicikicchānusayo, bhavarāgānusayo, avijjānusayo. Yaṃ loke piyarūpaṃ sātarūpaṃ ettha sattānaṃ rāgānusayo anuseti. Yaṃ loke appiyarūpaṃ asātarūpaṃ ettha sattānaṃ paṭighānusayo anuseti. Iti imesu dvīsu dhammesu avijjānupatitā. Tadekaṭṭho māno ca diṭṭhi ca vicikicchā ca daṭṭhabbā. Ayaṃ sattānaṃ anusayo. ^2-816

Katamañca sattānaṃ caritaṃ? Puññābhisaṅkhāro, apuññābhisaṅkhāro, āneñjābhisaṅkhāro, parittabhūmako vā mahābhūmako vā – idaṃ sattānaṃ caritaṃ. ^2-817

Katamā ca sattānaṃ adhimutti? Santi sattā hīnādhimuttikā, santi sattā paṇītādhimuttikā. Hīnādhimuttikā sattā hīnādhimuttike satte sevanti bhajanti payirupāsanti. Paṇītādhimuttikā sattā paṇītādhimuttike satte sevanti bhajanti payirupāsanti.
Atītampi addhānaṃ hīnādhimuttikā sattā hīnādhimuttike satte seviṃsu bhajiṃsu payirupāsiṃsu. Paṇītādhimuttikā sattā paṇītādhimuttike satte seviṃsu bhajiṃsu payirupāsiṃsu.
Anāgatampi addhānaṃ hīnādhimuttikā sattā hīnādhimuttike satte sevissanti bhajissanti payirupāsissanti. Paṇītādhimuttikā sattā paṇītādhimuttike satte sevissanti bhajissanti payirupāsissanti. Ayaṃ sattānaṃ adhimutti. ^2-818

Katame te sattā mahārajakkhā? Dasa kilesavatthūni – lobho, doso, moho, māno, diṭṭhi, vicikicchā, thinaṃ, uddhaccaṃ, ahirikaṃ, anottappaṃ. Yesaṃ sattānaṃ imāni dasa kilesavatthūni āsevitāni bhāvitāni bahulīkatāni ussadagatāni, ime te sattā mahārajakkhā. ^2-819

Katame te sattā apparajakkhā? Yesaṃ sattānaṃ imāni dasa kilesavatthūni anāsevitāni abhāvitāni abahulīkatāni anussadagatāni, ime te sattā apparajakkhā. ^2-820

Katame te sattā mudindriyā? Pañcindriyāni – saddhindriyaṃ, vīriyindriyaṃ, satindriyaṃ, samādhindriyaṃ, paññindriyaṃ. Yesaṃ sattānaṃ imāni pañcindriyāni anāsevitāni abhāvitāni abahulīkatāni anussadagatāni, ime te sattā mudindriyā. ^2-821

Katame te sattā tikkhindriyā? Yesaṃ sattānaṃ imāni pañcindriyāni āsevitāni bhāvitāni bahulīkatāni ussadagatāni, ime te sattā tikkhindriyā. ^2-822

Katame te sattā dvākārā? Ye te sattā pāpāsayā pāpānusayā pāpacaritā pāpādhimuttikā mahārajakkhā mudindriyā, ime te sattā dvākārā. ^2-823

Katame te sattā svākārā? Ye te sattā kalyāṇāsayā kalyāṇacaritā kalyāṇādhimuttikā apparajakkhā tikkhindriyā, ime te sattā svākārā. ^2-824

Katame te sattā duviññāpayā? Ye ca te sattā dvākārā, teva te sattā duviññāpayā. Ye ca te sattā svākārā, teva te sattā suviññāpayā. ^2-825

Katame te sattā abhabbā? Ye te sattā kammāvaraṇena samannāgatā kilesāvaraṇena samannāgatā vipākāvaraṇena samannāgatā assaddhā acchandikā duppaññā abhabbā niyāmaṃ okkamituṃ kusalesu dhammesu sammattaṃ, ime te sattā abhabbā. ^2-826

Katame te sattā bhabbā? Ye te sattā na kammāvaraṇena samannāgatā na kilesāvaraṇena samannāgatā na vipākāvaraṇena samannāgatā saddhā chandikā paññavanto bhabbā niyāmaṃ okkamituṃ kusalesu dhammesu sammattaṃ, ime te sattā bhabbāti. Yā tattha paññā pajānanā…pe… amoho dhammavicayo sammādiṭṭhi – idaṃ tathāgatassa parasattānaṃ parapuggalānaṃ indriyaparopariyattaṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ. ^2-827

(7) Tattha katamaṃ tathāgatassa jhānavimokkhasamādhisamāpattīnaṃ saṃkilesaṃ vodānaṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ? Jhāyīti. Cattāro jhāyī. Atthekacco jhāyī sampattiyeva [sampattiyeva (ka.)] samānaṃ vipattīti pacceti, atthekacco jhāyī vipattiṃyeva [vipattiyeva (ka.)] samānaṃ sampattīti pacceti, atthekacco jhāyī sampattiyeva samānaṃ sampattīti pacceti, atthekacco jhāyī vipattiyeva samānaṃ vipattīti pacceti – ime cattāro jhāyī.
Aparepi cattāro jhāyī. Atthekacco jhāyī dandhaṃ samāpajjati khippaṃ vuṭṭhāti, atthekacco jhāyī khippaṃ samāpajjati dandhaṃ vuṭṭhāti, atthekacco jhāyī dandhaṃ samāpajjati dandhaṃ vuṭṭhāti, atthekacco jhāyī khippaṃ samāpajjati khippaṃ vuṭṭhāti – ime cattāro jhāyī.
Aparepi cattāro jhāyī. Atthekacco jhāyī samādhismiṃ samādhikusalo hoti, na samādhismiṃ samāpattikusalo; atthekacco jhāyī samādhismiṃ samāpattikusalo hoti, na samādhismiṃ samādhikusalo; atthekacco jhāyī samādhismiṃ samādhikusalo ca hoti, samādhismiṃ samāpattikusalo ca; atthekacco jhāyī neva samādhismiṃ samādhikusalo hoti, na samādhismiṃ samāpattikusalo – ime cattāro jhāyī.
"Jhāna"nti. Cattāri jhānāni – paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ, dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ, tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ, catutthaṃ jhānaṃ.
"Vimokkho"ti. Aṭṭha vimokkhā. Rūpī rūpāni passati – ayaṃ paṭhamo vimokkho.
Ajjhattaṃ arūpasaññī bahiddhā rūpāni passati – ayaṃ dutiyo vimokkho.
Subhanteva adhimutto hoti – ayaṃ tatiyo vimokkho.
Sabbaso rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā "ananto ākāso"ti ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati – ayaṃ catuttho vimokkho.
Sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma "anantaṃ viññāṇa"nti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati – ayaṃ pañcamo vimokkho.
Sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma "natthi kiñci"ti ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati – ayaṃ chaṭṭho vimokkho.
Sabbaso ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samatikkamma nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati – ayaṃ sattamo vimokkho.
Sabbaso nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ samatikkamma saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ upasampajja viharati – ayaṃ aṭṭhamo vimokkho.
"Samādhī"ti. Tayo samādhī – savitakkasavicāro samādhi, avitakkavicāramatto samādhi, avitakkaavicāro samādhi.
"Samāpattī"ti. Nava anupubbavihārasamāpattiyo – paṭhamajjhānasamāpatti, dutiyajjhānasamāpatti, tatiyajjhānasamāpatti, catutthajjhānasamāpatti, ākāsānañcāyatanasamāpatti, viññāṇañcāyatanasamāpatti, ākiñcaññāyatanasamāpatti, nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasamāpatti, saññāvedayitanirodhasamāpatti.
"Saṃkilesa"nti hānabhāgiyo dhammo. "Vodāna"nti visesabhāgiyo dhammo. "Vuṭṭhāna"nti. Vodānampi vuṭṭhānaṃ, tamhā tamhā samādhimhā vuṭṭhānampi vuṭṭhānanti. Yā tattha paññā pajānanā…pe… amoho dhammavicayo sammādiṭṭhi – idaṃ tathāgatassa jhānavimokkhasamādhisamāpattīnaṃ saṃkilesaṃ vodānaṃ vuṭṭhānaṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ. ^2-828

(8) Tattha katamaṃ tathāgatassa pubbenivāsānussati yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ? Idha tathāgato anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati, seyyathidaṃ – ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo tissopi jātiyo catassopi jātiyo pañcapi jātiyo dasapi jātiyo vīsampi jātiyo tiṃsampi jātiyo cattālīsampi jātiyo paññāsampi jātiyo jātisatampi jātisahassampi jātisatasahassampi anekepi saṃvaṭṭakappe anekepi vivaṭṭakappe anekepi saṃvaṭṭavivaṭṭakappe "amutrāsiṃ evaṃnāmo evaṃgotto evaṃvaṇṇo evamāhāro evaṃsukhadukkhappaṭisaṃvedī evamāyupariyanto, so tato cuto amutra udapādiṃ; tatrāpāsiṃ evaṃnāmo evaṃgotto evaṃvaṇṇo evamāhāro evaṃsukhadukkhappaṭisaṃvedī evamāyupariyanto, so tato cuto idhūpapanno"ti. Iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussaratīti. Yā tattha paññā pajānanā…pe… amoho dhammavicayo sammādiṭṭhi – idaṃ tathāgatassa pubbenivāsānussati yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ. ^2-829

(9) Tattha katamaṃ tathāgatassa sattānaṃ cutūpapātaṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ? Idha tathāgato dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāti – "ime vata bhonto sattā kāyaduccaritena samannāgatā vacīduccaritena samannāgatā manoduccaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ upavādakā micchādiṭṭhikā micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā. Te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapannā. Ime vā pana bhonto sattā kāyasucaritena samannāgatā vacīsucaritena samannāgatā manosucaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ anupavādakā sammādiṭṭhikā sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā. Te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannā"ti. Iti dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānātīti. Yā tattha paññā pajānanā…pe… amoho dhammavicayo sammādiṭṭhi – idaṃ tathāgatassa sattānaṃ cutūpapātaṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ. ^2-830

(10) Tattha katamaṃ tathāgatassa āsavānaṃ khayaṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇaṃ? Idha tathāgato āsavānaṃ khayā anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ paññāvimuttiṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharatīti. Yā tattha paññā pajānanā…pe… amoho dhammavicayo sammādiṭṭhi – idaṃ tathāgatassa āsavānaṃ khayaṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇanti.
Dasakaṃ.
Ñāṇavibhaṅgo niṭṭhito. ^2-831

## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Dasakaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-10|↑↑↑]]

Dasavidhena ñāṇavatthuniddese aṭṭhānanti hetupaṭikkhepo, anavakāsoti paccayapaṭikkhepo; kāraṇañhi tadāyattavuttitāya attano phalassa ṭhānanti ca avakāsoti ca vuccati. Yaṃ yaṃ hi puthujjano niccaṃ sukhaṃ attāti gaṇhāti, taṃ taṃ ariyasāvako aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ anattāti gaṇhanto gāhaṃ viniveṭheti; mātughātādīni pañca ānantariyakammāni puthujjano karoti, na ariyasāvako; ekissā lokadhātuyā dve sammāsambuddhā apubbaṃ acarimaṃ nuppajjanti anacchariyattā desanāya visesābhāvato vivādabhāvato ca; yathā nimbabījādīni amadhurameva phalaṃ nibbattenti, evaṃ kāyaduccaritādīni amadhurameva vipākaṃ nibbattenti. Dutiyabalaniddese gatiupadhikālapayogasampattīhi paṭibāhitaṃ pāpakammaṃ vipākaṃ adatvā catasso vipattiyo āgamma deti, catūhi vipattīhi paṭibāhitaṃ kalyāṇakammaṃ catasso sampattiyo āgamma deti; idaṃ tathāgatassa kammantaravipākantarajānanañāṇaṃ akampiyaṭṭhena dutiyabalaṃ. Tatiyabalaniddese maggoti vā paṭipadāti vā kammassevetaṃ nāmaṃ; sakalagāmavāsikesu ekato ekaṃ migaṃ jīvitā voropentesupi taṃ kammaṃ āyūhanakkhaṇeyeva nānā hoti, taṃ tathāgato ‘esa niraye nibbattissati, esa tiracchānayoniyaṃ, esa pettivisaye’ti nirayagāminiṃ…pe… nibbānagāminiṃ paṭipadaṃ pajānāti. Catutthabalaniddese khandhāyatanadhātunānattaṃ, anekadhātunānādhātulokanānattañca na kevalaṃ upādinnakasaṅkhāralokassa, anupādinnakasaṅkhāralokassāpi pajānāti; sabbaññubuddhānaṃyeva hi etaṃ balaṃ, na aññesaṃ. Pañcamabalaniddese hīnādhimuttikā hīnādhimuttike, paṇītādhimuttikā paṇītādhimuttike sevanti bhajanti payirupāsanti; taṃ ajjhāsayadhātu niyāmeti. Chaṭṭhabalaniddese sattānaṃ āsayaṃ anusayaṃ caritaṃ adhimuttaṃ, apparajakkhamahārajakkhatikkhindriyamudindriyasvākāradvākārasuviññāpayaduviññāpayabhabbābhabbabhāvañca pajānāti; indriyaparopariyattiñāṇañca āsayānusayañāṇañca ekato hutvā ekaṃ balañāṇaṃ nāma jātaṃ. Sattamabalaniddese cattāro jhāyī, cattāri jhānāni, vimokkhā, savitakkasavicārādayo samādhī, samāpattiyo, hānabhāgiyo visesabhāgiyo ca dhammo, vodānampi vuṭṭhānaṃ, tamhā tamhā samādhimhā vuṭṭhānampi vuṭṭhānanti vibhattaṃ. Aṭṭhamabalaniddese anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ, navamabalaniddese dibbena cakkhunāti sabbaṃ visuddhimagge vitthāritameva; dasamabalaniddese cetovimuttinti phalasamādhiṃ, paññāvimuttinti phalañāṇaṃ. Paravādī pana "dasabalañāṇaṃ nāma pāṭiyekkaṃ natthi, sabbaññutañāṇassevāyaṃ pabhedo"ti āha; taṃ na tathā daṭṭhabbaṃ. Dasabalañāṇañhi sakasakakiccameva jānāti – paṭhamaṃ kāraṇākāraṇameva, dutiyaṃ kammantaravipākantarameva, tatiyaṃ kammaparicchedameva, catutthaṃ dhātunānattakaraṇameva, pañcamaṃ sattānaṃ ajjhāsayādhimuttimeva, chaṭṭhaṃ indriyānaṃ tikkhamudubhāvameva, sattamaṃ jhānādīhi saddhiṃ tesaṃ saṃkilesādimeva, aṭṭhamaṃ pubbenivutthakhandhasantatimeva, navamaṃ sattānaṃ cutipaṭisandhimeva, dasamaṃ saccaparicchedameva; sabbaññutañāṇaṃ pana etehi jānitabbañca tato uttaritarañca pajānāti, etesaṃ pana kiccaṃ na sabbaṃ karoti – jhānaṃ hutvā appetuṃ, iddhi hutvā vikubbituṃ, maggo hutvā kilese khepetuṃ na sakkoti. Paṭipāṭiyā satta ñāṇāni kāmāvacarāni, tato dve rūpāvacarāni, avasāne ekaṃ lokuttaraṃ; sabbaññutañāṇaṃ pana savitakkasavicārameva kāmāvacarameva lokiyameva. Iti aññadeva dasabalañāṇaṃ, aññaṃ sabbaññutañāṇaṃ.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-practice.md`)

### Dasakaniddeso (Ñāṇa)

[[#^toc-16-10|↑↑↑]]

Dasakaniddese yathā nimbabījakosātakībījādīni amadhurameva phalaṃ nibbattenti, evaṃ kāyaduccaritādīni amadhurameva vipākaṃ nibbattenti, kāyasucaritādīni madhurameva vuttāni. Catūhi sampattīhi paṭibāhitampi pāpakammaṃ payogavipattiṃ āgamma vipaccati, catūhi vipattīhi paṭibāhitampi kalyāṇakammaṃ payogasampattiṃ āgamma vipaccati; yo pana bahumpi kusalākusalaṃ kammaṃ katvā kalyāṇamittaṃ nissāya arahattaṃ pāpuṇāti, etassa kammavipāko ‘nāhosi’ nāma. Sīlavantānaṃ sīlavantasevanameva, paññavantānaṃ paññavantasevanameva hoti, taṃ ajjhāsayadhātu niyāmeti; heṭṭhimaṃ heṭṭhimañhi paguṇajjhānaṃ uparimassa uparimassa padaṭṭhānaṃ hotīti vodānampi vuṭṭhānaṃ. Tasmā yoginā "idaṃ niccaṃ, idaṃ sukhaṃ, eso me attā"ti gāhaṃ pahāya, pāṇātipātādīni dasa akusalakammāni akaronto, sammāpayoge patiṭṭhāya tīṇi sucaritāni pūretvā kusalaṃ upasampādetvā, paṇītādhimuttike kalyāṇamitte sevanto bhajanto payirupāsanto, tathāgatassa dasabalañāṇe cittaṃ pasādento, sabbasaṅkhāre aniccato dukkhato anattato passanto cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

And what are the underlying tendencies of beings? There are seven underlying tendencies: the underlying tendency to sensual lust, the underlying tendency to aversion, the underlying tendency to conceit, the underlying tendency to views, the underlying tendency to doubt, the underlying tendency to lust for existence, the underlying tendency to ignorance. Whatever in the world is a dear and agreeable form — there the underlying tendency of beings to lust lies latent. Whatever in the world is a disagreeable and unpleasant form — there the underlying tendency of beings to aversion lies latent. So ignorance lies fallen upon these two things; and conceit, views, and doubt, standing together with it, should be seen there too. This is the underlying tendency of beings. ^2-816

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-817]]

And what is the conduct of beings? The meritorious formation, the demeritorious formation, the imperturbable formation, whether on a limited plane or on a great plane — this is the conduct of beings. ^2-817

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-818]]

And what is the inclination of beings? There are beings of inferior inclination; there are beings of refined inclination. Beings of inferior inclination resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination. Beings of refined inclination resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of refined inclination.
In the past too, beings of inferior inclination resorted to, frequented, and attended upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination resorted to, frequented, and attended upon beings of refined inclination.
In the future too, beings of inferior inclination will resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination will resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of refined inclination. This is the inclination of beings. ^2-818

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-819]]

Which beings have much dust in their eyes? The ten grounds of defilement: greed, hate, delusion, conceit, views, doubt, dullness, restlessness, shamelessness, lack of moral dread. Those beings by whom these ten grounds of defilement have been practised, developed, cultivated, and heaped up — these are the beings with much dust in their eyes. ^2-819

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-820]]

Which beings have little dust in their eyes? Those beings by whom these ten grounds of defilement have not been practised, not developed, not cultivated, and not heaped up — these are the beings with little dust in their eyes. ^2-820

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-821]]

Which beings have dull faculties? The five faculties: the faculty of confidence, the faculty of energy, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, the faculty of wisdom. Those beings by whom these five faculties have not been practised, not developed, not cultivated, and not heaped up — these are the beings with dull faculties. ^2-821

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-822]]

Which beings have sharp faculties? Those beings by whom these five faculties have been practised, developed, cultivated, and heaped up — these are the beings with sharp faculties. ^2-822

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-823]]

Which beings are of bad character? Those beings who have evil dispositions, evil underlying tendencies, evil conduct, and evil inclinations, with much dust in their eyes and dull faculties — these are the beings of bad character. ^2-823

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-824]]

Which beings are of good character? Those beings who have good dispositions, good conduct, and good inclinations, with little dust in their eyes and sharp faculties — these are the beings of good character. ^2-824

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-825]]

Which beings are hard to instruct? Those beings who are of bad character are the very beings hard to instruct. And those beings who are of good character are the very beings easy to instruct. ^2-825

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-826]]

Which beings are incapable? Those beings who possess the obstruction of action, possess the obstruction of defilement, possess the obstruction of result, who are without confidence, without desire, and without wisdom, and are incapable of entering the fixed course of rightness in wholesome things — these are the incapable beings. ^2-826

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-827]]

Which beings are capable? Those beings who do not possess the obstruction of action, do not possess the obstruction of defilement, do not possess the obstruction of result, who have confidence, desire, and wisdom, and are capable of entering the fixed course of rightness in wholesome things — these are the capable beings. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the higher and lower state of the faculties of other beings, of other persons. ^2-827

(7) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the defilement, the cleansing, and the emergence in the meditative absorptions, liberations, concentrations, and attainments? "A meditator": there are four meditators. There is one meditator who takes what is truly success to be failure; there is one meditator who takes what is truly failure to be success; there is one meditator who takes what is truly success to be success; there is one meditator who takes what is truly failure to be failure — these are the four meditators.
There are another four meditators. There is one meditator who enters slowly and emerges quickly; there is one meditator who enters quickly and emerges slowly; there is one meditator who enters slowly and emerges slowly; there is one meditator who enters quickly and emerges quickly — these are the four meditators.
There are another four meditators. There is one meditator who is skilled in the concentration within concentration but not skilled in the attainment within concentration; there is one meditator who is skilled in the attainment within concentration but not skilled in the concentration within concentration; there is one meditator who is skilled both in the concentration within concentration and in the attainment within concentration; there is one meditator who is skilled neither in the concentration within concentration nor in the attainment within concentration — these are the four meditators.
"Meditative absorption": there are four meditative absorptions — the first meditative absorption, the second meditative absorption, the third meditative absorption, the fourth meditative absorption.
"Liberation": there are eight liberations. Possessing material form, one sees material forms — this is the first liberation.
Not perceiving material form internally, one sees material forms externally — this is the second liberation.
One is resolved only upon the beautiful — this is the third liberation.
Completely passing beyond perceptions of material form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, not attending to perceptions of diversity, aware that "space is infinite", one enters and dwells in the field of infinite space — this is the fourth liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of infinite space, aware that "consciousness is infinite", one enters and dwells in the field of infinite consciousness — this is the fifth liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of infinite consciousness, aware that "there is nothing", one enters and dwells in the field of nothingness — this is the sixth liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, one enters and dwells in the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception — this is the seventh liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, one enters and dwells in the cessation of perception and feeling — this is the eighth liberation.
"Concentration": there are three concentrations — concentration with thought and with examination, concentration without thought but with examination only, concentration without thought and without examination.
"Attainment": there are nine successive dwelling attainments — the attainment of the first meditative absorption, the attainment of the second meditative absorption, the attainment of the third meditative absorption, the attainment of the fourth meditative absorption, the attainment of the field of infinite space, the attainment of the field of infinite consciousness, the attainment of the field of nothingness, the attainment of the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, the attainment of the cessation of perception and feeling.
"Defilement": the state that shares in decline. "Cleansing": the state that shares in distinction. "Emergence": cleansing is also emergence; emerging from this or that concentration is also emergence. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the defilement, the cleansing, and the emergence in the meditative absorptions, liberations, concentrations, and attainments. ^2-828

(8) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the recollection of past lives? Here the Tathāgata recollects his manifold past lives, that is: one birth, two births, three births, four births, five births, ten births, twenty births, thirty births, forty births, fifty births, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births, many eons of world-contraction, many eons of world-expansion, many eons of world-contraction and expansion: "There I was so named, of such a clan, of such appearance, with such food, experiencing such happiness and pain, with such a life span; passing away from there, I arose elsewhere; and there too I was so named, of such a clan, of such appearance, with such food, experiencing such happiness and pain, with such a life span; passing away from there, I was reborn here." Thus with its aspects and details he recollects his manifold past lives. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the recollection of past lives. ^2-829

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-830]]

(9) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the passing away and rebirth of beings? Here the Tathāgata, with the divine eye, purified and surpassing the human, sees beings passing away and being reborn — inferior and refined, beautiful and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate — and he knows beings faring according to their actions: "These beings, indeed, endowed with bodily misconduct, endowed with verbal misconduct, endowed with mental misconduct, revilers of the noble ones, of wrong view, undertaking action based on wrong view — with the breakup of the body after death they have been reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell. But these beings, endowed with good bodily conduct, endowed with good verbal conduct, endowed with good mental conduct, not revilers of the noble ones, of right view, undertaking action based on right view — with the breakup of the body after death they have been reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world." Thus with the divine eye, purified and surpassing the human, he sees beings passing away and being reborn — inferior and refined, beautiful and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate — and he knows beings faring according to their actions. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the passing away and rebirth of beings. ^2-830

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-831]]

(10) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the destruction of the mental taints? Here the Tathāgata, with the destruction of the mental taints, having realised for himself with direct knowledge, in this very life, the taintless liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom, enters and dwells in it. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the destruction of the mental taints.
The Tens.
The Analysis of Knowledge is finished. ^2-831
