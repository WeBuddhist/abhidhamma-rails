## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Pañhāpucchakaṃ (Dhātu)

[[#^toc-3-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchake aṭṭhārasannaṃ dhātūnaṃ heṭṭhā vuttanayānusāreneva kusalādibhāvo veditabbo. Ārammaṇattikesu pana 'cha dhātuyo parittārammaṇā'ti idaṃ pañcannaṃ cakkhuviññāṇādīnaṃ manodhātuyā ca ekantena pañcasu rūpārammaṇādīsu pavattiṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ; 'dve dhātuyo'ti vuttānaṃ pana dhammadhātumanoviññāṇadhātūnaṃ manāyatanadhammāyatanesu vuttanayeneva parittamahaggataappamāṇanavattabbārammaṇatā veditabbā. Iti imasmimpi pañhāpucchake soḷasa dhātuyo kāmāvacarā, dve catubhūmakā lokiyalokuttaramissakā kathitā. Evamayaṃ dhātuvibhaṅgopi suttantabhājanīyaabhidhammabhājanīyapañhāpucchakavasena teparivaṭṭaṃ nīharitvāva bhājetvā desitoti veditabbaṃ.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-practice.md`)

### Pañhāpucchakaṃ (Dhātu)

[[#^toc-3-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchake aṭṭhārasannaṃ dhātūnaṃ heṭṭhā vuttanayānusāreneva kusalākusalābyākatabhāvo veditabbo. Ārammaṇattikesu ca pañcannaṃ cakkhuviññāṇādīnaṃ manodhātuyā ca ekantena pañcasu rūpārammaṇādīsu pavattito parittārammaṇatā; dhammadhātumanoviññāṇadhātūnaṃ pana manāyatanadhammāyatanesu vuttanayeneva parittamahaggatāppamāṇārammaṇatā veditabbā. Iti soḷasa dhātuyo kāmāvacarā, dve catubhūmikā lokiyalokuttaramissakā kathitā. Tasmā yoginā "pañcadvāre uppannā viññāṇadhātu parittārammaṇāva, manoviññāṇadhātu pana appamāṇārammaṇāpi hotī"ti sallakkhetvā, parittesu ārammaṇesu rajjanaṃ dussanaṃ muyhanañca pahāya, manoviññāṇadhātuṃ maggaphalanibbānesu upanento appamāṇārammaṇaṃ bhāvanaṃ upasampādetvā, kāmāvacarato uttari nīharanto cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

#### The Section of Questions and Answers ^2-3-3-0

There are eighteen elements – the eye element, the element of sights, the eye-consciousness element; the ear element, the element of sounds, the ear-consciousness element; the nose element, the element of smells, the nose-consciousness element; the tongue element, the element of tastes, the tongue-consciousness element; the body element, the element of things touched, the body-consciousness element; the mind element, the element of mental objects, and the mind-consciousness element.
186. Of the eighteen elements, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral… how many involve conflict, how many are free from conflict? ^2-186

##### The Triads ^2-3-3-1-0

187. Sixteen elements are neutral. Two elements may be wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral. Ten elements cannot be described as joined with pleasant feeling, joined with painful feeling, or joined with feeling that is neither painful nor pleasant. Five elements are joined with feeling that is neither painful nor pleasant. The body-consciousness element may be joined with pleasant feeling or with painful feeling. The mind-consciousness element may be joined with pleasant feeling, with painful feeling, or with feeling that is neither painful nor pleasant. The element of mental objects may be joined with pleasant feeling, with painful feeling, or with feeling that is neither painful nor pleasant, or may not be describable as joined with any of these.
Ten elements are neither results nor things that produce results. Five elements are results. The mind element may be a result, or neither a result nor something that produces results. Two elements may be results, may produce results, or may be neither.
Ten elements are taken up at birth and able to be clung to. The element of sounds is not taken up but able to be clung to. Five elements may be taken up and able to be clung to, or not taken up but able to be clung to. Two elements may be taken up and able to be clung to, not taken up but able to be clung to, or neither taken up nor able to be clung to.
Sixteen elements are undefiled yet open to defilement. Two elements may be defiled and open to defilement, undefiled yet open to defilement, or neither defiled nor open to defilement. Fifteen elements are without thought and examination. The mind element has both thought and examination. The mind-consciousness element may have both thought and examination, examination only, or neither. The element of mental objects may have both thought and examination, examination only, or neither, or may not be describable as any of these. Ten elements cannot be described as accompanied by joy, by happiness, or by equanimity. Five elements are accompanied by equanimity. The body-consciousness element is not accompanied by joy; it may be accompanied by happiness or not; it is not accompanied by equanimity; or it may not be describable as "accompanied by happiness". Two elements may be accompanied by joy, by happiness, or by equanimity, or may not be describable as accompanied by any of these.
Sixteen elements are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. Two elements may be abandoned by seeing, by development, or by neither. Sixteen elements have root causes to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by development. Two elements may have root causes to be abandoned by seeing, root causes to be abandoned by development, or root causes to be abandoned by neither. Sixteen elements neither lead to further accumulation nor to dismantling. Two elements may lead to accumulation, to dismantling, or to neither. Sixteen elements belong neither to trainees nor to those beyond training. Two elements may belong to trainees, to those beyond training, or to neither.
Sixteen elements are limited. Two elements may be limited, expanded, or measureless. Ten elements have no object. Six elements have a limited object. Two elements may have a limited object, an expanded object, or a measureless object, or may not be describable as having any of these. Sixteen elements are middling. Two elements may be inferior, middling, or superior. Sixteen elements are not fixed in their outcome. Two elements may be fixed in wrongness, fixed in rightness, or not fixed.
Ten elements have no object. Six elements cannot be described as having the path as their object, the path as their cause, or the path as their dominant factor. Two elements may have the path as their object, the path as their cause, or the path as their dominant factor, or may not be describable as any of these. Ten elements may be arisen or bound to arise; they cannot be described as "not arisen". The element of sounds may be arisen or not arisen; it cannot be described as "bound to arise". Six elements may be arisen, not arisen, or bound to arise. The element of mental objects may be arisen, not arisen, or bound to arise, or may not be describable as any of these.
Seventeen elements may be past, future, or present. The element of mental objects may be past, future, or present, or may not be describable as past, future, or present. Ten elements have no object. Six elements have a present object. Two elements may have a past object, a future object, or a present object, or may not be describable as having any of these; they may be internal, external, or both internal and external.
Ten elements have no object. Six elements may have an internal object, an external object, or an object both internal and external. Two elements may have an internal object, an external object, or an object both internal and external, or may not be describable as having any of these. The element of sights is visible and offers resistance. Nine elements are invisible and offer no resistance. Eight elements are invisible and offer no resistance. ^2-187
