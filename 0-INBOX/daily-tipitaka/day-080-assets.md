## Pāli source verses

##### Viññāṇakkhandho ^2-1-1-5-0

26. Tattha katamo viññāṇakkhandho? Yaṃ kiñci viññāṇaṃ atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā yaṃ dūre santike vā, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati viññāṇakkhandho. ^2-26

27. Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ atītaṃ? Yaṃ viññāṇaṃ atītaṃ niruddhaṃ vigataṃ vipariṇataṃ atthaṅgataṃ abbhatthaṅgataṃ uppajjitvā vigataṃ atītaṃ atītaṃsena saṅgahitaṃ, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ sotaviññāṇaṃ ghānaviññāṇaṃ jivhāviññāṇaṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaṃ – idaṃ vuccati viññāṇaṃ atītaṃ.
Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ anāgataṃ? Yaṃ viññāṇaṃ ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ asañjātaṃ anibbattaṃ anabhinibbattaṃ apātubhūtaṃ anuppannaṃ asamuppannaṃ anuṭṭhitaṃ asamuṭṭhitaṃ anāgataṃ anāgataṃsena saṅgahitaṃ, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ sotaviññāṇaṃ ghānaviññāṇaṃ jivhāviññāṇaṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaṃ – idaṃ vuccati viññāṇaṃ anāgataṃ.
Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ paccuppannaṃ? Yaṃ viññāṇaṃ jātaṃ bhūtaṃ sañjātaṃ nibbattaṃ abhinibbattaṃ pātubhūtaṃ uppannaṃ samuppannaṃ uṭṭhitaṃ samuṭṭhitaṃ paccuppannaṃ paccuppannaṃsena saṅgahitaṃ, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ sotaviññāṇaṃ ghānaviññāṇaṃ jivhāviññāṇaṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaṃ – idaṃ vuccati viññāṇaṃ paccuppannaṃ. ^2-27

28. Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ ajjhattaṃ? Yaṃ viññāṇaṃ tesaṃ tesaṃ sattānaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ niyakaṃ pāṭipuggalikaṃ upādinnaṃ, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ sotaviññāṇaṃ ghānaviññāṇaṃ jivhāviññāṇaṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaṃ – idaṃ vuccati viññāṇaṃ ajjhattaṃ.
Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ bahiddhā? Yaṃ viññāṇaṃ tesaṃ tesaṃ parasattānaṃ parapuggalānaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ niyakaṃ pāṭipuggalikaṃ upādinnaṃ, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ sotaviññāṇaṃ ghānaviññāṇaṃ jivhāviññāṇaṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaṃ – idaṃ vuccati viññāṇaṃ bahiddhā. ^2-28

29. Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ oḷārikaṃ sukhumaṃ? Akusalaṃ viññāṇaṃ oḷārikaṃ, kusalābyākatā viññāṇā sukhumā. Kusalākusalā viññāṇā oḷārikā, abyākataṃ viññāṇaṃ sukhumaṃ. Dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ oḷārikaṃ, sukhāya ca adukkhamasukhāya ca vedanāhi sampayuttā viññāṇā sukhumā. Sukhadukkhāhi vedanāhi sampayuttā viññāṇā oḷārikā, adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ sukhumaṃ. Asamāpannassa viññāṇaṃ oḷārikaṃ, samāpannassa viññāṇaṃ sukhumaṃ. Sāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ oḷārikaṃ, anāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ sukhumaṃ. Taṃ taṃ vā pana viññāṇaṃ upādāyupādāya viññāṇaṃ oḷārikaṃ sukhumaṃ daṭṭhabbaṃ. ^2-29

30. Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ hīnaṃ paṇītaṃ? Akusalaṃ viññāṇaṃ hīnaṃ, kusalābyākatā viññāṇā paṇītā. Kusalākusalā viññāṇā hīnā, abyākataṃ viññāṇaṃ paṇītaṃ. Dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ hīnaṃ, sukhāya ca adukkhamasukhāya ca vedanāhi sampayuttā viññāṇā paṇītā. Sukhadukkhāhi vedanāhi sampayuttā viññāṇā hīnā, adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ paṇītaṃ. Asamāpannassa viññāṇaṃ hīnaṃ, samāpannassa viññāṇaṃ paṇītaṃ. Sāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ hīnaṃ, anāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ paṇītaṃ. Taṃ taṃ vā pana viññāṇaṃ upādāyupādāya viññāṇaṃ hīnaṃ paṇītaṃ daṭṭhabbaṃ. ^2-30

31. Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ dūre? Akusalaṃ viññāṇaṃ kusalābyākatehi viññāṇehi dūre; kusalābyākatā viññāṇā akusalā viññāṇā dūre; kusalaṃ viññāṇaṃ akusalābyākatehi viññāṇehi dūre; akusalābyākatā viññāṇā kusalā viññāṇā dūre; abyākataṃ viññāṇaṃ kusalākusalehi viññāṇehi dūre; kusalākusalā viññāṇā abyākatā viññāṇā dūre. Dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ sukhāya ca adukkhamasukhāya ca vedanāhi sampayuttehi viññāṇehi dūre; sukhāya ca adukkhamasukhāya ca vedanāhi sampayuttā viññāṇā dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttā viññāṇā dūre; sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ dukkhāya ca adukkhamasukhāya ca vedanāhi sampayuttehi viññāṇehi dūre; dukkhāya ca adukkhamasukhāya ca vedanāhi sampayuttā viññāṇā sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā viññāṇā dūre; adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ sukhadukkhāhi vedanāhi sampayuttehi viññāṇehi dūre; sukhadukkhāhi vedanāhi sampayuttā viññāṇā adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā viññāṇā dūre. Asamāpannassa viññāṇaṃ samāpannassa viññāṇā dūre; samāpannassa viññāṇaṃ asamāpannassa viññāṇā dūre. Sāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ anāsavā viññāṇā dūre; anāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ sāsavā viññāṇā dūre – idaṃ vuccati viññāṇaṃ dūre.
Tattha katamaṃ viññāṇaṃ santike? Akusalaṃ viññāṇaṃ akusalassa viññāṇassa santike; kusalaṃ viññāṇaṃ kusalassa viññāṇassa santike; abyākataṃ viññāṇaṃ abyākatassa viññāṇassa santike. Dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttassa viññāṇassa santike; sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttassa viññāṇassa santike; adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ viññāṇaṃ adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttassa viññāṇassa santike. Asamāpannassa viññāṇaṃ asamāpannassa viññāṇassa santike; samāpannassa viññāṇaṃ samāpannassa viññāṇassa santike. Sāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ sāsavassa viññāṇassa santike; anāsavaṃ viññāṇaṃ anāsavassa viññāṇassa santike – idaṃ vuccati viññāṇaṃ santike. Taṃ taṃ vā pana viññāṇaṃ upādāyupādāya viññāṇaṃ dūre santike daṭṭhabbaṃ.
Suttantabhājanīyaṃ. ^2-31

## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

#### Viññāṇakkhandho

[[#^toc-1-1-5|↑↑↑]]

Viññāṇakkhandhaniddese 'yaṃ kiñci viññāṇa'nti catubhūmakaviññāṇaṃ pariyādiyati; cakkhuviññāṇantiādīni atītādivasena niddiṭṭhaviññāṇaṃ sabhāvato dassetuṃ vuttāni. Tattha cakkhuviññāṇādīni pañca cakkhupasādādivatthukāneva; manoviññāṇaṃ hadayavatthukampi avatthukampi. Sabbaṃ catubhūmakaviññāṇaṃ. Sesaṃ vedanākkhandhasadisamevāti. Ayaṃ viññāṇakkhandhaniddeso.

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ

[[#^toc-1-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ nāma tesaṃyeva khandhānaṃ abhidhammanayena vibhajanaṃ. Satthā imasmiṃ abhidhammabhājanīye khandhaṃ bhājento tike gahetvā dukesu pakkhipi, duke gahetvā tikesu pakkhipi, tike ca duke ca ubhatovaḍḍhananīhārena āhari, avasāne sattavidhādivasena bahuvidhena dassesi. Iti dukamūlako, tikamūlako, ubhatovaḍḍhitako, bahuvidhavāroti cattāro mahāvārā. Kasmā evaṃ bahudhā? Puggalajjhāsayena ceva desanāvilāsena ca — ye devaputtā tike ādāya dukesu pakkhipitvā kathiyamānaṃ paṭivijjhituṃ sakkonti, tesaṃ sappāyavasena tathā desesi; sammāsambuddho ca attano mahāvisayatāya yathā yathā icchati tathā tathā desetuṃ sakkoti.

#### Rūpakkhandho (Abhidhamma)

[[#^toc-1-2-1|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye rūpakkhandhaniddeso heṭṭhā dhammasaṅgaṇiyaṃ rūpakaṇḍe vitthāritanayeneva veditabbo — tattha ekavidhādivasena rūpassa vibhāgo vitthārito, tasmā idha na puna vitthārito.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-practice.md`)

#### Viññāṇakkhandho

[[#^toc-1-1-5|↑↑↑]]

Viññāṇakkhandhaniddese cakkhuviññāṇādīni pañca cakkhupasādādivatthukāneva, manoviññāṇaṃ hadayavatthukampi avatthukampi; sabbaṃ catubhūmakaṃ viññāṇaṃ. Na sakkā viññāṇaṃ "manosamphassaja"nti niddisituṃ — viññāṇameva hi mano nāma. Yassa yogino "viññāṇaṃ paccayuppannaṃ, cakkhuñca paṭicca rūpe ca uppajjati cakkhuviññāṇa"nti pākaṭaṃ hoti, so "attā viññātā"ti gāhaṃ pajahati. Tasmā yoginā viññāṇe attagāhaṃ pahāya, "paṭiccasamuppannaṃ ida"nti sallakkhetvā, cittānupassanaṃ bhāvetvā, viññāṇe anupādāya cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-vibhanga-atthakatha.md#^2-66]]
[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-vibhanga-atthakatha.md#^2-67]]

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ

[[#^toc-1-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye satthā khandhe bhājento tike gahetvā dukesu pakkhipi, duke gahetvā tikesu pakkhipi, ubhatovaḍḍhananīhārena āhari, avasāne bahuvidhena dassesi — iti cattāro mahāvārā. Etaṃ pana bahudhā desanaṃ puggalajjhāsayena ceva desanāvilāsena ca katanti veditabbaṃ: yassa yena ākārena paṭivijjhanaṃ sappāyaṃ, tassa tenākārena desesi. Tasmā yoginā "nāyaṃ dhammo ekeneva nayena gahetabbo"ti ñatvā, attano cittassa sappāyaṃ kammaṭṭhānanayaṃ gahetvā, mānaṃ diṭṭhiparāmāsañca pahāya, nānānayehi khandhe sammasanto paññaṃ upasampādetvā cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

#### Rūpakkhandho (Abhidhamma)

## English translation (from `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-Contemporary-English-Abhidhamma/en-vibhanga-ai.md`)

26. What is the aggregate of consciousness? Whatever consciousness there is — internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near — taken together as a single whole: this is called the aggregate of consciousness. ^2-26

27. What is past consciousness? Consciousness that has ceased … called past, included in the past division: eye-consciousness, ear-consciousness, nose-consciousness, tongue-consciousness, body-consciousness, mind-consciousness — this is called past consciousness.
What is future consciousness? Consciousness that is not yet born … called future, included in the future division: eye-consciousness … mind-consciousness — this is called future consciousness.
What is present consciousness? Consciousness that is born … called present, included in the present division: eye-consciousness … mind-consciousness — this is called present consciousness. ^2-27

28. What is internal consciousness? Consciousness belonging internally, individually, to this or that being: eye-consciousness … mind-consciousness — this is called internal consciousness.
What is external consciousness? Consciousness belonging internally, individually, to this or that other being or other person: eye-consciousness … mind-consciousness — this is called external consciousness. ^2-28

29. What is gross or subtle consciousness? Unwholesome consciousness is gross; wholesome-and-indeterminate consciousness is subtle. Wholesome-and-unwholesome consciousness is gross; indeterminate consciousness is subtle. Consciousness associated with painful feeling is gross; consciousness associated with pleasant and neutral feeling is subtle. Consciousness associated with pleasant-and-painful feeling is gross; consciousness associated with neutral feeling is subtle. The consciousness of one who has not attained absorption is gross; the consciousness of one who has attained absorption is subtle. Consciousness affected by taints is gross; consciousness unaffected by taints is subtle. But consciousness is gross or subtle only relative to this or that other consciousness. ^2-29

30. What is inferior or superior consciousness? Unwholesome consciousness is inferior; wholesome-and-indeterminate consciousness is superior. Wholesome-and-unwholesome consciousness is inferior; indeterminate consciousness is superior. Consciousness associated with painful feeling is inferior; consciousness associated with pleasant and neutral feeling is superior. Consciousness associated with pleasant-and-painful feeling is inferior; consciousness associated with neutral feeling is superior. The consciousness of one who has not attained absorption is inferior; the consciousness of one who has attained absorption is superior. Consciousness affected by taints is inferior; consciousness unaffected by taints is superior. But consciousness is inferior or superior only relative to this or that other consciousness. ^2-30

31. What is far consciousness? Unwholesome consciousness is far from wholesome-and-indeterminate consciousness; wholesome-and-indeterminate consciousness is far from unwholesome consciousness; wholesome consciousness is far from unwholesome-and-indeterminate consciousness; unwholesome-and-indeterminate consciousness is far from wholesome consciousness. Indeterminate consciousness is far from wholesome-and-unwholesome consciousness; wholesome-and-unwholesome consciousness is far from indeterminate consciousness. Consciousness associated with painful feeling is far from consciousness associated with pleasant and neutral feeling; consciousness associated with pleasant and neutral feeling is far from consciousness associated with painful feeling; consciousness associated with pleasant feeling is far from consciousness associated with painful and neutral feeling; consciousness associated with painful and neutral feeling is far from consciousness associated with pleasant feeling; consciousness associated with neutral feeling is far from consciousness associated with pleasant-and-painful feeling; consciousness associated with pleasant-and-painful feeling is far from consciousness associated with neutral feeling. The consciousness of one who has not attained absorption is far from the consciousness of one who has attained absorption, and the consciousness of one who has attained absorption is far from the consciousness of one who has not. Consciousness affected by taints is far from consciousness unaffected by taints, and consciousness unaffected by taints is far from consciousness affected by taints — this is called far consciousness.
What is near consciousness? Unwholesome consciousness is near unwholesome consciousness; wholesome consciousness is near wholesome consciousness; indeterminate consciousness is near indeterminate consciousness. Consciousness associated with painful feeling is near consciousness associated with painful feeling; consciousness associated with pleasant feeling is near consciousness associated with pleasant feeling; consciousness associated with neutral feeling is near consciousness associated with neutral feeling. The consciousness of one who has not attained absorption is near the consciousness of one who has not, and the consciousness of one who has attained absorption is near the consciousness of one who has. Consciousness affected by taints is near consciousness affected by taints, and consciousness unaffected by taints is near consciousness unaffected by taints. This is called near consciousness. But consciousness is far or near only relative to this or that other consciousness.
The Discourse Method [is finished]. ^2-31

#### The Abhidhamma Method ^2-1-2-0

32. The five aggregates: the aggregate of form, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, the aggregate of consciousness. ^2-32

##### The Aggregate of Form ^2-1-2-1-0

33. What is the aggregate of form? As a single category: all form is not a root-cause, is without a root-cause, is dissociated from a root-cause, is conditioned, is constructed, is form, belongs to the world, is subject to taints, is a fetter, a tie, a flood, a bond, a hindrance, a misapprehension, a clinging, and a defilement; it is indeterminate, has no object, has no mental factors, is dissociated from mind, is neither a result nor productive of a result, is defiled without itself being a defilement; it is neither accompanied by applied and sustained thought, nor merely by sustained thought, and is without both; it is not accompanied by joy, by pleasure, or by equanimity; it is abandoned neither by seeing nor by development, and its root-cause is abandoned by neither; it neither leads to accumulation nor to dispersal; it is neither a trainee's nor one beyond training; it is limited, belongs to the sense sphere, not to the fine-material sphere, not to the immaterial sphere, is included, not unincluded, undetermined, does not lead out of the round; it has already arisen, is cognizable by the six kinds of consciousness, is impermanent, and is overcome by old age. Thus, as a single category, this is the aggregate of form.
As two categories: there is form derived from the four great elements, and form that is not so derived. There is form that is clung to, and form that is not clung to. There is form that is both clung to and a basis for clinging, and form that is not clung to but is a basis for clinging. There is form that is visible, and form that is invisible. There is form that offers resistance, and form that offers none. There is form that is a faculty, and form that is not a faculty. There is form that is a great element, and form that is not a great element. There is form that is communication, and form that is not communication. There is form originating from mind, and form not originating from mind. There is form that co-arises with mind, and form that does not. There is form that follows mind's course, and form that does not. There is form that is internal, and form that is external. There is form that is gross, and form that is subtle. There is form that is far, and form that is near … up to … there is form that is edible nutriment, and form that is not edible nutriment. Thus, as two categories, this is the aggregate of form.
(This should be elaborated in the same way as in the Analysis of Form.)
As three categories: whatever form is internal is derived from the four great elements; whatever form is external is either derived from them or not. Whatever form is internal is clung to; whatever form is external is either clung to or not. Whatever form is internal is both clung to and a basis for clinging; whatever form is external is either both clung to and a basis for clinging, or not clung to but a basis for clinging … up to … whatever form is internal is not edible nutriment; whatever form is external is either edible nutriment or not. Thus, as three categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As four categories: whatever form is derived from the four great elements is either clung to or not; whatever form is not so derived is either clung to or not. Whatever form is derived from the four great elements is both clung to and a basis for clinging, or not clung to but a basis for clinging; whatever form is not so derived is either of these as well. Whatever form is derived from the four great elements offers resistance or offers none; whatever form is not so derived offers resistance or offers none. Whatever form is derived from the four great elements is gross or subtle; whatever form is not so derived is gross or subtle. Whatever form is derived from the four great elements is far or near; whatever form is not so derived is far or near … up to … form that is seen, heard, sensed, or cognized. Thus, as four categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As five categories: the earth element, the water element, the fire element, the air element, and whatever form is derived from the four great elements. Thus, as five categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As six categories: form cognizable by eye-consciousness, form cognizable by ear-consciousness, form cognizable by nose-consciousness, form cognizable by tongue-consciousness, form cognizable by body-consciousness, and form cognizable by mind-consciousness. Thus, as six categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As seven categories: form cognizable by eye-consciousness, ear-consciousness, nose-consciousness, tongue-consciousness, body-consciousness, the mind-element, and the mind-consciousness-element. Thus, as seven categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As eight categories: form cognizable by eye-consciousness, ear-consciousness, nose-consciousness, and tongue-consciousness; form cognizable by body-consciousness — this being accompanied by either pleasant or painful contact; the mind-element; and the mind-consciousness-element. Thus, as eight categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As nine categories: the eye faculty, the ear faculty, the nose faculty, the tongue faculty, the body faculty, the female faculty, the male faculty, the life faculty, and whatever form is not a faculty. Thus, as nine categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As ten categories: the eye faculty, the ear faculty, the nose faculty, the tongue faculty, the body faculty, the female faculty, the male faculty, the life faculty, and form that is not a faculty, which either offers resistance or offers none. Thus, as ten categories, this is the aggregate of form.
As eleven categories: the eye base, the ear base, the nose base, the tongue base, the body base, the form base, the sound base, the smell base, the taste base, the tangible-object base, and whatever form is invisible, offers no resistance, and is included in the mind-object base. Thus, as eleven categories, this is the aggregate of form.
This is called the aggregate of form. ^2-33
