## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

#### Vedanākkhandho (Abhidhamma)

[[#^toc-1-2-2|↑↑↑]]

Vedanākkhandhaniddese 'ekavidhenā'ti ekakoṭṭhāsena; 'phassasampayutto'ti phassena sampayutto — sabbāpi catubhūmikavedanā. Sahetukaduke sahetukā catubhūmikavedanā, ahetukā kāmāvacarāva. Ayaṃ vedanākkhandho ekavidhena phassasampayuttato dassito, duvidhena sahetukāhetukato, tividhena jātito, catubbidhena bhūmantarato, pañcavidhena indriyato, chabbidhena vatthuto. Tattha sukhindriyadukkhindriyāni kāyappasādavatthukāni kāmāvacarāneva; somanassindriyaṃ chaṭṭhavatthukaṃ vā avatthukaṃ vā tebhūmakaṃ; domanassindriyaṃ chaṭṭhavatthukaṃ kāmāvacaraṃ; upekkhindriyaṃ catuppasādavatthukaṃ chaṭṭhavatthukaṃ avatthukañca catubhūmakaṃ. Sattavidhena manosamphassajā bhedato, aṭṭhavidhena kāyasamphassajā bhedato, navavidhadasavidhesu manoviññāṇadhātusamphassajā kusalādivasena tidhā bhinnā.

Vedanākkhandhaniddesassa pariyosāne cha vedanā cakkhusamphassajātiādivasena sampayuttapaccayavasena vuttā. Catubhūmikavedanā ca cakkhudvārādīsu tīhi ākārehi labbhati — upanissayavasena, samatikkamavasena, bhāvanāvasena. Manodvāre pana tīhipi ākārehi labbhanti: ekacco hi jātijarābyādhimaraṇaṃ bhayato disvā 'na kho sakkā nipajjitvā niddāyantena jātiādīhi muccituṃ, āditova vīriyaṃ kātuṃ sīlaṃ sodhetuṃ vaṭṭatī'ti cintetvā paṭipajjitvā arahattaṃ pāpuṇāti; tattha parikammavedanā kāmāvacarā, aṭṭhasamāpattivedanā rūpāvacarārūpāvacarā, maggaphalavedanā lokuttarāti. Aparo dhammārammaṇaṃ 'kiṃ nissita'nti pariggaṇhanto cattāri mahābhūtāni upādārūpañca 'rūpa'nti, tadārammaṇe dhamme 'arūpa'nti pariggaṇhitvā, sappaccayaṃ nāmarūpaṃ pariggaṇhitvā tīṇi lakkhaṇāni āropetvā vipassanāpaṭipāṭiyā saṅkhāre sammasitvā arahattaṃ pāpuṇāti.

#### Saññā-Saṅkhāra-Viññāṇakkhandhā (Abhidhamma)

[[#^toc-1-2-3|↑↑↑]]

Saññākkhandhādayopi vedanākkhandhe vuttena upāyena veditabbā. Kevalañhi saññākkhandhaniddese tikesu vedanāttikapītittikāpi labbhanti, dukesu ca sukhasahagatadukādayopi. Saṅkhārakkhandhaniddese phassassāpi saṅkhārakkhandhapariyāpannattā 'phassasampayutto'ti avatvā 'cittasampayutto'ti vuttaṃ; dukesu cettha hetudukādayopi labbhanti, tikā saññākkhandhasadisā eva. Viññāṇakkhandhaniddese cakkhusamphassajādibhāvaṃ avatvā 'cakkhuviññāṇa'ntiādi vuttaṃ — na hi sakkā viññāṇaṃ 'manosamphassaja'nti niddisituṃ; sesaṃ saññākkhandhe vuttasadisameva. Imesaṃ tiṇṇampi khandhānaṃ niddeseyeva vedanākkhandhaniddesato atirekatikadukā laddhā, tesaṃ vasena vārappabhedo veditabbo.

#### Saññā-Saṅkhāra-Viññāṇakkhandhā (Abhidhamma)

[[#^toc-1-2-3|↑↑↑]]

Saññākkhandhādayo vedanākkhandhe vuttena upāyena veditabbā. Saññākkhandhaniddese tikesu vedanāttikapītittikāpi labbhanti; saṅkhārakkhandhaniddese phassassāpi saṅkhārakkhandhapariyāpannattā "cittasampayutto"ti vuttaṃ; viññāṇakkhandhaniddese "cakkhuviññāṇa"ntiādi vuttaṃ, na hi sakkā viññāṇaṃ manosamphassajanti niddisituṃ. Iti ekasmiṃ nayamhi gahite sesā tenupāyena gahitāva honti. Tasmā yoginā ekasmiṃ khandhe sammasanañāṇaṃ paṭiladdhaṃ sesesupi upanetvā, "sabbe pañca khandhā aniccā dukkhā anattā"ti ekarasena passanto, khandhesu attagāhaṃ pahāya, sabbaso anupādāya cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

##### The Aggregate of Perception ^2-1-2-3-0

62. What is the aggregate of perception? As a single category: perception is associated with contact.
As two categories: there is perception that has a root-cause, and perception that is without a root-cause.
As three categories: there is perception that is wholesome, perception that is unwholesome, and perception that is indeterminate.
As four categories: there is perception belonging to the sense sphere, to the fine-material sphere, to the immaterial sphere, and perception that is unincluded.
As five categories: perception associated with the pleasure faculty, the pain faculty, the joy faculty, the displeasure faculty, and the equanimity faculty. Thus, as five categories, this is the aggregate of perception.
As six categories: perception born of eye-contact, ear-contact, nose-contact, tongue-contact, body-contact, and mind-contact. Thus, as six categories, this is the aggregate of perception.
As seven categories: perception born of eye-contact, ear-contact, nose-contact, tongue-contact, body-contact, mind-element-contact, and mind-consciousness-element-contact. Thus, as seven categories, this is the aggregate of perception.
As eight categories: perception born of eye-contact … up to … body-contact — the last accompanied by pleasure or by pain — together with perception born of mind-element-contact and mind-consciousness-element-contact. Thus, as eight categories, this is the aggregate of perception.

64. As a single category: Perception is associated with contact.
As two categories: there is perception that has a root-cause, and perception that is without a root-cause.
As three categories: there is perception that is a result, perception that produces a result, and perception that is neither. There is perception that is clung to and a basis for clinging, perception that is not clung to but a basis for clinging, and perception that is neither clung to nor a basis for clinging. There is perception that is defiled and a basis for defilement, perception that is undefiled but a basis for defilement, and perception that is neither. There is perception with applied and sustained thought, perception with only sustained thought, and perception with neither. There is perception accompanied by joy, perception accompanied by pleasure, and perception accompanied by equanimity. There is perception abandoned by seeing, perception abandoned by development, and perception abandoned by neither. There is perception whose root-cause is abandoned by seeing, whose root-cause is abandoned by development, and whose root-cause is abandoned by neither. There is perception that leads to accumulation, that leads to dispersal, and that does neither. There is perception belonging to a trainee, to one beyond training, and to neither. There is perception that is limited, that is exalted, and that is measureless. There is perception with a limited object, with an exalted object, and with a measureless object. There is perception that is inferior, that is middling, and that is superior. There is perception fixed in wrongness, fixed in rightness, and undetermined. There is perception with the path as its object, with the path as its root-cause, and with the path as its dominant factor. There is perception that has arisen, that has not arisen, and that is due to arise. There is perception that is past, that is future, and that is present. There is perception with a past object, with a future object, and with a present object. There is perception that is internal, that is external, and that is both. There is perception with an internal object, with an external object, and with both an internal and an external object. Thus, as ten categories, this is the aggregate of perception. ^2-64
