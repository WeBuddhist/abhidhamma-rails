#### Pañhāpucchakaṃ ^2-1-3-0

150. Pañcakkhandhā – rūpakkhandho, vedanākkhandho, saññākkhandho, saṅkhārakkhandho, viññāṇakkhandho. ^2-150

151. Pañcannaṃ khandhānaṃ kati kusalā, kati akusalā, kati abyākatā…pe… kati saraṇā, kati araṇā? ^2-151

##### Tikaṃ ^2-1-3-1-0

152. Rūpakkhandho abyākato. Cattāro khandhā siyā kusalā, siyā akusalā, siyā abyākatā. Dve khandhā na vattabbā – "sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā"tipi, "dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttā"tipi, "adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā"tipi. Tayo khandhā siyā sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā, siyā dukkhāya vedanāya sampayuttā, siyā adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttā. Rūpakkhandho nevavipākanavipākadhammadhammo. Cattāro khandhā siyā vipākā, siyā vipākadhammadhammā, siyā nevavipākanavipākadhammadhammā. Rūpakkhandho siyā upādinnupādāniyo, siyā anupādinnupādāniyo. Cattāro khandhā siyā upādinnupādāniyā, siyā anupādinnupādāniyā, siyā anupādinnaanupādāniyā.
Rūpakkhandho asaṃkiliṭṭhasaṃkilesiko. Cattāro khandhā siyā saṃkiliṭṭhasaṃkilesikā, siyā asaṃkiliṭṭhasaṃkilesikā, siyā asaṃkiliṭṭhaasaṃkilesikā. Rūpakkhandho avitakkaavicāro. Tayo khandhā siyā savitakkasavicārā, siyā avitakkavicāramattā, siyā avitakkaavicārā. Saṅkhārakkhandho siyā savitakkasavicāro, siyā avitakkavicāramatto, siyā avitakkaavicāro, siyā na vattabbo – "savitakkasavicāro"tipi, "avitakkavicāramatto"tipi, "avitakkaavicāro"tipi. Rūpakkhandho na vattabbo – "pītisahagato"tipi, "sukhasahagato"tipi, "upekkhāsahagato"tipi. Vedanākkhandho siyā pītisahagato na sukhasahagato na upekkhāsahagato, siyā na vattabbo – "pītisahagato"ti. Tayo khandhā siyā pītisahagatā, siyā sukhasahagatā, siyā upekkhāsahagatā, siyā na vattabbā – "pītisahagatā"tipi, "sukhasahagatā"tipi, "upekkhāsahagatā"tipi.
Rūpakkhandho neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbo. Cattāro khandhā siyā dassanena pahātabbā, siyā bhāvanāya pahātabbā, siyā neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbā. Rūpakkhandho neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbahetuko. Cattāro khandhā siyā dassanena pahātabbahetukā, siyā bhāvanāya pahātabbahetukā, siyā neva dassanena na bhāvanāya pahātabbahetukā. Rūpakkhandho nevācayagāmināpacayagāmī. Cattāro khandhā siyā ācayagāmino, siyā apacayagāmino, siyā nevācayagāmināpacayagāmino. Rūpakkhandho nevasekkhanāsekkho. Cattāro khandhā siyā sekkhā, siyā asekkhā, siyā nevasekkhanāsekkhā. Rūpakkhandho paritto. Cattāro khandhā siyā parittā, siyā mahaggatā, siyā appamāṇā. Rūpakkhandho anārammaṇo. Cattāro khandhā siyā parittārammaṇā, siyā mahaggatārammaṇā, siyā appamāṇārammaṇā, siyā na vattabbā – "parittārammaṇā"tipi, "mahaggatārammaṇā"tipi, "appamāṇārammaṇā"tipi. Rūpakkhandho majjhimo. Cattāro khandhā siyā hīnā, siyā majjhimā, siyā paṇītā. Rūpakkhandho aniyato. Cattāro khandhā siyā micchattaniyatā, siyā sammattaniyatā, siyā aniyatā.
Rūpakkhandho anārammaṇo. Cattāro khandhā siyā maggārammaṇā, siyā maggahetukā, siyā maggādhipatino, siyā na vattabbā – "maggārammaṇā"tipi, "maggahetukā"tipi, "maggādhipatino"tipi; siyā uppannā, siyā anuppannā, siyā uppādino; siyā atītā, siyā anāgatā, siyā paccuppannā. Rūpakkhandho anārammaṇo. Cattāro khandhā siyā atītārammaṇā, siyā anāgatārammaṇā, siyā paccuppannārammaṇā, siyā na vattabbā – "atītārammaṇā"tipi, "anāgatārammaṇā"tipi, "paccuppannārammaṇā"tipi; siyā ajjhattā, siyā bahiddhā, siyā ajjhattabahiddhā. Rūpakkhandho anārammaṇo. Cattāro khandhā siyā ajjhattārammaṇā, siyā bahiddhārammaṇā, siyā ajjhattabahiddhārammaṇā, siyā na vattabbā – "ajjhattārammaṇā"tipi, "bahiddhārammaṇā"tipi, "ajjhattabahiddhārammaṇā"tipi. Cattāro khandhā anidassanaappaṭighā. Rūpakkhandho siyā sanidassanasappaṭigho, siyā anidassanasappaṭigho, siyā anidassanaappaṭigho. ^2-152

## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Pañhāpucchakaṃ

[[#^toc-1-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchane pañcannaṃ khandhānaṃ "katikusalā? Katiakusalā? Katiabyākatā"tiādinā nayena yaṃ labbhati yañca na labbhati taṃ sabbaṃ pucchitvā, vissajjane "rūpakkhandho abyākato"tiādinā nayena yaṃ labbhati tadeva uddhaṭaṃ. Ārammaṇattikesu cattāro khandhā pañcapaṇṇāsa kāmāvacaradhamme ārabbha rajjantassa dussantassa muyhantassa saṃvarantassa sammasantassa ca parittārammaṇā honti, sattavīsati rūpārūpāvacaradhamme ārabbha mahaggatārammaṇā, maggaphalanibbānāni paccavekkhantassa appamāṇārammaṇā, paññattiṃ paccavekkhaṇakāle navattabbārammaṇā. Tathā attano khandhadhātuāyatanāni ārabbha ajjhattārammaṇā, paresaṃ ārabbha bahiddhārammaṇā. Iti bhagavā imaṃ khandhavibhaṅgaṃ suttantabhājanīyādivasena tayo parivaṭṭe nīharitvā bhājento dassesi; tīsupi parivaṭṭesu ekova paricchedo — rūpakkhandho hi sabbattha kāmāvacaroyeva, cattāro khandhā catubhūmakā lokiyalokuttaramissakā kathitā.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-practice.md`)

### Pañhāpucchakaṃ

[[#^toc-1-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchane pañcannaṃ khandhānaṃ kusalākusalābyākatādivasena pañhā pucchitvā vissajjitā. Ārammaṇattikesu cattāro khandhā kāmāvacaradhamme ārabbha rajjantassa dussantassa muyhantassa vā parittārammaṇā honti, saṃvarantassa sammasantassa vā tatheva; maggaphalanibbānāni paccavekkhantassa appamāṇārammaṇā. Attano khandhe ārabbha ajjhattārammaṇā, paresaṃ ārabbha bahiddhārammaṇā. Iti ekasmiṃyeva ārammaṇe rajjanaṃ vā saṃvaraṇaṃ vā hoti. Tasmā yoginā "ārammaṇe patiṭṭhitaṃ me cittaṃ rajjati vā saṃvarati vā"ti sallakkhetvā, ārammaṇesu rāgadosamohe pahāya, tesuyeva saṃvaraṃ sammasanañca upasampādetvā, ajjhattabahiddhā khandhe samaṃ passanto cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

## English translation (from `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-Contemporary-English-Abhidhamma/en-vibhanga-ai.md`)

150. The five aggregates: the aggregate of form, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, the aggregate of consciousness. ^2-150

151. Of the five aggregates, how many are wholesome, how many are unwholesome, how many are indeterminate … up to … how many are with strife, how many are without strife? ^2-151

##### The Triads ^2-1-3-1-0

152. The aggregate of form is indeterminate. The other four aggregates may be wholesome, may be unwholesome, or may be indeterminate. It cannot rightly be said of two aggregates — feeling and perception — that they are "associated with pleasant feeling," or "associated with painful feeling," or "associated with neither-pleasant-nor-painful feeling." Three aggregates — perception, formations, and consciousness — may be associated with pleasant feeling, may be associated with painful feeling, or may be associated with neither-pleasant-nor-painful feeling. The aggregate of form is neither a result nor productive of a result. The other four aggregates may be results, may be productive of results, or may be neither. The aggregate of form may be both clung to and a basis for clinging, or may be not clung to but a basis for clinging. The other four aggregates may be clung to and a basis for clinging, may be not clung to but a basis for clinging, or may be neither clung to nor a basis for clinging.
The aggregate of form is undefiled but a basis for defilement. The other four aggregates may be defiled and a basis for defilement, may be undefiled but a basis for defilement, or may be neither. The aggregate of form is without applied and sustained thought. Three aggregates — feeling, perception, and consciousness — may have applied and sustained thought, may have only sustained thought, or may have neither. The aggregate of formations may have applied and sustained thought, may have only sustained thought, may have neither, or it cannot rightly be said of it that it "has applied and sustained thought," or "has only sustained thought," or "has neither." It cannot rightly be said of the aggregate of form that it is "accompanied by joy," "accompanied by pleasure," or "accompanied by equanimity." The aggregate of feeling may be accompanied by joy without being accompanied by pleasure or equanimity, or it cannot rightly be said of it that it is "accompanied by joy." Three aggregates may be accompanied by joy, may be accompanied by pleasure, may be accompanied by equanimity, or it cannot rightly be said of them that they are "accompanied by joy," "accompanied by pleasure," or "accompanied by equanimity."
The aggregate of form is abandoned neither by seeing nor by development. The other four aggregates may be abandoned by seeing, may be abandoned by development, or may be abandoned by neither. The aggregate of form has a root-cause that is abandoned neither by seeing nor by development. The other four aggregates may have a root-cause abandoned by seeing, may have a root-cause abandoned by development, or may have a root-cause abandoned by neither. The aggregate of form neither leads to accumulation nor to dispersal. The other four aggregates may lead to accumulation, may lead to dispersal, or may do neither. The aggregate of form belongs to neither a trainee nor one beyond training. The other four aggregates may belong to a trainee, may belong to one beyond training, or may belong to neither. The aggregate of form is limited. The other four aggregates may be limited, may be exalted, or may be measureless. The aggregate of form has no object. The other four aggregates may have a limited object, may have an exalted object, or may have a measureless object, or it cannot rightly be said of them that they have "a limited object," "an exalted object," or "a measureless object." The aggregate of form is middling. The other four aggregates may be inferior, may be middling, or may be superior. The aggregate of form is undetermined. The other four aggregates may be fixed in wrongness, may be fixed in rightness, or may be undetermined.
The aggregate of form has no object. The other four aggregates may have the path as their object, may have the path as their root-cause, may have the path as their dominant factor, or it cannot rightly be said of them that they have "the path as object," "the path as root-cause," or "the path as dominant factor"; they may have arisen, may not have arisen, or may be due to arise; they may be past, may be future, or may be present. The aggregate of form has no object. The other four aggregates may have a past object, may have a future object, or may have a present object, or it cannot rightly be said of them that they have "a past object," "a future object," or "a present object"; they may be internal, may be external, or may be both. The aggregate of form has no object. The other four aggregates may have an internal object, may have an external object, may have both an internal and an external object, or it cannot rightly be said of them that they have "an internal object," "an external object," or "both." The other four aggregates are invisible and offer no resistance. The aggregate of form may be visible and offer resistance, may be invisible and offer resistance, or may be invisible and offer no resistance. ^2-152
