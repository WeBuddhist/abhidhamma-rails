## Pāli source verses

### Dhātuvibhaṅgo ^2-3-0

#### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ ^2-3-1-0

172. Cha dhātuyo – pathavīdhātu [paṭhavīdhātu (sī. syā.) evamuparipi], āpodhātu, tejodhātu, vāyodhātu, ākāsadhātu, viññāṇadhātu. ^2-172

173. Tattha katamā pathavīdhātu? Pathavīdhātudvayaṃ – atthi ajjhattikā, atthi bāhirā. Tattha katamā ajjhattikā pathavīdhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharigataṃ kakkhaḷattaṃ kakkhaḷabhāvo ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – kesā lomā nakhā dantā taco maṃsaṃ nhāru [nahāru (sī.)] aṭṭhi aṭṭhimiñjaṃ [aṭṭhimiñjā (sī.)] vakkaṃ hadayaṃ yakanaṃ kilomakaṃ pihakaṃ papphāsaṃ antaṃ antaguṇaṃ udariyaṃ karīsaṃ, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharigataṃ kakkhaḷattaṃ kakkhaḷabhāvo ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘ajjhattikā pathavīdhātu’.
Tattha katamā bāhirā pathavīdhātu? Yaṃ bāhiraṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharigataṃ kakkhaḷattaṃ kakkhaḷabhāvo bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – ayo lohaṃ tipu sīsaṃ sajjhaṃ [sajjhu (syā.)] muttā maṇi veḷuriyo saṅkho silā pavāḷaṃ rajataṃ jātarūpaṃ lohitaṅko [lohitaṅgo (syā. ka.), lohitako (?)] masāragallaṃ tiṇaṃ kaṭṭhaṃ sakkharā kaṭhalaṃ [kathalaṃ (ka.)] bhūmi pāsāṇo pabbato, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi bāhiraṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharigataṃ kakkhaḷattaṃ kakkhaḷabhāvo bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘bāhirā pathavīdhātu’. Yā ca ajjhattikā pathavīdhātu yā ca bāhirā pathavīdhātu, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati "pathavīdhātu". ^2-173

174. Tattha katamā āpodhātu? Āpodhātudvayaṃ – atthi ajjhattikā, atthi bāhirā. Tattha katamā ajjhattikā āpodhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ āpo āpogataṃ sineho sinehagataṃ [sneho snehagataṃ (syā.)] bandhanattaṃ rūpassa ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – pittaṃ semhaṃ pubbo lohitaṃ sedo medo assu vasā kheḷo siṅghāṇikā lasikā muttaṃ, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ āpo āpogataṃ sineho sinehagataṃ bandhanattaṃ rūpassa ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘ajjhattikā āpodhātu’.
Tattha katamā bāhirā āpodhātu? Yaṃ bāhiraṃ āpo āpogataṃ sineho sinehagataṃ bandhanattaṃ rūpassa bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – mūlaraso khandharaso tacaraso pattaraso puppharaso phalaraso khīraṃ dadhi sappi navanītaṃ telaṃ madhu phāṇitaṃ bhummāni vā udakāni antalikkhāni vā, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi bāhiraṃ āpo āpogataṃ sineho sinehagataṃ bandhanattaṃ rūpassa bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘bāhirā āpodhātu’. Yā ca ajjhattikā āpodhātu yā ca bāhirā āpodhātu, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati "āpodhātu". ^2-174

175. Tattha katamā tejodhātu? Tejodhātudvayaṃ – atthi ajjhattikā, atthi bāhirā. Tattha katamā ajjhattikā tejodhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ tejo tejogataṃ usmā usmāgataṃ usumaṃ usumagataṃ ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – yena ca santappati yena ca jīrīyati yena ca pariḍayhati yena ca asitapītakhāyitasāyitaṃ sammā pariṇāmaṃ gacchati, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ tejo tejogataṃ usmā usmāgataṃ usumaṃ usumagataṃ ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘ajjhattikā tejodhātu’.
Tattha katamā bāhirā tejodhātu? Yaṃ bāhiraṃ tejo tejogataṃ usmā usmāgataṃ usumaṃ usumagataṃ bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – kaṭṭhaggi palālaggi [sakalikaggi (sabbattha)] tiṇaggi gomayaggi thusaggi saṅkāraggi indaggi aggisantāpo sūriyasantāpo kaṭṭhasannicayasantāpo tiṇasannicayasantāpo dhaññasannicayasantāpo bhaṇḍasannicayasantāpo, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi bāhiraṃ tejo tejogataṃ usmā usmāgataṃ usumaṃ usumagataṃ bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘bāhirā tejodhātu’. Yā ca ajjhattikā tejodhātu yā ca bāhirā tejodhātu, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati "tejodhātu". ^2-175

176. Tattha katamā vāyodhātu? Vāyodhātudvayaṃ – atthi ajjhattikā, atthi bāhirā. Tattha katamā ajjhattikā vāyodhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ vāyo vāyogataṃ thambhitattaṃ rūpassa ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – uddhaṅgamā vātā adhogamā vātā kucchisayā vātā koṭṭhāsayā [koṭṭhasayā (sī. syā.)] vātā aṅgamaṅgānusārino vātā satthakavātā khurakavātā uppalakavātā assāso passāso iti vā, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ vāyo vāyogataṃ thambhitattaṃ rūpassa ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘ajjhattikā vāyodhātu’.
Tattha katamā bāhirā vāyodhātu? Yaṃ bāhiraṃ vāyo vāyogataṃ thambhitattaṃ rūpassa bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – puratthimā vātā pacchimā vātā uttarā vātā dakkhiṇā vātā sarajā vātā arajā vātā sītā vātā uṇhā vātā parittā vātā adhimattā vātā kāḷavātā verambhavātā pakkhavātā supaṇṇavātā tālavaṇṭavātā vidhūpanavātā, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi bāhiraṃ vāyo vāyogataṃ thambhitattaṃ rūpassa bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘bāhirā vāyodhātu’. Yā ca ajjhattikā vāyodhātu yā ca bāhirā vāyodhātu, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati "vāyodhātu". ^2-176

177. Tattha katamā ākāsadhātu? Ākāsadhātudvayaṃ – atthi ajjhattikā, atthi bāhirā. Tattha katamā ajjhattikā ākāsadhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ ākāso ākāsagataṃ aghaṃ aghagataṃ vivaro vivaragataṃ asamphuṭṭhaṃ maṃsalohitehi ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – kaṇṇacchiddaṃ nāsacchiddaṃ mukhadvāraṃ, yena ca asitapītakhāyitasāyitaṃ ajjhoharati, yattha ca asitapītakhāyitasāyitaṃ santiṭṭhati, yena ca asitapītakhāyitasāyitaṃ adhobhāgaṃ nikkhamati, yaṃ vā panaññampi atthi ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ ākāso ākāsagataṃ aghaṃ aghagataṃ vivaro vivaragataṃ asamphuṭṭhaṃ maṃsalohitehi ajjhattaṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘ajjhattikā ākāsadhātu’.
Tattha katamā bāhirā ākāsadhātu? Yaṃ bāhiraṃ ākāso ākāsagataṃ aghaṃ aghagataṃ vivaro vivaragataṃ asamphuṭṭhaṃ catūhi mahābhūtehi bahiddhā anupādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘bāhirā ākāsadhātu’. Yā ca ajjhattikā ākāsadhātu yā ca bāhirā ākāsadhātu, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati "ākāsadhātu". ^2-177

178. Tattha katamā viññāṇadhātu? Cakkhuviññāṇadhātu, sotaviññāṇadhātu, ghānaviññāṇadhātu, jivhāviññāṇadhātu, kāyaviññāṇadhātu, manoviññāṇadhātu – ayaṃ vuccati "viññāṇadhātu".
Imā cha dhātuyo. ^2-178

179. Aparāpi cha dhātuyo – sukhadhātu, dukkhadhātu, somanassadhātu, domanassadhātu, upekkhādhātu, avijjādhātu. ^2-179

180. Tattha katamā sukhadhātu? Yaṃ kāyikaṃ sātaṃ kāyikaṃ sukhaṃ kāyasamphassajaṃ sātaṃ sukhaṃ vedayitaṃ kāyasamphassajā sātā sukhā vedanā – ayaṃ vuccati "sukhadhātu".
Tattha katamā dukkhadhātu? Yaṃ kāyikaṃ asātaṃ kāyikaṃ dukkhaṃ kāyasamphassajaṃ asātaṃ dukkhaṃ vedayitaṃ kāyasamphassajā asātā dukkhā vedanā – ayaṃ vuccati "dukkhadhātu".
Tattha katamā somanassadhātu? Yaṃ cetasikaṃ sātaṃ cetasikaṃ sukhaṃ cetosamphassajaṃ sātaṃ sukhaṃ vedayitaṃ cetosamphassajā sātā sukhā vedanā – ayaṃ vuccati "somanassadhātu".
Tattha katamā domanassadhātu? Yaṃ cetasikaṃ asātaṃ cetasikaṃ dukkhaṃ cetosamphassajaṃ asātaṃ dukkhaṃ vedayitaṃ cetosamphassajā asātā dukkhā vedanā – ayaṃ vuccati "domanassadhātu".
Tattha katamā upekkhādhātu? Yaṃ cetasikaṃ neva sātaṃ nāsātaṃ cetosamphassajaṃ adukkhamasukhaṃ vedayitaṃ cetosamphassajā adukkhamasukhā vedanā – ayaṃ vuccati "upekkhādhātu".
Tattha katamā avijjādhātu? Yaṃ aññāṇaṃ adassanaṃ anabhisamayo ananubodho asambodho appaṭivedho asaṅgāhaṇā apariyogāhaṇā asamapekkhanā apaccavekkhaṇā apaccakkhakammaṃ dummejjhaṃ bālyaṃ asampajaññaṃ moho pamoho sammoho avijjā avijjogho avijjāyogo avijjānusayo avijjāpariyuṭṭhānaṃ avijjālaṅgī moho akusalamūlaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati "avijjādhātu".
Imā cha dhātuyo. ^2-180

181. Aparāpi cha dhātuyo – kāmadhātu, byāpādadhātu, vihiṃsādhātu, nekkhammadhātu, abyāpādadhātu, avihiṃsādhātu. ^2-181

## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ (Dhātu)

[[#^toc-3-1|↑↑↑]]

Imasmiṃ dhātūnaṃ suttantabhājanīye bhagavā sabbā dhātuyo chahi chahi dhātūhi saṅkhipitvā tīhi chakkehi dassento cha dhātuyotiādimāha. Tattha chāti gaṇanaparicchedo, dhātuyoti paricchinnadhammanidassanaṃ; dhātvaṭṭho nāma sabhāvaṭṭho, sabhāvaṭṭho nāma suññataṭṭho, suññataṭṭho nāma nissattaṭṭho. Iti pathavīdhātūti patiṭṭhānadhātu, āpodhātūti ābandhanadhātu, tejodhātūti paripācanadhātu, vāyodhātūti vitthambhanadhātu, ākāsadhātūti asamphuṭṭhadhātu, viññāṇadhātūti vijānanadhātu. Iminā kiṃ dassitaṃ hoti? Dhātumanasikāro. Imasmiṃ pana dhātumanasikāre kammaṃ katvā vipassanaṃ paṭṭhapetvā uttamatthaṃ arahattaṃ pāpuṇitukāmena catupārisuddhisīlaṃ sodhetabbaṃ, dasa pubbapalibodhā chinditabbā, ācariyassa santike sattavidhena uggahakosallena dasavidhena ca manasikārakosallena kammaṭṭhānaṃ uggaṇhitabbaṃ, tato pañcaṅgasamannāgate senāsane vivittokāsagatena vaṇṇasaṇṭhānadisokāsaparicchedavasena kesādīsu ekeko koṭṭhāso 'acetano abyākato suñño nissatto'ti manasikātabbo. Tiṇṇaṃ pana chakkānaṃ vasena aṭṭhārasa dhātuyo pariggahitāva honti; iti imasmiṃ suttantabhājanīye soḷasa dhātuyo kāmāvacarā, dve tebhūmikāti evamettha sammasanacārova kathitoti veditabbo.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-practice.md`)

### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ (Dhātu)

[[#^toc-3-1|↑↑↑]]

Suttantabhājanīye tīhi chakkehi dhātuyo saṅkhipitvā dassitā; tesu paṭhamena dhātumanasikāro dassito. Imasmiṃ pana dhātumanasikāre kammaṃ katvā vipassanaṃ paṭṭhapetvā uttamatthaṃ arahattaṃ pāpuṇitukāmena catupārisuddhisīlaṃ sodhetabbaṃ — sīlavato hi kammaṭṭhānabhāvanā ijjhati; visuddhasīlena ca sīle patiṭṭhāya dasa pubbapalibodhā chinditabbā, chinnapalibodhena sattavidhena uggahakosallena dasavidhena ca manasikārakosallena kammaṭṭhānaṃ nijjaṭaṃ paguṇaṃ kātabbaṃ, kesādīsu ekekakoṭṭhāso "acetano abyākato suñño nissatto"ti manasikātabbo. Tatiyacchakke ca kāmadhātu byāpādadhātu vihiṃsādhātūti tayo akusalavitakkā, nekkhammadhātu abyāpādadhātu avihiṃsādhātūti tayo kusalā dassitā; abyāpādo ca mettāya adhivacanaṃ, avihiṃsā karuṇāya, nekkhammaṃ sabbassāpi kusalassa. Byāpādo hi sattesu uppannoyeva kammapathaṃ bhindati, tathā vihiṃsā. Tasmā yoginā kāmabyāpādavihiṃsāvitakke pahāya, nekkhammaṃ mettaṃ karuṇañca upasampādetvā, sīle patiṭṭhitena dhātumanasikārena cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

### Analysis of the Elements ^2-3-0

#### Classification According to the Discourses ^2-3-1-0

172. There are six elements – the earth element, the water element, the fire element, the air element, the space element, and the consciousness element. ^2-172

173. Therein, what is the earth element? The earth element is twofold: there is internal and there is external. Therein, what is the internal earth element? Whatever internally, in oneself, is hard, solid, hardness, the state of being hard, internal and taken up at birth – namely: head hair, body hair, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones, bone marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, membranes, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, stomach contents, and excrement; or whatever else internally, in oneself, is hard, solid, hardness, the state of being hard, internal and taken up at birth – this is called 'the internal earth element'.
Therein, what is the external earth element? Whatever externally is hard, solid, hardness, the state of being hard, external and not taken up – namely: iron, copper, tin, lead, silver ore, pearl, gems, cat's-eye, conch shell, stone, coral, silver, gold, ruby, emerald, grass, wood, gravel, potsherds, earth, rock, and mountains; or whatever else externally is hard, solid, hardness, the state of being hard, external and not taken up – this is called 'the external earth element'. The internal earth element and the external earth element, gathered and grouped together: this is called "the earth element". ^2-173

174. Therein, what is the water element? The water element is twofold: there is internal and there is external. Therein, what is the internal water element? Whatever internally, in oneself, is water, watery, moisture, moistness, the cohesion of material form, internal and taken up at birth – namely: bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, saliva, mucus, joint fluid, and urine; or whatever else internally, in oneself, is water, watery, moisture, moistness, the cohesion of material form, internal and taken up at birth – this is called 'the internal water element'.
Therein, what is the external water element? Whatever externally is water, watery, moisture, moistness, the cohesion of material form, external and not taken up – namely: the juice of roots, the juice of stems, the juice of bark, the juice of leaves, the juice of flowers, the juice of fruits, milk, curds, ghee, butter, oil, honey, molasses, and the waters of the earth or of the sky; or whatever else externally is water, watery, moisture, moistness, the cohesion of material form, external and not taken up – this is called 'the external water element'. The internal water element and the external water element, gathered and grouped together: this is called "the water element". ^2-174

175. Therein, what is the fire element? The fire element is twofold: there is internal and there is external. Therein, what is the internal fire element? Whatever internally, in oneself, is fire, fiery, heat, heatedness, warmth, warmness, internal and taken up at birth – namely: that by which one is warmed, that by which one ages, that by which one is burned, and that by which what is eaten, drunk, chewed, and tasted is fully digested; or whatever else internally, in oneself, is fire, fiery, heat, heatedness, warmth, warmness, internal and taken up at birth – this is called 'the internal fire element'.
Therein, what is the external fire element? Whatever externally is fire, fiery, heat, heatedness, warmth, warmness, external and not taken up – namely: fire of wood, fire of straw, fire of grass, fire of cow dung, fire of husks, fire of rubbish, lightning fire, the heat of fire, the heat of the sun, the heat of a heap of wood, the heat of a heap of grass, the heat of a heap of grain, the heat of a heap of goods; or whatever else externally is fire, fiery, heat, heatedness, warmth, warmness, external and not taken up – this is called 'the external fire element'. The internal fire element and the external fire element, gathered and grouped together: this is called "the fire element". ^2-175

176. Therein, what is the air element? The air element is twofold: there is internal and there is external. Therein, what is the internal air element? Whatever internally, in oneself, is air, airy, the distension of material form, internal and taken up at birth – namely: winds going upward, winds going downward, winds in the belly, winds in the bowels, winds coursing through the limbs, cutting winds, piercing winds, throbbing winds, breathing in and breathing out; or whatever else internally, in oneself, is air, airy, the distension of material form, internal and taken up at birth – this is called 'the internal air element'.
Therein, what is the external air element? Whatever externally is air, airy, the distension of material form, external and not taken up – namely: east winds, west winds, north winds, south winds, dusty winds, dust-free winds, cold winds, hot winds, gentle winds, strong winds, black winds, high-altitude winds, winds from birds' wings, winds from a supaṇṇa bird, winds from palm-leaf fans, winds from a winnowing fan; or whatever else externally is air, airy, the distension of material form, external and not taken up – this is called 'the external air element'. The internal air element and the external air element, gathered and grouped together: this is called "the air element". ^2-176

177. Therein, what is the space element? The space element is twofold: there is internal and there is external. Therein, what is the internal space element? Whatever internally, in oneself, is space, spatial, a void, a hollow, an opening, a gap, not touched by flesh and blood, internal and taken up at birth – namely: the opening of the ear, the opening of the nose, the door of the mouth, that by which one swallows what is eaten, drunk, chewed, and tasted, that in which what is eaten, drunk, chewed, and tasted stays, and that by which what is eaten, drunk, chewed, and tasted passes out below; or whatever else internally, in oneself, is space, spatial, a void, a hollow, an opening, a gap, not touched by flesh and blood, internal and taken up at birth – this is called 'the internal space element'.
Therein, what is the external space element? Whatever externally is space, spatial, a void, a hollow, an opening, a gap, not touched by the four great elements, external and not taken up – this is called 'the external space element'. The internal space element and the external space element, gathered and grouped together: this is called "the space element". ^2-177

178. Therein, what is the consciousness element? The eye-consciousness element, the ear-consciousness element, the nose-consciousness element, the tongue-consciousness element, the body-consciousness element, and the mind-consciousness element – this is called "the consciousness element".
These are the six elements. ^2-178

179. There are another six elements – the pleasure element, the pain element, the gladness element, the sadness element, the equanimity element, and the ignorance element. ^2-179

180. Therein, what is the pleasure element? Bodily comfort, bodily pleasure; the comfortable, pleasant experience born of body-contact; the comfortable, pleasant feeling born of body-contact – this is called "the pleasure element".
Therein, what is the pain element? Bodily discomfort, bodily pain; the uncomfortable, painful experience born of body-contact; the uncomfortable, painful feeling born of body-contact – this is called "the pain element".
Therein, what is the gladness element? Mental comfort, mental pleasure; the comfortable, pleasant experience born of mind-contact; the comfortable, pleasant feeling born of mind-contact – this is called "the gladness element".
Therein, what is the sadness element? Mental discomfort, mental pain; the uncomfortable, painful experience born of mind-contact; the uncomfortable, painful feeling born of mind-contact – this is called "the sadness element".
Therein, what is the equanimity element? What is mentally neither comfortable nor uncomfortable; the experience born of mind-contact that is neither painful nor pleasant; the feeling born of mind-contact that is neither painful nor pleasant – this is called "the equanimity element".
Therein, what is the ignorance element? Not knowing, not seeing, not comprehending, not awakening, not fully awakening, not penetrating, not grasping, not fathoming, not weighing evenly, not reflecting, not seeing directly; poor discernment, foolishness, lack of clear understanding; delusion, bewilderment, complete confusion; ignorance, the flood of ignorance, the yoke of ignorance, the underlying tendency to ignorance, the obsession of ignorance, the barrier of ignorance; delusion, the root of the unwholesome – this is called "the ignorance element".
These are the six elements. ^2-180
