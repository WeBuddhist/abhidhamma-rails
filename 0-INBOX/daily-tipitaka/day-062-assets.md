## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-summaries.md`)

#### Parāmāsagocchakaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-4-2-9|↑↑↑]]

Parāmāsagocchakanikkhepakathāyaṃ parāmāsānaṃ nikkhepo āsavagocchakanikkhepakathāyaṃ vuttanayeneva veditabbo. Upādiṇṇattike kiñcāpi khīṇāsavassa khandhā paresaṃ upādānassa paccayā honti, maggaphalanibbānāni pana aggahitāni aparāmaṭṭhāni anupādiṇṇāneva – tejussadattā taṇhāmānadiṭṭhivasena gahaṇassa paccayā na hontīti.

[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-dhammasangani-atthakatha#^1-1691]]
[[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/pi-dhammasangani-atthakatha#^1-1692]]

#### Mahantaradukaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-4-2-10|↑↑↑]]

Mahantaradukanikkhepakathāyaṃ kenaci viññeyyadukaniddese cakkhuviññeyyāti cakkhuviññāṇena vijānitabbā. Sesapadesupi eseva nayo. Kenaci viññeyyāti cakkhuviññāṇādīsu kenaci ekena vijānitabbā; kenaci na viññeyyāti teneva na vijānitabbā. Rūpaṃ pana cakkhuviññeyyaṃ saddo na cakkhuviññeyyoti imamatthaṃ gahetvā vīsati dukā vibhattāti veditabbā.

## Practice notes (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-1-practice.md`)

#### Parāmāsagocchakaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-3-2-9|↑↑↑]]

Parāmāsagocchake parāmāso nikkhitto — "idameva saccaṃ moghamañña"nti sabhāvaṃ atikkamitvā aññathā diṭṭhivasena āmasanaṃ. Yaṃ maggaphalanibbānaṃ, taṃ aparāmaṭṭhaṃ, taṇhāmānadiṭṭhīnaṃ agocaro. Tasmā yoginā diṭṭhiparāmāsaṃ "aniccā anattā saṅkhārā"ti passanto pahāya, kismiñci saṅkhāre "etaṃ mama, eso me attā"ti aggahetvā, aparāmaṭṭhena cittena viharitabbaṃ.

#### Mahantaradukaṃ (Nikkhepa)

[[#^toc-3-2-10|↑↑↑]]

Mahantaraduke "kenaci viññeyyā kenaci na viññeyyā"tiādinā vīsati dukā nikkhittā — rūpaṃ cakkhuviññeyyaṃ na sotaviññeyyaṃ, saddo sotaviññeyyo na cakkhuviññeyyoti. Iti dhammā nānāviññāṇehi nānāgocarā. Tasmā yoginā channaṃ dvārānaṃ visaye yathāvato ñatvā, ekekasmiṃ dvāre "idha diṭṭhaṃ, idha suta"nti sallakkhetvā, indriyasaṃvaraṃ pūretvā, saṅkhāre "anattā"ti passitabbaṃ.

## English translation (from `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-Contemporary-English-Abhidhamma/en-dhammasangani-ai.md`)

The unincluded paths, and their fruits, and the unconditioned element—these phenomena are not clung to. ^1-1184
Those phenomena that are associated with them: the aggregate of feeling... up to... the aggregate of consciousness—these phenomena are associated with clinging to views. ^1-1185
Those phenomena that are disassociated from them: the aggregate of feeling... up to... the aggregate of consciousness; and all matter, and the unconditioned element—these phenomena are disassociated from clinging to views. ^1-1186
That very clinging is both clinging and clung to. ^1-1187
Those phenomena that are clung to, excluding those (the clingings), the remaining defilement-associated wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate phenomena belonging to the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere; the aggregate of form... up to... the aggregate of consciousness—these phenomena are clung to but not clinging. ^1-1188
Those phenomena that are disassociated from them: defilement-associated wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate phenomena belonging to the sense sphere, fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere; the aggregate of form... up to... the aggregate of consciousness—these phenomena are disassociated from clinging but clung to. ^1-1189
The unincluded paths, their fruits, and the unconditioned element. These phenomena are disjoined from clinging and not clung to. ^1-1190
The aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, the aggregate of consciousness. These phenomena are with object. ^1-1191
All matter, and the unconditioned element. These phenomena are without object. ^1-1192
Eye-consciousness, ear-consciousness, nose-consciousness, tongue-consciousness, body-consciousness, mind-element, mind-consciousness element. These phenomena are consciousness. ^1-1193
The aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, all matter, and the unconditioned element. These phenomena are not consciousness. ^1-1194
The aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations. These phenomena are mental factors. ^1-1195
Consciousness, all matter, and the unconditioned element. These phenomena are not mental factors. ^1-1196
The aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations. These phenomena are associated with consciousness. ^1-1197
All matter, and the unconditioned element. These phenomena are dissociated from consciousness. Consciousness should not be described as associated with consciousness, nor as dissociated from consciousness. ^1-1198
The feeling aggregate, perception aggregate, and volitional formations aggregate. These states are conjoined with mind. ^1-1199
All matter and the unconditioned element. These states are disjoined from mind. Mind should not be said to be conjoined with mind, nor disjoined from mind. ^1-1200
The feeling aggregate, perception aggregate, volitional formations aggregate; bodily intimation, vocal intimation; and whatever other mind-born, mind-rooted, mind-originated matter there is, such as the visible form base, sound base, smell base, taste base, tangible object base, space element, water element, lightness of matter, malleability of matter, wieldiness of matter, production of matter, continuity of matter, and edible food. These states arise from mind. ^1-1201
Mind, the remaining matter, and the unconditioned element. These states do not arise from mind. ^1-1202
The feeling aggregate, perception aggregate, volitional formations aggregate, bodily intimation, and vocal intimation. These states are co-existent with mind. ^1-1203
Mind, the remaining matter, and the unconditioned element. These states are not co-existent with mind. ^1-1204
The feeling aggregate, perception aggregate, volitional formations aggregate, bodily intimation, and vocal intimation. These states follow the mind. ^1-1205
Mind, the remaining matter, and the unconditioned element. These states do not follow the mind. ^1-1206
The feeling aggregate, perception aggregate, and volitional formations aggregate. These states are conjoined with mind and arise from mind. ^1-1207
Mind, all matter, and the unconditioned element. These states are not conjoined with mind and do not arise from mind. ^1-1208
Which states are conjoined with, arisen from, and co-existent with consciousness? The feeling aggregate, the perception aggregate, the volitional formations aggregate – these states are conjoined with, arisen from, and co-existent with consciousness. ^1-1209
Which states are not conjoined with, arisen from, and co-existent with consciousness? Consciousness, all matter, and the unconditioned element – these states are not conjoined with, arisen from, and co-existent with consciousness. ^1-1210
Which states follow the arising of consciousness and are conjoined with it? The feeling aggregate, the perception aggregate, the volitional formations aggregate – these states follow the arising of consciousness and are conjoined with it. ^1-1211
Which states do not follow the arising of consciousness and are conjoined with it? Consciousness, all matter, and the unconditioned element – these states do not follow the arising of consciousness and are conjoined with it. ^1-1212
The eye-base... up to... the mind-base – these states are internal. ^1-1213
The visible form-base... up to... the phenomena-base – these states are external. ^1-1214
The eye-base... up to... edible food – these states are derived. ^1-1215
