# Day 46 — Impermanence of Matter

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The list of *derived material form* finishes today with its most subtle members: the space element, the lightness, softness, and workability of matter, and the four marks of its life — growth, continuity, decay, and impermanence (*aniccatā*). Matter is not static. It is built up, carried on, worn down, and broken, moment by moment.

Then a new section begins, the threefold sorting (*Tikaniddeso*), which lines up each kind of matter as inside or outside, near or far, coarse or fine. The thread running through both halves is impermanence. Even the body's "decay" and "impermanence" are listed as real, present features of it. Nothing here stands still long enough to be a self.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I see my body being built up and worn down, moment by moment?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ANICCATĀ — Impermanence (of Matter)

**Pronunciation:** *a-NICH-cha-tā* — "cc" is a crisp doubled "ch," the "ā" held long.

**In today's passage:** The text lists *rūpassa aniccatā*, "the impermanence of matter," as one of the subtle kinds of matter — right beside its growth, continuity, and decay. So the breaking-up of the body is not an idea added on later; it is a real, present feature of the body itself.

**Why it matters:** This is the heart of insight. When you watch the body as something always being made and unmade, never holding still, the grip of "this is me, this is mine" loosens on its own. Seen kindly, impermanence does not frighten — it frees. You hold the body more lightly and meet its changes with peace.
