# Day 69 — Mindfulness and Effort

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today closes this whole chapter of pairs. It ends on the tools of daily practice. The verses move through guarding the senses and eating with care. Then comes the heart of it: forgetfulness or mindfulness (*sati*) — losing this moment, or holding it steady.

Around mindfulness stand its partners: clear awareness, calm (*samatha*), insight (*vipassanā*), and right effort. Near the end comes a moving pair. A sense of urgency (*saṃvega*) — waking up to aging, sickness, and death — leads not to fear but to effort. We rouse energy for good states and let go of bad ones. The chapter's last words are "not shrinking back from effort": doing the work with care, without pause, never dropping the load. It is a fitting close. The map ends by pointing back to steady, patient work.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I meet this day with mindfulness — and keep going gently, without dropping the load?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SATI — Mindfulness

**Pronunciation:** *SA-ti* — two light beats.

**In today's passage:** *Sati* means "mindfulness" — remembering to stay present, not letting the moment slip. Today's verses place it right against its opposite, forgetfulness: "letting slip, forgetting." Mindfulness is called a faculty, a power, and a factor of the path — the thread that ties all the other tools together.

**Why it matters:** Mindfulness is not straining or blanking the mind. It is simply not losing what is happening now — this breath, this step, this feeling. The teaching lists it as the guard at every sense door and the ground of both calm and insight. You can begin at any moment, with one thing done fully attended. Each time you notice you have drifted and gently return, that is mindfulness — and that return is the practice itself.
