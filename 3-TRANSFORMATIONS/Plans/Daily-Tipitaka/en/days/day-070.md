# Day 70 — The Great Recap Begins

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today we start the fourth and last part of this book: the Commentary chapter (*Aṭṭhakathākaṇḍa*). The chapters before it named and sorted the mind's states. This one draws out their meaning by showing *where* each state arises.

It opens with the threes (*tika*). Which states are wholesome (*kusala*)? The good in all four planes of mind. Which are unwholesome? The twelve unwholesome types of consciousness. Which are neither — the indeterminate (*abyākata*)? Results, action, matter, and Nibbāna. Then the verses move through more threes, sorting states by whether they come with pleasant, painful, or neutral feeling. They end at the "learner," one still on the path. Note one point of style: when the text says "consciousness," it also means the mental factors that come with it — like a king, who never arrives alone.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When a state arises in me, can I simply see it — wholesome, unwholesome, or neither?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ATTHUDDHĀRA — Drawing Out the Meaning

**Pronunciation:** *at-thud-DHĀ-ra* — "th" as in "thin", stress on the third beat.

**In today's passage:** *Atthuddhāra* means "drawing out the meaning." The whole final chapter is one long atthuddhāra. It takes the plain lists from the earlier chapters and, for each state, shows where it actually arises — in which planes, in which types of consciousness.

**Why it matters:** It is one thing to name a state, another to know where it lives. When you can see where anger or kindness tends to arise in you, you can meet it earlier and more wisely. Drawing out the meaning turns a list you have memorised into something you can use.
