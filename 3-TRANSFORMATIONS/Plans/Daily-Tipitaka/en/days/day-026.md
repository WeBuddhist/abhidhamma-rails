# Day 26 — Where Results Begin

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

For many days the chant has walked through wholesome and unwholesome minds — the minds we *make*. Today that long list closes. Verses 420–430 finish the last of the twelve unwholesome (*akusala*) minds, ending on doubt and restlessness. Then the text turns a corner.

From verse 431 a new kind of mind appears. It is neither wholesome nor unwholesome. It is *abyākata* — indeterminate, "left unscored." This is *vipāka* (resultant): a mind that is simply the ripe fruit of past deeds. The first one named is eye-consciousness of the sense sphere (*kāmāvacara*) — the bare moment of seeing, with a neutral feeling. We do not choose it. It just lands, the way a seed planted long ago finally shows a leaf.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What in me right now is not a fresh choice, but only the result of something I did before?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### VIPĀKA — Result

**Pronunciation:** vi-PAA-ka — three syllables. The middle one is long; stress it.

**In today's passage:** *Vipāka* means the *result* of past action ripening now. The minds from verse 431 on are pure result — eye-consciousness simply receiving a sight. They are *abyākata* (indeterminate): not good, not bad, just fruit. The commentary's word for this is *kammaphala* — "the fruit of deeds." Such a mind makes no new kamma; it only finishes old kamma.

**Why it matters:** Much of a day is *vipāka* — a pleasant sight, a sudden noise, an ache. These are results landing, not choices. Seeing this gives a small freedom: I did not pick the result, but I can pick my response. The result is the old seed; the response plants the next one.
