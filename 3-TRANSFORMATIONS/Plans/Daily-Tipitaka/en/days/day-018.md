# Day 18 — Closing the Form Sphere, Opening the Formless

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today's verses cross two borders. Verses 263–264 hold the *asubha-jhāna* — the foulness-of-the-body practice, taught for minds prone to lust. The first jhāna meets a corpse-image and stays steady. Verse 264 closes the whole *fine-material sphere*. A long chapter ends.

Verses 265–268 open the *formless sphere*. The four *arūpa-jhānas* drop even the kasiṇa disc: infinite space, then infinite consciousness, then no-thing, then a state that is neither perception nor non-perception. Verses 269–276 sum it all up under the *three-plane wholesome* — naming the sense, form, and formless cells side by side, each sorted as low, middle, or high.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What would it take for my wholesome moments to be steadier than they are — more wholehearted, less occasional?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### ARŪPA — Formless

**Pronunciation:** a-RUU-pa — three syllables; "a-" means "without", "rūpa" means form. Stress the middle syllable, which has a long "ū".

**In today's passage:** *Arūpa* is the opposite of *rūpa* (form). Verses 265–268 open the four *arūpa-jhānas*: the mind that has mastered the form jhānas now lets even the kasiṇa disc go, and rests on infinite space, then infinite consciousness, then no-thing, then neither-perception-nor-non-perception. Each step releases one more handhold.

**Why it matters:** You do not need to enter the arūpa jhānas to learn what they teach. They teach a direction — that the mind can keep letting go, past every object, past every image, past every familiar handhold. The wholesome path does not end at a peaceful feeling. It keeps moving toward the simplest, freest thing the mind can be.
