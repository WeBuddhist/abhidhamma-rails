# Day 72 — The Conditioned and the Unconditioned

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today's pairs move through three small clusters. First the roots close: which states are *not* roots? Everything else — the wholesome, the unwholesome, results, action, matter, and Nibbāna.

Then comes a striking pair: the conditioned (*saṅkhata*) and the unconditioned (*asaṅkhata*). Whatever arises from causes is "conditioned," and all of it is impermanent. Only one thing is unconditioned — Nibbāna, which has no cause and does not pass away. Last come the taints (*āsava*): sense-craving, craving to exist, holding views, and not-knowing. These deep habits keep the mind flowing on. The verses show where each arises, then name what is fully free of them: the noble paths, their fruits, and Nibbāna.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I hold one changing thing today a little more lightly, knowing it was only ever conditioned?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SAṄKHATA — The Conditioned

**Pronunciation:** *SANG-kha-ta* — "sang" as in "song", stress on the first beat.

**In today's passage:** *Saṅkhata* means "put together, made from causes." Today's pair sets it against *asaṅkhata*, the "unconditioned." Whatever has a cause is saṅkhata, and everything saṅkhata is impermanent. Only Nibbāna is unconditioned — uncaused, and never passing away.

**Why it matters:** A mood, a worry, a wave of craving — each one is saṅkhata: built from causes, and already on its way out. Seeing a hard state that way loosens its hold. You do not have to fight it or believe it; you can watch it pass, and lean toward the one peace that was never built and cannot break.
