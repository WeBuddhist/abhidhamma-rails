# Day 15 — Eight Kasiṇas and the Bases of Mastery

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today the catalogue widens. Verse 202 closes the *sixteenfold method* on the earth disc. Verse 203 sweeps the same sixteen across all eight *kasiṇas* — the focus discs of earth, water, fire, wind, and the four colours. The point is simple: the method works on any steady object.

Verses 204–205 open a new angle — the *abhibhāyatana* (base of mastery). Here the practitioner does not shut the world out to enter jhāna. They hold an outer form in view and *master it from inside*. Verses 206–213 walk this through four paths and two objects. Verse 214 opens another *eightfold round*.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where in my daily life can I practise holding one thing steady — not blocking the rest, just letting one focus stand?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### KASIṆA — Focus Disc

**Pronunciation:** ka-SI-na — three syllables; the "ṇ" is a soft "n". Stress the second syllable.

**In today's passage:** A *kasiṇa* is a single steady object the practitioner uses to settle the mind for jhāna. There are eight: four elements (earth, water, fire, wind) and four colours (blue, yellow, red, white). Today's verse 203 says the whole sixteenfold method works on any of them — the disc is just the doorway.

**Why it matters:** You do not need a clay disc to learn what a kasiṇa teaches. It teaches single focus — that the mind can rest on one chosen thing and let the rest of the world be. Try it for thirty seconds today on something ordinary: a candle, a leaf, your breath. Holding one thing well is the seed of every deeper practice.
