# Day 84 — Even-Minded

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Perception continues, turned through set after set. Is it wholesome, unwholesome, or neither? Stained or clean? Small, expanded, or measureless? Each verse opens with the same line and then asks a different question of the same thing.

The day's last verse brings a triad worth pausing on: perception can come with joy, with pleasure, or with equanimity (*upekkhā*). Equanimity is the quiet one. It is not coldness or not-caring — it is the mind meeting something without leaning toward it or pushing it away. The commentary notes that this feeling-tone reaches further than the others: it runs through all four planes of mind, right up to the deepest states. Where excitement cannot follow, evenness can.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where could I meet something today without leaning in or pulling back?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### UPEKKHĀ — Equanimity

**Pronunciation:** *u-PEK-khā* — the "kh" is a breathy "k".

**In today's passage:** *Upekkhā* means "equanimity" — an even tone, neither pleasant nor painful. The mind is not pulled either way. Today's last verse sets it beside joy and pleasure. These are the three ways perception can be coloured. The teaching adds that equanimity reaches every plane of mind. The brighter tones do not.

**Why it matters:** Equanimity is easy to mistake for not caring. The difference is attention. Not caring looks away; equanimity keeps looking and stays steady. It lets you sit with something hard without flinching. That is why the deepest states rest on it, and not on excitement.
