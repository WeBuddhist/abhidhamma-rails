# Day 67 — Lightning and Diamond

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the dry Abhidhamma pairs come to a close, and a warmer set begins — the Suttanta pairs, told with images. The change is striking. After hundreds of terse definitions, the text suddenly speaks of lightning and diamond.

The wisdom of the three lower paths is "like lightning": a flash in the dark that shows the road for a moment. The wisdom of the arahant path is "like a diamond": it cuts through everything and never dulls. Around these sit plain pairs anyone can use — foolish and wise, dark and bright, conduct that later torments and conduct that never does. The message is direct: some states darken a life, others brighten it, and clear knowing is what finally cuts the dark away.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where in my life do I need a flash of light — and where the diamond that cuts for good?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### VIJJĀ — True Knowing

**Pronunciation:** *VIJ-jaa* — two beats; a hard "j".

**In today's passage:** The Suttanta pairs open with states "partaking of knowledge" (*vijjā*) and states "partaking of ignorance" (*avijjā*). *Vijjā* is the knowing that sees things as they really are; *avijjā*, ignorance, is the not-knowing at the root of every harmful state. The text then pictures this knowing: like lightning on the three lower paths, and like a diamond on the arahant path.

**Why it matters:** Every dark, tormenting state traces back to not-knowing; every bright, freeing one grows from knowing. This reframes practice simply. You are not just trying to be good — you are trading ignorance for sight. At first the seeing comes in flashes, like lightning in a storm. In the end it cuts like a diamond, and what it cuts never returns.
