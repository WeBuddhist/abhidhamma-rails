# Day 135 — Beyond Form

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses reach the subtlest calm of all — the *arūpāvacara* (formless) meditations, where the mind steps past even the perception of form and rests in vast open space, then in boundless awareness, then in nothingness, then in a stillness "neither perception nor non-perception".

The chapter then closes with a round of questions that sort all these jhānas by whether they are a *hetu* (a root cause) or grow from roots — the Abhidhamma's careful way of showing how every state of mind is conditioned, none of it a self. This is deep territory, and no one needs to reach it to benefit from it. Even hearing it stretches the sense of what a trained mind can become.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I hold even the highest states lightly, as conditioned, not "mine"?*

**v. 632.** Here a monk, on the occasion when he develops the path for rebirth in the immaterial sphere — completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, accompanied by the perception of the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, with the abandoning of happiness… he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption — on that occasion there is contact… there is non-distraction. These things are wholesome. When, because that same immaterial-sphere wholesome action has been done and accumulated, as its result — completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, accompanied by the perception of the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, with the abandoning of happiness… he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption — on that occasion there is the two-factored meditative absorption: equanimity, one-pointedness of mind. This is called "the fourth meditative absorption". The remaining things are associated with the meditative absorption.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ARŪPĀVACARA — The Formless Sphere

**Pronunciation:** *a-rū-pā-va-ca-ra* — "rūpa" means form; the "a-" in front means "without".

**In today’s passage:** *Arūpāvacara* means the formless sphere — the meditative states beyond form, where the mind rests in space, then in infinite awareness, then in nothingness, then in a stillness past ordinary perception. They are the highest reaches of calm the jhāna practice opens.

**Why it matters:** Most of us will not touch these states, and that is fine. Their value here is perspective: they show how far a steadied mind can travel, and how every level is still conditioned, not a self to grasp. Holding even great attainments lightly is itself the practice — less clinging, clearer seeing.
