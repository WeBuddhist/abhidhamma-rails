# Day 135 — Beyond Form

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses reach the subtlest calm of all. These are the *arūpāvacara* — the "formless" states. Here the mind steps past even the sense of form. It rests in open space, then in awareness without edge, then in nothingness, then in a stillness "neither perception nor non-perception".

The chapter then closes with a set of questions. They sort all these jhānas by cause: is a state a *hetu* (a root), or does it grow from roots? This is the Abhidhamma's careful way of showing that every state of mind is caused. None of it is a self. This is deep ground, and no one needs to reach it to gain from it. Even hearing it stretches what we think a trained mind can be.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I hold even the highest states lightly, as passing, not "mine"?*

**v. 632.** Here a monk, on the occasion when he develops the path for rebirth in the immaterial sphere — completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, accompanied by the perception of the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, with the abandoning of happiness… he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption — on that occasion there is contact… there is non-distraction. These things are wholesome. When, because that same immaterial-sphere wholesome action has been done and accumulated, as its result — completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, accompanied by the perception of the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, with the abandoning of happiness… he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption — on that occasion there is the two-factored meditative absorption: equanimity, one-pointedness of mind. This is called "the fourth meditative absorption". The remaining things are associated with the meditative absorption.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ARŪPĀVACARA — The Formless Sphere

**Pronunciation:** *a-rū-pā-va-ca-ra* — "rūpa" means form; the "a-" in front means "without".

**In today’s passage:** *Arūpāvacara* means the formless sphere. These are the states beyond form. The mind rests first in space, then in awareness without limit, then in nothingness, then in a stillness past normal perception. They are the highest reaches of calm the jhāna path opens.

**Why it matters:** Most of us will not touch these states, and that is fine. Their worth here is scale. They show how far a steady mind can go. They also show that every level is still caused, not a self to grasp. Holding even great states lightly is the practice — less clinging, clearer seeing.
