# Day 50 — The Flowing Body

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today finishes the threefold sorting (*Tikaniddeso*). The text closes by lining up the subtle kinds of matter — among them the *continuity* of matter (*santati*), its growth, its decay, and its impermanence. These four name the life of any physical thing: it is built up, carried on, worn down, and broken.

So the body is not a fixed object but a stream — new matter rising and old matter falling away, moment after moment. Seeing this flow is the doorway out of "this is a solid me." It also softens the heart: a stream cannot be owned or held, only watched with care.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I watch my body as a flowing stream, not a fixed thing?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SANTATI — The Continuity of Matter

**Pronunciation:** *SAN-ta-ti* — three short, even beats.

**In today's passage:** *Rūpassa santati*, "the continuity of matter," is listed as one of the subtle kinds of matter, beside its growth, decay, and impermanence. It names the way matter keeps renewing itself — one moment's body giving way to the next, like a flame passed along.

**Why it matters:** Continuity is why the body *seems* to stay the same while it is really always changing. Seeing the stream — new matter rising, old falling away — breaks the spell of a fixed "me." You hold the body as a flow, not a possession. That view brings calm, and a gentler care for a body that is only passing through.
