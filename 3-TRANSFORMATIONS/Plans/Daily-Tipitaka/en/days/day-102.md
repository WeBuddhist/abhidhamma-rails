# Day 102 — Not Craving Alone

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Six verses today, and they look almost identical. Each asks the same four questions and gives a slightly wider answer. What is the origin of suffering? Craving. Then: craving and the remaining defilements. Then: and the remaining unwholesome states. Then: and the three wholesome roots that are still subject to the taints. Each pass takes in more than the one before.

That widening is the whole point. In the discourses, the second truth is craving and stops there. Here the Abhidhamma says craving is not the only thing feeding suffering. Other defilements do it. Unwholesome states do it. Even wholesome states do it, while they are still tangled with the taints — a good deed done for a good rebirth still keeps the wheel turning. That is why this section drops the word "noble" and simply says four truths.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What good thing am I doing for a reason I have not looked at?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SAMUDAYA — Origin

**Pronunciation:** *sa-mu-da-ya* — "sa-mu-DA-ya".

**In today's passage:** *Samudaya* is the origin — what suffering rises up out of. Today the definition is widened six times over. It starts at craving alone, then takes in the other defilements, then the unwholesome states, and finally wholesome states that are still bound up with the taints.

**Why it matters:** It is easy to think one thing is the problem — this craving, that habit. The widening says otherwise. Suffering has many feeders, and some of them look good from outside. Wanting to be seen as generous is still wanting. That is not a reason to stop doing good. It is a reason to be honest about why you are doing it.
