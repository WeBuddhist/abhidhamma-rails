# Day 160 — Four Ways to Go Astray

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The fours continue. Craving can arise in a monk over robes, food, a dwelling, or always-better things. The commentary says the four noble traditions of simple living were taught to end these four. Then come four ways of going astray: through desire, hate, delusion, and fear. There are four distortions, like seeing what changes as lasting. There is ignoble speech, like saying "I saw it" when you did not.

Then the fives begin. They name the five lower and five higher fetters, five darts, and the five hindrances. They name five very heavy deeds, such as killing one's mother or father. The day ends with the dangers of impatience: you are disliked, you make enemies, you make many mistakes, and you die confused.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When I decide something, am I pulled by liking, anger, confusion, or fear?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### AGATI — Going Astray

**Pronunciation:** *a-ga-ti* — three short syllables. Say "A-ga-ti".

**In today’s passage:** *Agati* means going astray — leaving the fair path. The verse names four causes: desire, hate, delusion, and fear. The commentary gives the example of a judge. He favours a friend, punishes an enemy, guesses out of confusion, or gives in to a powerful person out of fear.

**Why it matters:** We all judge and share things out — at work, at home, among friends. Each of these four forces can bend a choice without our noticing. Before deciding, check them one by one. A fair choice made calmly protects you and others.
