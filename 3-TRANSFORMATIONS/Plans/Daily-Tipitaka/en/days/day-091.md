# Day 91 — The Second Turning Ends

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The last long stretch of the Abhidhamma pass. Consciousness goes through the remaining sets — coloured by joy, by pleasure, or by equanimity; past, future, present; inner, outer, both.

Then the range closes on two words: *Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ* — "the Abhidhamma Method." The whole second turning is done. Every one of the five heaps has now been taken apart by strict analysis, each counted one way, two ways, three, four, five, six, and on to ten, through four great rounds. It is the longest single stretch of the chapter, and today it ends. What remains is the third and shortest turning: the question method.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *After all this counting, what do I actually understand better than I did?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ABHIDHAMMABHĀJANĪYA — The Abhidhamma Method

**Pronunciation:** *a-bhi-dham-ma-bhā-ja-NĪ-ya* — long, but plain: *abhi-dhamma* then *bhā-ja-nī-ya*.

**In today's passage:** *Abhidhammabhājanīya* means "the division made in the Abhidhamma way." It is the second of the chapter's three turnings, and today's last line closes it. Where the discourse method used the language of the suttas, this one used strict counting — every heap by one, by two, by three, on to ten.

**Why it matters:** This method is not dry for its own sake. Counting a thing from every side is how you stop taking it as one solid lump. "My feeling" becomes tone, root, plane, faculty, door — and something you thought was you turns out to be a set of parts, each arising by conditions. That loosening is the whole purpose of the counting.
