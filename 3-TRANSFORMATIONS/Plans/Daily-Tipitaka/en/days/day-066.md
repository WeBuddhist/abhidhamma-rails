# Day 66 — The Long Work

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the "back" pairs continue, and they turn to the second way states are given up. Yesterday's three fetters fall in a single clear seeing. But greed, hatred, and delusion do not all go at once. What remains of them is "abandoned by cultivation" (*bhāvanā*) — worn down slowly, over the higher stages of the path.

The rest of today's verses are a great sorting grid. Each state is checked against a pair: does it come with initial thought or not, with rapture or not, with pleasure or with equanimity; does it belong to the sense world, the form world, or the formless. It reads dry, but the point is quiet and steady: every state can be placed, and nothing is left outside the map.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What in me will not change in a flash, but asks for the long, patient work of cultivation?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### BHĀVANĀ — Cultivation

**Pronunciation:** *bhaa-va-NAA* — three beats; the "bh" is a breathy "b".

**In today's passage:** *Bhāvanā* means "cultivation" — literally, "bringing into being." Today's verses mark off the states "abandoned by cultivation": the greed, hatred, and delusion that a single insight does not remove. They thin out only through long, repeated practice on the higher paths.

**Why it matters:** It is easy to hope for one big breakthrough that fixes everything. The teaching is honest: some things break fast, but most change slowly. Cultivation is the patient repeating — of mindfulness, of calm, of clear seeing — that wears a habit down until it cannot return. Knowing this guards against discouragement. Slow change is not failure; it is exactly how the deeper stains are removed.
