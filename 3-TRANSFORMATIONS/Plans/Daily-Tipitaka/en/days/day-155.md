# Day 155 — The Kept Grudge

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The list of ones ends today. It names over-estimating conceit: thinking you have reached a goal you have not reached. The commentary tells of a respected monk who believed for sixty years that he was fully awake. His student asked him to make a charging elephant with his mind-power. When it came at him, he ran in fear — and saw the truth. Then come the conceit "I am" and thoughts that pull us back to family, home, and gain.

Next, the chapter turns to pairs. Anger comes first, and resentment follows it. Contempt hides the good others have done. Then come envy and stinginess, and deceit and treachery. Last are pairs of views about the self and the world: that it lasts forever, or that it is cut off.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Is there an old anger I keep feeding?*

**v. 877.** Therein, what is the conceit of one who is worse, "I am worse"? Here someone is worse on account of birth, or clan, or good family… or on one ground or another, and he regards himself as equal to others; relying on that, he nurses self-belittling. Whatever self-belittling of this kind there is, belittling oneself, the state of belittling oneself, the despising, the looking down, the state of looking down on oneself, self-contempt, self-scorn, self-disparagement — this is called "the conceit of one who is worse, 'I am worse'".

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### UPANĀHA — Resentment

**Pronunciation:** *u-pa-nā-ha* — the third syllable is long. Say "u-pa-NAA-ha".

**In today’s passage:** *Upanāha* is resentment — anger that is kept and tied on. The verse says, "First there is anger, afterwards resentment." The commentary says such a person cannot let go of a grudge. It keeps burning, like a torch of rotten wood.

**Why it matters:** Anger is a flash. Resentment is choosing to keep it alive. Each time we replay the story, we tie the knot again. You do not have to approve of what someone did in order to stop replaying it. Letting the grudge go is first of all a kindness to your own heart.
