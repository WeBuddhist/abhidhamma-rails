# Day 49 — Verbal Intimation

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The threefold sorting (*Tikaniddeso*) keeps going. Today it works through matter by what it is *not* — not the visible-form base, not bodily or verbal intimation, and so on. This sounds dry, but it does something useful. It pulls the body apart into many separate kinds of matter, each with its own narrow role. The lump we call "my body" dissolves into a crowd of small, impersonal parts.

Along the way the text names verbal intimation (*vacīviññatti*) — the way the mind shows itself in speech, just as it shows in gesture. The practice is to watch body and speech as many passing events, none of them a self.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I see "my body" as a crowd of small parts, not one solid thing?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### VACĪVIÑÑATTI — Verbal Intimation

**Pronunciation:** *va-CHEE-viñ-ñat-ti* — "c" is a soft "ch," "ñ" like the "ny" in "canyon."

**In today's passage:** Beside bodily intimation, the text names *vacīviññatti*, verbal intimation — the matter by which a wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral mind shows itself in speech. When the mind moves to speak, it shapes sound into words, and the intention is "made known."

**Why it matters:** Your speech, like your posture, is always reporting your mind. Noticing this is a doorway to right speech. When you feel harsh words forming, you can catch the harsh mind behind them and let them go. Watched gently, speech becomes a place of practice all day — a clear way to do a little less harm.
