# Day 16 — From Small Objects to Boundless

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today the catalogue shifts from small outer forms to boundless ones. Verses 221–222 close the eightfold round on small objects. Verses 223–224 take the same set and sort it by fair or foul colour. Different temperaments are drawn to different shades — the teaching names them so each kind of practitioner is met.

Verses 225–226 open *appamāṇa* — boundless objects of mastery. The mind stops shrinking the object and lets it open wide. Verses 227–231 walk this through four paths. Verses 232–234 cover two object pairs. Verses 235–239 step into another eightfold round, this time on the wide.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Is my attention drawn more to the small and steady, or to the wide and open — and what does that say about the next step?*

**v. 239.** Which phenomena are wholesome? At the time when one develops the path to rebirth in the Form Realm, internally non-percipient of form, one perceives external forms as boundless, thinking: 'Having overcome them, I know and see them,' secluded from sense pleasures... one enters and dwells in the first jhāna with easy practice, slow attainment, from a limited origin, with a boundless object; at that time, there is contact... non-distraction... these phenomena are wholesome.

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### APPAMĀṆA — Boundless

**Pronunciation:** ap-pa-MAA-na — four syllables; double "p" gets a small held beat. Stress the third syllable, which has a long "ā".

**In today's passage:** *Appamāṇa* means without a measure — boundless, immeasurable. Verses 225–226 turn the bases of mastery toward boundless objects: not a small disc, but a wide field. The mind that has steadied on the small can now open to the wide without losing its centre.

**Why it matters:** *Appamāṇa* is the word used later for the four brahmavihāras (loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, equanimity) — those qualities are called boundless because you do not run out of them as you give them away. Today's hint: when you have steadied on something small in your own life, see if you can let one good quality stretch wider. The small comes first; the boundless follows.
