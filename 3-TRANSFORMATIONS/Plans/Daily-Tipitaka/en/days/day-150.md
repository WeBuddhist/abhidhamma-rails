# Day 150 — Many Paths, One Goal

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses cover a lot of ground quickly. They finish the fours, including the four modes of *paṭipadā* (practice): difficult or easy, with understanding that comes slowly or quickly. Then come the short groups — concentration with five factors, the six direct knowledges, the seventy-seven grounds of knowledge tied to dependent origination, wisdom in the paths and fruits, and the nine step-by-step attainments.

The day ends by opening the group of ten with the first of the Buddha's powers. He knows what is possible and what is impossible. It is impossible, for example, for someone who has truly seen the Dhamma to take anything as lasting. The modes of practice carry a kind message for the rest of us. Some people struggle and some find it easy. Some see quickly and some slowly. All of them still lead to the same goal.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I accept the pace of my own practice without comparing it to anyone else’s?*

**v. 801.** (9) Therein, what are the four modes of practice? Wisdom of difficult practice and slow direct knowledge, wisdom of difficult practice and quick direct knowledge, wisdom of easy practice and slow direct knowledge, wisdom of easy practice and quick direct knowledge. *(…)* (d) Therein, what is wisdom of easy practice and quick direct knowledge? The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view that arises in one producing concentration without trouble and without difficulty and knowing that state quickly: this is called "wisdom of easy practice and quick direct knowledge". These are the four modes of practice.

**v. 809.** (1) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the possible as possible and the impossible as impossible? Here the Tathāgata knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any formation as permanent — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some formation as permanent — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any formation as pleasant — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some formation as pleasant — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any thing as self — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some thing as self — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should deprive his mother of life — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should deprive his mother of life — this is possible." *(…)* He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to good bodily conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to good bodily conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to good verbal conduct… one given to good mental conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to good verbal conduct… one given to good mental conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is possible." "Whatever things are causes and conditions of whatever things — that, accordingly, is the possible; whatever things are not causes and not conditions of whatever things — that, accordingly, is the impossible." The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the possible as possible and the impossible as impossible.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PAṬIPADĀ — Mode of Practice

**Pronunciation:** *pa-ṭi-pa-dā* — four syllables. Say "pa-ti-PA-daa".

**In today’s passage:** *Paṭipadā* means the way of practice. Today’s verses name four modes: difficult practice with slow understanding, difficult with quick, easy with slow, and easy with quick. The commentary explains the difficulty as the work of calming the defilements, and the slowness as the time insight takes.

**Why it matters:** It is easy to judge yourself when practice feels hard or slow. The text does not. Hard and slow is still a mode of practice, still on the path. What matters is that you keep walking. Kindness toward your own pace makes it easier to keep going.
