# Day 146 — Ten Powers, Five Senses

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses finish the list of groups. The fives through nines are short: concentration with five factors, the six direct knowledges, and so on. Then comes the group of ten. These are the ten *bala* (powers) of the Buddha — ten kinds of knowledge that let him teach without fear and "roar his lion's roar".

Then the long explanation begins, starting again from the group of one. It looks closely at the five kinds of sense consciousness. Each needs its own base and its own object. Eye-consciousness does not hear; ear-consciousness does not see. They do not arise without attention, and they never overlap. This quiet detail matters. It shows that the mind we take to be one solid thing is really a stream of brief, separate moments.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I notice, just once today, a moment of plain seeing before the mind adds its story?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### BALA — Power

**Pronunciation:** *ba-la* — two short syllables. Say "BA-la".

**In today’s passage:** *Bala* means power. The ten Tathāgata-powers are ten kinds of knowledge. The commentary says they are called powers because they cannot be shaken and because they hold others up. This is the Buddha’s power of knowing, not of body.

**Why it matters:** It is worth pausing on what the Buddha counted as real strength. Not force, not control, but seeing clearly — what is possible, which actions lead where, what each person needs. That kind of strength is open to us too, in small measure, each time we see a little more clearly.
