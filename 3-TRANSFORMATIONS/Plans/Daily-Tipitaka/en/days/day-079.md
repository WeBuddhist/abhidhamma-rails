# Day 79 — Volition Is the Doer

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Perception finishes today and formations (*saṅkhāra*) begin. The last perception verses sort it as inner or outer, coarse or fine, near or far — the same eleven-fold sweep, applied to the part of mind that recognises and labels.

Then comes the fourth heap. The text names it by one word: volition (*cetanā*) — the mind's act of willing. The commentary explains the choice: of all the factors bundled here, volition is the leading one, "because it is obvious in the work of piling up." Name the leader and the rest come with it. This is the heap where karma lives, since volition is what makes an act wholesome or unwholesome. The day's last verse sorts formations as "near" — unwholesome near unwholesome, wholesome near wholesome, like keeping like.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What am I actually willing, underneath what I am doing?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### CETANĀ — Volition (Willing)

**Pronunciation:** *che-ta-NĀ* — "ce" like "che" in "chess"; stress on the last beat.

**In today's passage:** *Cetanā* means "volition" — the mind's act of willing. The aggregate of formations holds many factors, but the text names it by this one. The commentary says volition is chosen because it leads: it does the piling-up, and it is the most obvious of the group. Name it, and the others are included.

**Why it matters:** The Buddha said, "Volition, monks, is what I call karma." Not the outcome, not the appearance — the willing behind the act. This puts practice somewhere you can actually reach: you cannot always control results, but you can turn and look at what you meant.
