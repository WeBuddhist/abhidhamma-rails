# Day 79 — Intention Is the Doer

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Perception finishes today and volition (*saṅkhāra*) begins. The last perception verses sort it as inner or outer, coarse or fine, near or far — the same eleven-fold sweep, applied to the part of mind that recognises and labels.

Then comes the fourth heap. The text names it by one word: intention (*cetanā*). The commentary explains the choice — of all the mental factors bundled here, intention is the leading one, "because it is obvious in the work of piling up." Name the leader and the rest come with it. This is the heap where karma lives: intention is what makes an act wholesome or unwholesome. The day's last verse sorts volitions as "near" — unwholesome near unwholesome, wholesome near wholesome, like keeping like.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What am I actually intending, underneath what I am doing?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### CETANĀ — Intention

**Pronunciation:** *che-ta-NĀ* — "ce" like "che" in "chess"; stress on the last beat.

**In today's passage:** *Cetanā* means "intention" — the mind's act of willing. The volition-heap holds many factors, but the text names it by this one. The commentary says intention is chosen because it leads: it is what does the piling-up, and it is the most obvious of the group. Name it, and the others are included.

**Why it matters:** The Buddha said, "Intention, monks, is what I call karma." Not the outcome, not the appearance — the willing behind the act. This puts practice somewhere you can actually reach: you cannot always control results, but you can turn and look at what you meant.
