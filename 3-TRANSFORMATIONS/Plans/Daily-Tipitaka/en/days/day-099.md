# Day 99 — One Door Opens Wide

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

One long verse today, and it closes the chapter on elements. This is the pairs section: the eighteen elements run against the two-part questions. Is this one a root cause? Does it have a root cause? Is it joined with one, or cut off from one? The answers repeat with small shifts, and the repetition is the point. This is chanted, not studied.

The teachers add one note worth carrying out of it. The five sense-consciousnesses always take small objects — a sight, a sound, whatever happens to be in front of them. The mind-consciousness element is not held to that. It can take a measureless object: the path, its fruit, *nibbāna*. Five doors open onto a narrow street. One opens onto everything. That is why the work happens where it does, and why the same mind that spins all day is also the one that can see.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where am I pointing the one door that opens wide?*

**v. 188.** Seventeen elements are not root causes. The element of mental objects may be a root cause or not. Sixteen elements are without root causes. Two elements may have root causes or not. Sixteen elements are dissociated from root causes. Two elements may be joined with root causes or dissociated from them. Sixteen elements cannot be described as "root causes with root causes" or as "having root causes without being root causes". *(…)* The Analysis of the Elements is finished.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### HETU — Root Cause

**Pronunciation:** *he-tu* — "HAY-too".

**In today's passage:** A *hetu* is a root — one of the six that give an act its moral flavour: greed, hate, and delusion, and their three opposites. Today's verse asks of each element whether it is a root, whether it has one, and whether it is joined with one. Sixteen have none at all. Only the mind-object element and mind-consciousness are ever in the running.

**Why it matters:** A root is not what you do. It is what you do it from. The same words spoken from goodwill and from ill will are two different acts. The list keeps pointing back to the mind, because the mind is the only place a root can sit. Tend the root, and the branches look after themselves.
