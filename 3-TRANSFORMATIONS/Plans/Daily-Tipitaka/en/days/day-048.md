# Day 48 — The World Outside

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The threefold sorting continues, now leaning on the outside (*bahiddhā*) — the matter of the world: the forms you see, the sounds you hear, the things you handle. The text keeps placing this outer matter beside the inner, so they can be seen as one nature. The day closes on visible form "external" — colour and light out in the world.

The teaching is the mirror of yesterday's. Just as the body inside is only matter the mind holds as "mine," the world outside is only matter the mind reaches for as "wanted" or "unwanted." Seen plainly — colour, sound, touch, arising and passing — the world stops being a set of prizes and threats, and the heart grows quiet.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can the world outside be just colour and sound — not things to win or escape?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### BAHIDDHĀ — External (the World Outside)

**Pronunciation:** *ba-HID-dhā* — "ddh" is a doubled, breathy "d," the "ā" held long.

**In today's passage:** *Bahiddhā* means "external" — the matter outside your body: forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and touches in the world. The threefold sorting sets this outer matter next to the inner, so both are seen as the same kind of thing.

**Why it matters:** We chase pleasant outer things and flee unpleasant ones, as if they were prizes and threats. But seen as bare matter — colour, sound, touch, arising and gone — the world loses that pull. This is not coldness; it is freedom. Meeting the outside world as just nature, you grasp less and rest more, and treat what is around you with a steadier, kinder mind.
