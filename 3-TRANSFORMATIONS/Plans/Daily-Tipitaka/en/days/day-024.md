# Day 24 — Three Roots

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Yesterday's verses opened the *power of shamelessness* but stopped before its twin. Today v. 388 brings the *power of fearlessness of wrongdoing* — the second of the pair. Then come the three unwholesome roots themselves: *lobho* (greed), *moho* (delusion), and the standing companion of greedy minds, *abhijjhā* (covetousness). The chant continues through *wrong view*, *shamelessness*, *fearlessness*, *calm*, *exertion*, and *non-distraction*, closing v. 397 with the summary that "these phenomena are unwholesome." V. 398 then sums the whole aggregate of mental formations for this first greed-mind.

From v. 399 the chant opens the second greed-mind — the same as the first but *prompted*, needing a little push. V. 400 opens the third — accompanied not by joy but by equanimity, still joined to wrong view. V. 403 begins its prompted twin.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of these three roots — greed, delusion, wrong view — is most familiar in my own mind today?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### MOHA — Delusion

**Pronunciation:** MO-ha — two syllables; the "h" is breathed lightly. Stress the first.

**In today's passage:** *Moha* is the second unwholesome root the chant names. V. 390 calls it lack of knowledge, lack of vision, no understanding, no awakening, no penetration — and ends with the great word *avijjā*, ignorance. The commentary says *moho sabbākusalānaṃ mūlaṃ* — delusion is the root of every unwholesome mind. Greed and hatred can each be missing. Delusion is in all twelve.

**Why it matters:** Delusion is not stupidity. It is the soft fog that lets the next grab look fine. Naming it gives a practitioner the simplest practice on offer — just see clearly, in one small place today. A little less fog is a little less harm.
