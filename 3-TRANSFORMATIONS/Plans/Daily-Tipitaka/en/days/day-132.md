# Day 132 — Rapture, Born of Letting Go

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The last hindrance to fall is *vicikicchā* (doubt) — the wavering, two-minded state that keeps the mind at a crossroads. Once it is put down, the passage crosses into the first *jhāna* (deep meditative calm): a mind stepped back from sense-wanting, still carrying gentle thought and examination, and filled with *pīti* (rapture) and happiness "born of seclusion".

These verses linger on that rapture and happiness — where they come from and how they feel in the body. The point is not exotic. When the mind stops grasping, a natural gladness rises that pleasure-seeking never delivers. Joy here is a by-product of letting go, not of getting more.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Have I felt the quiet gladness that comes from wanting less, even for a moment?*

**v. 587.** "And experiences happiness with the body": therein, what is happiness? ## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PĪTI — Rapture

**Pronunciation:** *pī-ti* — a long "ee", then a light "ti".

**In today’s passage:** *Pīti* is rapture — an uplifting gladness that fills the mind and even the body as it settles. In the first jhāna it arises "born of seclusion", meaning it comes from stepping back from sense-wanting, not from feeding it.

**Why it matters:** We usually chase good feeling by getting something. Today’s text points the other way: a deeper gladness shows up when the grasping stops. Knowing this changes how you meet a craving — you can let it pass, trusting that calm has its own reward. A little less chasing today, a little more ease.
