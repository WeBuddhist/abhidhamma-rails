# Day 138 — Compassion, Analysed

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The chapter closes by looking closely at *karuṇā* (compassion) and its companions. Compassion is defined as the heart's response to a being who is suffering — not pity from above, but a warm wish for their pain to ease. Sympathetic joy and equanimity are treated alongside it.

Then come the sorting questions: which of these states is a *hetu* (a root cause), and which grow from roots? Loving-kindness, the text notes, stands as a root, while the others rest on roots. This is the Abhidhamma's quiet reminder that even the noblest feelings are conditioned events, arising from causes — not a fixed self being good. That takes the pride out of kindness and leaves the kindness itself.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When I meet someone's difficulty today, can I meet it with warmth rather than turning away?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KARUṆĀ — Compassion

**Pronunciation:** *ka-ru-ṇā* — a short "ka", a long "roo", then "naa".

**In today’s passage:** *Karuṇā* is compassion — the heart moved by another’s suffering and wishing it to ease. The Abhidhamma defines it precisely and then asks how it is caused, showing it as a conditioned state, not a personal virtue to be proud of.

**Why it matters:** Seeing compassion as something that arises from causes is freeing. You do not have to "be a compassionate person". You just meet suffering with warmth, again and again, and the quality grows. Each small turn toward instead of away is more good done, and a mind a little more open.
