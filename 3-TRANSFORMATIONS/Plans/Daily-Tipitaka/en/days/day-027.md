# Day 27 — The Five Bare Senses

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Yesterday the chant entered resultant (*vipāka*) minds — minds that are the ripe fruit of past good deeds, not fresh choices. Today it stays there and counts them out: the five sense consciousnesses (*pañcaviññāṇa*).

These are the plainest minds the book describes. Eye-consciousness only sees. Ear-consciousness only hears. Nose, tongue, and body do the same for smell, taste, and touch. Each is just a bare moment of knowing one thing through one door. The chant gives the factors of seeing (vv. 433–442), then runs the other four, ending with body-consciousness (vv. 443–454). Four of them carry a neutral feeling — equanimity (*upekkhā*). Body-consciousness alone carries *sukha* (pleasure), because touch strikes harder than the others. None of them holds greed, hate, or wisdom; they are simply good kamma meeting a welcome object.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I notice the bare seeing or hearing, before my mind adds a story to it?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### CAKKHUVIÑÑĀṆA — Eye-consciousness

**Pronunciation:** chak-khu-vin-NYAA-na — five syllables. The "c" is like the "ch" in *church*; stress the long fourth syllable.

**In today's passage:** *Cakkhuviññāṇa* is the bare moment of seeing — knowing a colour or shape through the eye, and nothing more. It is the first of the five sense consciousnesses the chant names today. The commentary says it only sees a visible object; it does not like, judge, or name it. That work comes later, in other minds.

**Why it matters:** Almost all our trouble starts *after* the bare sense moment, when the mind piles on liking, story, and reaction. Catching the plain seeing or hearing — even once — shows that the raw moment is clean. The craving and the upset are extra. A little space there is a little less harm.
