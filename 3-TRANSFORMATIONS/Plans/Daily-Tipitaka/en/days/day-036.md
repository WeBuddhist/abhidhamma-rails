# Day 36 — The Mind That Merely Acts

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

The unwholesome results finish their round today. After the five senses, a mind-element (*manodhātu*) receives the unwished-for object. Then a mind-consciousness element (*manoviññāṇadhātu*) looks into it, evenly. Quiet clerks — they do for painful objects just what their twins did for pleasant ones.

Then something new enters: functional (*kiriya*) minds — minds that merely act. They are neither wholesome nor unwholesome, and they plant no kamma. The commentary's image: a flower on a tree whose root is cut. It blooms, but no fruit follows. The first such mind simply turns attention toward each sight or sound. Another is rare and dear — the joyful one by which, we are told, the Buddha smiled.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *How much of my day is bare function — and where do I add the seed?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### KIRIYA — Functional

**Pronunciation:** ki-RI-ya — three syllables. Say "key-ree-ya."

**In today's passage:** A *kiriya* mind is functional — mere doing. It plants no kamma and bears no result. The commentary's image is exact: a flower on a cut-root tree, blooming without fruit. The mind that turns toward every object is kiriya; so is the smile of an arahant (a fully freed one) — joy that plants nothing.

**Why it matters:** Almost everything we do plants seeds. The functional minds give us a glimpse of action without grasping — response without residue. We cannot live there yet, but we can lean: act, release, and let the moment close clean.
