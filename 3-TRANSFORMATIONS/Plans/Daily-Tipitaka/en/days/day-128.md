# Day 128 — The Noble Eightfold Path

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

This chapter maps the path itself — the eight factors the Buddha called the way out of suffering: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. Today the text simply lists them, then begins to define each one in turn.

The commentary makes one quiet point worth carrying. The path is called *ariya* (noble), and even where a shorter, fivefold list is given, it is still the same noble path — the three factors of speech and action simply fold in when there is nothing wrong to give up. In plain terms, this is one road with eight parts, not eight separate roads. Walk any part with care and the others come with it.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of the eight feels most alive in me right now — and which have I been leaving out?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### MAGGAṄGA — Path Factor

**Pronunciation:** *mag-gaṅ-ga* — hold the "gg" a beat, as in "big game"; the "ṅ" is the "ng" in "sing".

**In today’s passage:** A *maggaṅga* is one factor, or "limb", of the path. The text names eight of them, from right view to right concentration. Each is a real quality you can grow — a way of seeing, choosing, speaking, acting, and steadying the mind — not just an idea to agree with.

**Why it matters:** Seeing the path as eight linked factors takes the pressure off. You do not have to perfect all eight at once. Strengthen the one that is weakest today — a kinder word, a clearer intention — and you have moved the whole path forward a little.
