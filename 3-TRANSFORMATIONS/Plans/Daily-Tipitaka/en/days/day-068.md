# Day 68 — Good Friends

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The Suttanta pairs continue, and today they read like a field guide to a healthy practice. First come two quiet guardians: moral shame (*hiri*), a heartfelt "I would not do that," and fear of wrongdoing (*ottappa*), a clear sense of the harm it would bring. Their absence — shamelessness and recklessness — is named as foolish and dark.

Then the text turns outward. It praises good friendship (*kalyāṇamittatā*): keeping company with people who are faithful, virtuous, learned, generous, and wise. It sets this against bad friendship, and adds many "skills" — skill in one's own faults, in meditative attainments, in the elements, in dependent origination. Woven in are uprightness, gentleness, patience, and kind speech. The drift is warm: awakening grows in good company and gentle habits, not in harshness.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Who are the friends that make me more faithful, kind, and wise — and am I becoming that friend for others?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KALYĀṆAMITTATĀ — Good Friendship

**Pronunciation:** *kal-YAA-na-mit-ta-taa* — the "ṇ" is a soft "n".

**In today's passage:** *Kalyāṇamittatā* means "good friendship" — the choice to keep close to people who are faithful, virtuous, learned, generous, and wise. Today's verses define it plainly and set it against bad friendship: time given to the faithless, unkind, and unwise.

**Why it matters:** The Buddha once said good friendship is not half of the holy life — it is the whole of it. Who we spend time with shapes what we value, notice, and become. This is practice you can do today, without sitting down to meditate: move a little closer to people who bring out your kindness and clarity, and a little further from what pulls you down. The company you keep is quietly making your mind.
