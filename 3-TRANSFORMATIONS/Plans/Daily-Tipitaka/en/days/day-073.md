# Day 73 — Families of Fetters

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the chapter runs through five "clusters" (*gocchaka*) — five families of states that bind the mind. This is how the whole commentary is built: not one defilement at a time, but in bunches, the way the Buddha once taught it to a struggling monk.

First come the ten fetters (*saṃyojana*), led by sense-craving and ill-will. Then the four knots (*gantha*) that tie the mind to the body. Then the four floods (*ogha*) that sweep beings along, and the four yokes (*yoga*) that harness them to more becoming. Last come the hindrances (*nīvaraṇa*) — the five that cover the mind and block calm and clear seeing. Each verse names which states these arise together with.

Seeing them in families is the point. A defilement rarely comes alone; catch one, and you often find its relatives close by.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When one hard state shows up, can I notice what it brought along with it?*

Ignorance arises in all unwholesome consciousnesses – these phenomena are fetters.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### GOCCHAKA — Cluster

**Pronunciation:** *GOCH-cha-ka* — "cch" like the "tch" in "catch", stress on the first beat.

**In today's passage:** *Gocchaka* means a "cluster" or "bunch," like a bunch of flowers on one stem. The Buddha sorted the mind's defilements into clusters — fetters, knots, floods, yokes, hindrances. Today's verses walk through five of these families in a row.

**Why it matters:** Defilements travel in families. When ill-will shows up, restlessness and not-knowing are often right behind it. Watching for the cluster, not just the one state in front of you, makes you harder to catch off guard — and shows you that loosening a single knot can begin to free the whole bunch.
