# Day 47 — Inside and Outside

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today sits fully in the threefold sorting (*Tikaniddeso*), which lines up every kind of matter as inside or outside, near or far, coarse or fine. The first and most useful split is inside and outside — your own body (*ajjhatta*) and the matter of the world and other beings (*bahiddhā*).

The commentary makes a sharp point. Whatever is taken up as "I" or "mine" is counted as *grasped*. The body is not grasped because it truly belongs to you; it is "yours" only because the mind keeps holding it. Today's practice is to look at your own form as just matter — the same stuff as everything outside — and to loosen that holding.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Is my body really "mine," or is it just matter the mind keeps holding?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### AJJHATTA — Internal (Your Own Body)

**Pronunciation:** *AJ-jhat-ta* — "jjh" is a doubled, breathy "j."

**In today's passage:** *Ajjhatta* means "internal" — the matter of your own body, set against *bahiddhā*, the matter outside. The threefold sorting keeps pairing the two so you can see them side by side.

**Why it matters:** We defend the inside body as "me" and treat the outside world as "other." But the commentary says the body is called "grasped" only because the mind takes it up as "mine." When you look at your own form as plain matter — the same as everything outside — the wall between "self" and "world" thins. Holding the body more lightly, you carry less fear and less pride.
