# Day 28 — The Mind Takes It In

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

The five bare senses only know their object for a single instant. Then the object has to be passed along. Today the chant names the two resultant minds that do the next jobs.

First comes the mind-element (*manodhātu*). Its task is *sampaṭicchana* — *receiving*. It catches the sight or sound the eye or ear just had, the way a catcher takes a thrown ball. It carries a neutral feeling, equanimity (*upekkhā*). Then comes the mind-consciousness element (*manoviññāṇadhātu*) accompanied by joy (*somanassa*). Its task is *santīraṇa* — *investigating*, a quick look at what was received. The joy version fires when the object is strongly pleasant. All of this is still *vipāka* — result, not choice: a small relay team handing one experience down the line, faster than we can notice.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Between sensing something and reacting to it, how many quiet steps does my mind take?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### SAMPAṬICCHANA — Receiving

**Pronunciation:** sam-pa-TICH-cha-na — five syllables. Stress the middle one; the "ch" is doubled and crisp.

**In today's passage:** *Sampaṭicchana* is the *receiving* of an object. After eye-consciousness sees, it stops; the mind-element (*manodhātu*) then takes the object in so the mind can work with it. The commentary calls this its one job: catch what the sense just had. It is a resultant mind — it makes no new kamma, it only receives.

**Why it matters:** We tend to feel that seeing and reacting are one event. Today shows they are not. There are quiet hand-offs in between — receiving, then investigating — each a separate tiny mind. Knowing the mind moves in steps makes a fast reaction feel a little less solid, and a little easier to interrupt.
