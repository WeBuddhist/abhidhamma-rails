# Day 162 — Let Me Lie Down

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses move quickly. The sixes end with six views about the self, such as "There is a self for me" and "There is no self for me." The commentary says both grip the self too tightly — as lasting forever, or as cut off.

Then come the sevens: seven *anusaya* (underlying tendencies), seven fetters, seven kinds of conceit, and seven views that the self is destroyed at death. The eights begin with eight grounds of defilement. Then come eight excuses for laziness. A monk has work to do, or has done it. He has a journey ahead, or has just travelled. He got too little food, or too much. He feels a little sick, or is just getting better. Each time he thinks, "Let me lie down."

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which excuse do I use most to put off what matters?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KUSĪTAVATTHU — Ground of Laziness

**Pronunciation:** *ku-sī-ta-vat-thu* — five syllables, the second long. Say "ku-SEE-ta-vat-thu".

**In today’s passage:** A *kusītavatthu* is a ground of laziness — a reason the mind gives for giving up. The verse lists eight, and each one ends the same way: "Well then, let me lie down." The person makes no effort to reach what is not yet reached.

**Why it matters:** These excuses sound sensible, and some rest is wise. But notice how any situation can become a reason to stop. Before or after a task, tired or rested, the mind finds an excuse. Seeing the pattern helps. Sometimes the kind answer to "let me lie down" is "five minutes more."

**953.** Tattha katamāni aṭṭha kusītavatthūni? *(…)* (Ja) puna caparaṃ bhikkhu gilānā vuṭṭhito [gilānavuṭṭhito (saddanībhi) a. ni. 6.16 pāḷiyā ṭīkā passitabbā] hoti aciravuṭṭhito gelaññā.
