# Day 62 — Misapprehension

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today finishes the group on misapprehension (*parāmāsa*) — the grip of "only this is true, all else is wrong" — and notes what lies beyond its reach: the path, its fruits, and the unconditioned are "not clung to," untouched by craving, conceit, or view.

Then the chapter turns to a large set of plain pairs, sorting states by which sense can know them: what the eye can cognize but the ear cannot, and so on. After the heavy defilement-lists, this is a cooling change of pace — a reminder that experience arrives through six separate doors, each with its own field. The thread joining the two halves: loosen the mind's grip on views, and meet each sense-door as it is.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Am I holding any view so tightly that I've stopped looking freshly?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PARĀMĀSA — Misapprehension

**Pronunciation:** *pa-RĀ-mā-sa* — "ā" held long.

**In today's passage:** *Parāmāsa* is the mind's habit of "misapprehending" — reaching past how a thing actually is and grasping it through a fixed view, in the form "only this is true, all else is false." The chapter notes that the path and its fruit are *aparāmaṭṭha* — beyond this grip entirely.

**Why it matters:** Misapprehension is subtler than obvious craving or anger. It is the quiet certainty that our take on things is simply *the truth*. Held loosely, a view is a useful tool; held as misapprehension, it closes the mind and breeds conflict. Seeing your own views as views — not as reality itself — keeps the heart open and the seeing fresh.
