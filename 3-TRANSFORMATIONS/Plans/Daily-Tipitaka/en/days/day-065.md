# Day 65 — The Three Fetters

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The long list of defilements (the states that stain the mind) ends today, and a new set of pairs begins. These are the "back" pairs — the text now sorts every state by how it is given up.

The first sorting is the most hopeful one. Some states are "abandoned by seeing" — cut the moment a person first sees the truth clearly. There are three, called the three fetters (*saṃyojana*): identity view (a fixed sense of self), doubt about the path, and clinging to rites and rituals as the way to be pure. The text defines each one closely. These three are the first chains to fall away at stream-entry, the first step of awakening.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of these three still has a hold on me — a fixed sense of self, nagging doubt, or trust in mere ritual?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SAṂYOJANA — Fetter

**Pronunciation:** *sang-YOH-ja-na* — four beats, soft "g".

**In today's passage:** A *saṃyojana* is a "fetter" — a chain that ties us to more rebirth. Today's verses name the first three: identity view (the sense of a fixed self), doubt (*vicikicchā*), and clinging to rites and rituals (*sīlabbataparāmāsa*). These three are "abandoned by seeing" — they break at the very first stage of awakening.

**Why it matters:** These are not exotic faults. They are the everyday self-story, the quiet doubt, and the hope that a ritual can do the inner work for us. The teaching says one clear seeing of the truth snaps all three at once. Knowing what falls first tells you where the path begins — and that the first chains are closer to breaking than they seem.
