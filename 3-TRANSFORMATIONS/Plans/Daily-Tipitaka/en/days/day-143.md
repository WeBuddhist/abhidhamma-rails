# Day 143 — Knowing What You Know

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The chapter ends today. The verses finish the results of unwholesome action. Then they turn to *kiriya* (functional) states — the minds of one who is fully awake, which act without planting new results. The text then shows where the four knowledges arise: in the good minds of those still training, and in the functional minds of those beyond training.

The last questions sort the knowledges by their objects. Knowledge of language, for example, takes only sound, and only in the present. The commentary tells a lively story about the fourth knowledge, *paṭibhāna* (ready understanding). A learned monk with a weak voice knew far more than a gifted speaker who knew little. Yet he could not move a crowd the way the speaker could. Knowing about a skill and having it are not the same.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where do I confuse knowing about something with being able to do it?*

## 4. 💡 Reading for Meaning

**Verses 742–750.** *(Full text rendered in the WeBuddhist app from the verse library; the first and last verses of the range are printed below so the plan-importer can find the right entries.)*

**v. 742.** There are three analytical knowledges: the analytical knowledge of meaning, the analytical knowledge of language, the analytical knowledge of ready understanding. *(…)* Which things are neutral? On the occasion when, because unwholesome action has been done and accumulated, the resultant mind element has arisen, accompanied by equanimity, with a sight as its object… or a tangible thing as its object… the resultant mind-consciousness element has arisen, accompanied by equanimity, with a sight as its object… or a mental object as its object, or based on whatever it may be — on that occasion there is contact, there is feeling, there is perception, there is intention, there is mind, there is thought, there is examination, there is equanimity, there is one-pointedness of mind, there is the mind faculty, there is the equanimity faculty, there is the life faculty; and whatever other immaterial, dependently arisen things there are on that occasion. These things are neutral. Knowledge with regard to these things is the analytical knowledge of meaning. The language by which those things are made known — knowledge with regard to the expression of the language of those principles is the analytical knowledge of language. The knowledge by which one knows those knowledges — "these knowledges illuminate this meaning" — knowledge with regard to knowledges is the analytical knowledge of ready understanding.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PAṬIBHĀNA — Ready Understanding

**Pronunciation:** *pa-ṭi-bhā-na* — four syllables. Say "pa-ti-BHAA-na".

**In today’s passage:** *Paṭibhāna* is ready understanding — knowledge that knows the other knowledges and sees what each one is for. The commentary notes its limit. It knows the work of the others, but it cannot do that work itself, like a learned teacher without a strong voice.

**Why it matters:** This is a gentle check on pride. Knowing the name of a quality is not the same as living it. Understanding patience does not make you patient. Honest about that gap, you keep practising instead of stopping at the idea — less fooling yourself, clearer seeing.
