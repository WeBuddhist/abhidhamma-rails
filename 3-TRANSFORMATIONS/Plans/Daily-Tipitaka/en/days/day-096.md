# Day 96 — Six Elements, No Owner

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

A new chapter opens today. The last one sorted experience into twelve bases (*āyatana*). This one sorts it into elements (*dhātu*). An element is a thing holding its own nature, and no more than that. The old teachers gloss the word bluntly: *dhātu* is a name for what has no life in it.

Three sets of six follow. First the six that make a body: earth, water, fire, air, space, and consciousness. Each is opened out into what is inside you and what is outside. Then six feelings — pleasure, pain, gladness, sadness, equanimity, and not-knowing. Last, six kinds of thought: sense desire, ill will, and cruelty, set against renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness. The Buddha taught the elements to loosen one old habit — the sense that a self sits somewhere inside, owning all of it. Take the body apart into parts, and no owner turns up.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *If I take myself apart, who is left holding the pieces?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### DHĀTU — Element

**Pronunciation:** *dhā-tu* — "DHAA-too"; the "dh" is a "d" with a puff of breath behind it.

**In today's passage:** *Dhātu* means element. Today it names the six that make up a body, and two more sets of six after that. The teachers give the word a sharp reading: it is a name for what has no life of its own. A thing holds its nature, and that is all it does. Nobody is running it.

**Why it matters:** We carry a quiet belief that a self sits behind our eyes, watching. The elements are a way to test that. Look for the owner in the hardness, the warmth, the breath, the knowing — and each time you find only the thing itself. Less of an owner means less to defend.
