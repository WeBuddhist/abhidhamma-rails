# Day 159 — Looking for Holes

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The threes close today. One set names three things that keep us from the good: not wanting to see noble people, not wanting to hear the true teaching, and a mind that looks for faults. The commentary says the fault-finder is like a tailor who spreads out cloth and looks only for the holes. Other sets join forgetfulness with a scattered mind. Unwise attention travels with the wrong path. Unwise attention means taking what changes as lasting, and what is painful as pleasant.

Then the fours begin. First come the four taints: sense pleasure, existence, views, and ignorance. Next come the four kinds of *upādāna* (clinging): to sense pleasures, to views, to rules and rituals, and to a belief in a self.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Do I see the good in others as clearly as their faults?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### UPĀRAMBHA — Fault-finding

**Pronunciation:** *u-pā-ram-bha* — four syllables, the second long. Say "u-PAA-ram-bha".

**In today’s passage:** *Upārambha* is fault-finding — searching for other people's weak points. The verse calls it contempt, scorn, and seeking out holes. The commentary says such a person rubs out all the good in others and stands only on their faults.

**Why it matters:** A fault-finding mind feels clever, but it closes the door to learning. It cannot hear a teaching while it is busy judging the teacher. Try a small exercise today. When you notice a fault in someone, also name one good quality they have.
