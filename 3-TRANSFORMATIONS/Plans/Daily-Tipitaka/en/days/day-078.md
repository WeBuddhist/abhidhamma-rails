# Day 78 — A New Book: The Five Heaps

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today we open the second book of the Abhidhamma, the *Vibhaṅga* — "The Analysis." Where the first book sorted states of mind, this one takes eighteen big topics and takes each apart. The first is the five aggregates (*khandha*).

*Khandha* means a heap or mass. The teaching says: what you call "me" is five heaps — body (*rūpa*), feeling (*vedanā*), perception (*saññā*), volition (*saṅkhāra*), and consciousness (*viññāṇa*). Today's verses walk through the first three. Each is sorted eleven ways: past, future, present; inner, outer; coarse, fine; low, high; far, near. Nothing is left out. The commentary explains why "heap": as with a great mass of water you cannot count the drops, so no one drop of you is the self. There are only heaps.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *If I am five heaps and no owner, what is left to defend?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KHANDHA — Heap (Aggregate)

**Pronunciation:** *KHAN-dha* — the "kh" is a breathy "k"; two beats.

**In today's passage:** *Khandha* means "heap" or "mass." The commentary gives the image of a great body of water: you cannot count its drops, so it is simply called a mass. In the same way, "I" is five heaps piled together — body, feeling, perception, volition, consciousness.

**Why it matters:** A heap has no owner inside it. Seeing yourself as five heaps is not bleak; it is a relief. There is no fixed self to protect, prop up, or take offence for. This whole book was taught to loosen that one idea — and today is the first loosening.
