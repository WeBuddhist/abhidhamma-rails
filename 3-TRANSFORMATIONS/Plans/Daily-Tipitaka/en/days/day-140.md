# Day 140 — The Heart of a Precept

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses finish the chapter on the five training rules. They look at each rule in two more ways. First the precept is described as *cetanā* (volition), the intention behind holding back. Then it is described as that intention together with all the other good qualities that arise with it. The text also asks which things count as "training" at all. Its answer is every wholesome state of mind.

The commentary makes the key point plain. The precept in the strictest sense is *virati* (abstinence) — the actual turning away from harm at the moment it is possible. The chapter closes by noting that precepts are kept here and now, toward something real in front of you. Keeping them is never only a plan for later.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When the chance to cause harm comes today, will I notice the moment to turn away?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### VIRATI — Abstinence

**Pronunciation:** *vi-ra-ti* — three short syllables. Say "vi-RA-ti".

**In today’s passage:** *Virati* is abstinence — the mind’s act of turning away from harm. The commentary calls it the precept in the strictest sense. The intention and the other good states around it are the precept in a looser sense, because they come with it.

**Why it matters:** Abstinence happens in a moment, not in a promise. It is the small inner "no" when the chance to lie, take, or strike is right in front of you. Each time you notice that moment and turn away, you do a little less harm, and the next time comes a little easier.
