# Day 19 — The Path Becomes Whole

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today the chanting crosses the biggest border yet. For weeks the verses walked through wholesome minds still inside the wheel of rebirth. Sense-sphere, fine-material, formless — all still bound to a place. This morning the verses turn to the *lokuttara* — the supramundane, "above the world." It is the moment a long-practising mind first touches the path that leads out.

V. 277 is one long opening sentence. A practitioner cultivates supramundane jhāna — for the abandoning of wrong views and for the attainment of the first stage, stream-entry. The list of factors inside that moment grows. The five path factors of earlier days are joined by three more — Right Speech, Right Action, and Right Livelihood — finishing the eightfold path. A new faculty appears too: *anaññātaññassāmītindriya*, "I shall know what is yet unknown." Verses 278–303 then define each factor one by one, ending at Right Mindfulness.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where in my own life is the eightfold path still seven, waiting for one more limb to grow back?*

## 4. Reading for Meaning

**Verses 277–303.** *(Full text rendered in the WeBuddhist app from the verse library; the first and last verses of the range are printed below so the plan-importer can find the right entries.)*

**v. 277.** Which states are wholesome? When one develops the supramundane jhāna, leading out (of saṃsāra), leading to the cessation of accumulation (of kamma), for the abandonment of wrong views, for the attainment of the first stage (of enlightenment), quite secluded from sensual desires…pe… one enters and abides in the first jhāna, with painful practice and slow direct knowledge. At that time, contact, feeling, perception, volition, consciousness, initial application, sustained application, zest, pleasure, one-pointedness of mind, the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, mind, joy, life, and the faculty "I shall know the unknown", right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration…pe… non-distraction arise; or whatever other dependently arisen formless states are present at that time — these states are wholesome.

…

**v. 303.** What on that occasion is Right Mindfulness? At that time, mindfulness, recollection, repeated recollection, remembering, holding in mind, non-floating away from the object, non-forgetfulness; mindfulness, the mindfulness faculty, the power of mindfulness, Right Mindfulness, the enlightenment factor of mindfulness, a path factor, included in the path — this is the Right Mindfulness that there then is.

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### MAGGA — Path

**Pronunciation:** MAGG-a — two syllables; the double "g" is held a touch longer than a single "g".

**In today's passage:** *Magga* is the path that leads out of suffering. Today the path becomes whole. Until now the chant carried only five of the eight path factors. Today the missing three appear: Right Speech (v. 299), Right Action (v. 300), and Right Livelihood (v. 301). The chant ends at Right Mindfulness (v. 303), with Right Concentration still to come tomorrow.

**Why it matters:** A path is something you walk. The Buddha did not draw a finish line in the sky. He named eight ordinary things a person can do — and said that doing them is the path. Less harm in your speech, less harm in your hands, less harm in how you earn your living. These are the path itself, not the warm-up.
