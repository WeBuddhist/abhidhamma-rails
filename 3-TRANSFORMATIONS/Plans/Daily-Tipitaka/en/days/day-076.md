# Day 76 — What Can Be Stained

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The defilements finish today, and the book's last set of pairs begins. First a fine distinction: which states are defilements *and* can be defiled? Only the defilements themselves. Which are open to defilement but are not defilements? Almost everything else worldly — even wholesome states, and all matter. This is what "liable to defilement" (*saṃkilesika*) means: a state can be good and still be touched by the stain of the round.

Then the verses reach the back pairs (*piṭṭhi-duka*), the final sortings. Among them come the feeling pairs — whether a state is carried along by rapture (*pīti*), by happiness, or by calm. Today's last verse lists the states *not* carried by rapture, quietly turning the book toward its close.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Even in my good moments, can I see what still leaves them open to being stained?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SAṂKILESIKA — Subject to Defilement

**Pronunciation:** *sang-ki-LE-si-ka* — five light beats, stress on the middle.

**In today's passage:** *Saṃkilesika* means "liable to defilement" — able to be stained by the defilements, whether or not it is a defilement itself. Today's verses split the two apart: the defilements are both; most worldly states, even good ones, can be stained but are not themselves defilements.

**Why it matters:** This is a humbling and useful line to hold. A kind, calm, worldly mind is still a worldly mind — it can be pulled back into craving or conceit. Knowing that keeps you honest and gentle with yourself. Only the noble path stands fully beyond stain; everything short of it is worth guarding with care.
