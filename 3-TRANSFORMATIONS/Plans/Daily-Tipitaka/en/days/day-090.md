# Day 90 — Turning It the Other Way

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today's first verse ends a round. "The dyad-rooted section" — the pass that took the pairs and folded the threes into them — is finished for consciousness. What follows is the second of four great rounds: the triad-rooted pass (*tikamūlaka*), which takes the threes first and folds the pairs into them instead.

Same material, opposite grip. The commentary is direct about why the Buddha taught it more than one way: different listeners take hold of it differently, so he showed it in the shape each could grasp. The day runs on through the counts and ends on the thinking triad — states with applied and sustained thought, with only the latter, or with neither.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When something will not go in one way, can I turn it and try the other?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### TIKAMŪLAKA — The Triad-Rooted Pass

**Pronunciation:** *ti-ka-MŪ-la-ka* — five light beats.

**In today's passage:** *Tikamūlaka* means "rooted in the threes." It is the second of four great passes through this material. The first, *dukamūlaka*, started from the pairs and folded the threes in. This one starts from the threes and folds the pairs in. Today's opening verse closes the first pass and the second begins.

**Why it matters:** The Buddha did not repeat himself for the sake of length. The commentary says he taught the same ground in several shapes because people take hold of it differently. That is permission worth having: if a teaching will not land one way, it is not that you cannot see it. Turn it round and come at it again.
