# Day 158 — The Drifting Mind

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today the threes come in small sets of faults that travel together. One set is three views: "there is no fault in sense pleasures," the view of a self, and the view that actions bear no fruit. The commentary says these match three wrong ideas: that the self lasts forever, that there is a fixed self, and that it is cut off at death.

Other sets join discontent with cruelty, and being hard to correct with bad friends. Restlessness travels with laziness. *Pamāda*, heedlessness, shows up in two different sets. So do shamelessness and lack of moral dread, and wanting more than you need. The day ends with lack of faith, stinginess, and laziness.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where am I letting my mind drift today?*

**v. U23.** Therein, what is laziness? Letting the mind loose in bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, mental misconduct, or the five strands of sense pleasure, and repeatedly letting it loose; acting without respect for the development of wholesome things, acting without perseverance, acting inconstantly, sluggish behaviour, putting down desire, putting down the responsibility, non-practice, non-development, non-cultivation, non-resolution, non-application, heedlessness — this is called "laziness".

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PAMĀDA — Heedlessness

**Pronunciation:** *pa-mā-da* — the middle syllable is long. Say "pa-MAA-da".

**In today’s passage:** *Pamāda* is heedlessness — letting the mind loose among sense pleasures and wrong actions. The verse also calls it doing good without care or steady effort. The commentary compares it to a chameleon that goes a little, stops a little, and never keeps going.

**Why it matters:** Heedlessness is rarely one big mistake. It is many small moments of drifting. One day we give or meditate, then we stop for weeks. A little practice done every day guards the mind better than a big effort now and then.
