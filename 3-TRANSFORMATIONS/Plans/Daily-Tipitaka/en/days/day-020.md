# Day 20 — Tranquillity Inside the Path

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Yesterday opened the supramundane chapter — the moment a long-practising mind first touches the path leading out of rebirth. Today the chant stays inside that same first jhāna and keeps naming what's in it. Right Concentration (v. 304) closes the eightfold path. Then the five powers — faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom — and two more, shame and fear of wrongdoing.

From v. 312 the verses turn to the three wholesome roots — non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion. After them come the same six pairs again and again: each quality belongs once to the felt body and once to the conscious mind. Tranquillity, lightness, softness, fitness, skill, straightness. The chant ends at *samatho* — calm.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of these qualities is closest at hand in my own practice — and which is furthest away?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### PASSADDHI — Tranquillity

**Pronunciation:** pa-SAD-dhi — three syllables; the "dh" is one soft breath. Stress the middle.

**In today's passage:** *Passaddhi* opens a string of six paired qualities inside today's chant. Each pair has a *kāya-* side (the felt body of feeling, perception, mental formations) and a *citta-* side (consciousness). Tranquillity is the first pair (vv. 320–321): the body settles, and the mind settles with it. Lightness, softness, fitness, skill, and straightness follow. The supramundane moment is not stiff — it is gathered and at rest.

**Why it matters:** When practice is going somewhere, the body knows before the mind explains it. Shoulders drop. Breath slows. Effort stops feeling like effort. The texts call this an enlightenment factor, not a side effect. If you want a small daily sign that something wholesome is taking root, this is it.
