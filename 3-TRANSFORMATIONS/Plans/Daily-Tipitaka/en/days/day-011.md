# Day 11 — Emptiness and the Spectrum of Wholesome Minds

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today's session brings two movements.

The first is the *Suññatavāro* — the Analysis through Emptiness (verses 121–145). This is a third pass over the same first wholesome mind-moment, this time stripping away the detailed definitions and presenting the groupings in their barest form: at that time there are phenomena, aggregates, bases, elements, nutriments, faculties, jhāna, path, powers, roots — each listed by name alone, without elaboration. The teaching is not that these categories are empty of content, but that they are empty of *self*. No person, no being, no "I" can be found within them. Verse 145 closes with the formal seal: "Suññatavāro. Paṭhamaṃ cittaṃ" — the Emptiness Analysis is complete. The first citta is complete.

The second movement opens at verse 146: the second citta, identical to the first except that it arises *with prompting* (*sasaṅkhārena*) rather than spontaneously. Then the third citta (verse 147–148): the first spontaneous mind accompanied by joy but *without* knowledge — the wisdom-faculties and wisdom-powers drop away. The fourth citta (verse 149): the same, but with prompting. The fifth citta (verse 150): the first spontaneous mind accompanied by *equanimity* (*upekkhā*) rather than joy — pīti and sukha are replaced by upekkhā, and the somanassindriya is replaced by upekkhindriya.

Five complete minds examined. Five variations on the theme of sense-sphere wholesome consciousness. The spectrum is becoming clear.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Of these five types of wholesome mind — with joy or equanimity, with knowledge or without, spontaneous or prompted — which feels closest to this moment?*

## 4. Reading for Meaning

### The Emptiness Analysis

At that same moment there are **phenomena**, there are **aggregates**, there are **sense-bases**, there are **elements**, there are **nutriments**, there are **faculties**, there is **jhāna**, there is a **path**, there are **powers**, there are **roots**; there is **contact**, there is **feeling**, there is **perception**, there is **intention**, there is **mind**; there is a feeling-aggregate, a perception-aggregate, a formations-aggregate, a consciousness-aggregate; there is a mind-base, a mind-faculty, a mind-consciousness-element; there is a mind-object-base, a mind-object-element — or whatever other co-arisen immaterial phenomena there are at that time. **These phenomena are wholesome.** *(v. 121)*

**What at that time are the phenomena?** The feeling-aggregate, the perception-aggregate, the formations-aggregate, the consciousness-aggregate — these at that time are the phenomena. *(v. 122)*

**What at that time are the aggregates?** The feeling-aggregate, the perception-aggregate, the formations-aggregate, the consciousness-aggregate — these at that time are the aggregates. *(v. 123)*

**What at that time are the sense-bases?** The mind-base and the mind-object-base. *(v. 124)*

**What at that time are the elements?** The mind-consciousness-element and the mind-object-element. *(v. 125)*

**What at that time are the nutriments?** The contact-nutriment, the mental-volition-nutriment, the consciousness-nutriment. *(v. 126)*

**What at that time are the faculties?** The faculty of faith, of energy, of mindfulness, of concentration, of wisdom, of mind, of joy, of life. *(v. 127)*

**What at that time is the jhāna?** Applied thought, sustained thought, rapture, pleasure, one-pointedness of mind. *(v. 128)*

**What at that time is the path?** Right view, right thought, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. *(v. 129)*

**What at that time are the powers?** The power of faith, of energy, of mindfulness, of concentration, of wisdom, of moral shame, of moral dread. *(v. 130)*

**What at that time are the roots?** Non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion. *(v. 131)*

*(Verses 132–143: contact through mind-consciousness-element are defined using abbreviated "…pe…" formulas, referring back to the Padabhājanī definitions.)*

**What at that time is the mind-object-base?** The feeling-aggregate, the perception-aggregate, the formations-aggregate. *(v. 144)*

**What at that time is the mind-object-element?** The feeling-aggregate, the perception-aggregate, the formations-aggregate. Or whatever other co-arisen immaterial phenomena there are at that time — **these phenomena are wholesome.**

**Suññatavāro. Paṭhamaṃ cittaṃ.** *(v. 145)*

*(The Emptiness Analysis is complete. The First Wholesome Mind is complete.)*

### The Spectrum of Wholesome Minds

**The Second Citta** *(v. 146):* Which phenomena are wholesome? At the time when the sense-sphere wholesome mind has arisen — accompanied by joy, associated with knowledge, **with prompting** — at that time there is contact… non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.** *Dutiyaṃ cittaṃ.*

The difference from the first citta: the second arises only when stimulated — by effort of will, by external encouragement, by a deliberate turning of the mind. The quality of knowing (*ñāṇa*) is identical; only the ease of arising differs.

---

**The Third Citta** *(v. 147–148):* Which phenomena are wholesome? At the time when the sense-sphere wholesome mind has arisen — accompanied by joy, **dissociated from knowledge** (*ñāṇavippayuttaṃ*), spontaneous — at that time there is contact, feeling, perception, intention, mind, applied thought, sustained thought, rapture, pleasure, one-pointedness; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, **mind, joy, life** — *(note: the faculty of wisdom drops away)* — right thought, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration — *(note: right view drops away)* — powers of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration — *(wisdom-power drops away)* — moral shame and moral dread; non-greed, non-hatred — *(non-delusion, which is identical to wisdom, drops away)* — and the qualities of tranquillity, lightness, pliancy, wieldiness, proficiency, uprightness, mindfulness, serenity, effort, non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.** *Tatiyaṃ cittaṃ.*

The third citta is joy-accompanied and spontaneous, but it arises without the simultaneous presence of wisdom. It is still wholesome; it still has 52 factors. But the knowledge-angle is absent in this moment. Most of our good actions arise in this mode.

**The Fourth Citta** *(v. 149):* The same as the third, **with prompting** (*sasaṅkhārena*). *Catutthaṃ cittaṃ.*

---

**The Fifth Citta** *(v. 150):* Which phenomena are wholesome? At the time when the sense-sphere wholesome mind has arisen — accompanied by **equanimity** (*upekkhāsahagataṃ*), associated with knowledge, spontaneous — at that time there is contact, feeling, perception, intention, mind, applied thought, sustained thought, **equanimity** *(upekkhā, replacing pīti and sukha)*, one-pointedness; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, mind, **equanimity** *(upekkhindriya, replacing somanassindriya)*, life; right view, right thought, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration; all seven powers; non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion; all qualities of tranquillity, lightness, pliancy, wieldiness, proficiency, uprightness, mindfulness, clear comprehension, serenity, insight, effort, non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.** *(v. 150)*

The fifth citta is the equanimity-accompanied counterpart of the first. Everything is the same except that rapture and pleasure are replaced by equanimity, and the joy-faculty by the equanimity-faculty. This mind arises in moments of calm, balanced goodness — where there is still knowledge and spontaneity, but the feeling-tone is neither elated nor distressed. *Pañcamaṃ cittaṃ.*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### SUÑÑATA — Emptiness

**Pronunciation:** SUÑ-ña-tā — three syllables; the "ññ" is a doubled palatal nasal, like the "ny" in "canyon" held slightly longer; the final "ā" is long. Stress on the first syllable.

**Literal sense:** *Suññata* is the abstract noun from *suñña*, empty, void. In this context it does not mean "nothing exists" — clearly, the Dhammasaṅgaṇī has just spent 120 verses showing that a great deal exists in a wholesome mind-moment. It means: empty of a self (*attā*), empty of a being (*satta*), empty of what belongs to a self.

**In today's passage:** The *Suññatavāro* presents the same groupings as the Koṭṭhāsavāro — aggregates, bases, elements, nutriments, faculties, jhāna, path, powers, roots — but defined only by name, without elaboration. The simplification is deliberate: these are the names of what is present; look among them and you will not find a person. The *pi-1-practice.md* rail identifies *suññatānupassanā* (contemplation of emptiness) as the direct antidote to *micchādiṭṭhi* (wrong view) and the path to establishing *sammādiṭṭhi*.

**Why it matters:** Suññata is not a nihilistic teaching. It is a precise observation: phenomena arise, they are nameable, they can be grouped, they can be analyzed — and none of them, singly or together, constitutes a self. The Abhidhamma's exhaustive enumeration is itself the demonstration. When we see all 56 factors laid out, named, and categorized, the question "which one is me?" becomes genuinely hard to answer — and that difficulty is the beginning of liberation.
