# Day 64 — The Defilements

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today rests fully in the defilements (*kilesa*) — the states that "stain" or "torment" the mind. The chapter defines them one by one, in rich detail. Greed comes first, under many names. Then hatred, traced through all its forms — the sting of harm to oneself or those one loves, or of good fortune for those one dislikes. Then delusion, conceit, wrong view, doubt, and the rest, ten in all.

Naming them so precisely is itself a practice. A vague "bad mood" is hard to work with. But "this is resentment, because I feel wronged" can be seen and released. The chapter's drift is clear: the defilements arise together, stain the mind, and are washed away by the path. Purifying the mind is the third of the Buddha's three instructions, and this is its map.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I name what is stirring in me precisely — and meet it without shame or struggle?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KILESA — The Defilements

**Pronunciation:** *ki-LEH-sa* — three light beats.

**In today's passage:** The chapter names ten *kilesa*, "defilements": greed, hatred, delusion, conceit, wrong view, doubt, sloth, restlessness, shamelessness, and fearlessness of wrongdoing. The word means what stains or torments the mind. Each is defined closely, and the commentary shows how they arise in clusters.

**Why it matters:** "Purify the mind" is one of the Buddha's three core instructions, and the defilements are exactly what is purified. Naming them precisely turns a fog of "feeling off" into something workable. When you can say "this is conceit," or "this is doubt," you can meet it with mindfulness instead of being run by it. Seen clearly and met steadily, the stain lifts, and the mind grows bright.
