# Day 100 — The Four Noble Truths

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Day one hundred, and the four noble truths. The chapter names them, then spends thirteen verses on the first one alone. Birth is suffering. Aging is suffering, and death. Sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, despair. Being stuck with what you dislike. Being parted from what you love. Not getting what you want. Then the summary line: in brief, the five heaps we cling to are suffering.

Each item is opened out in turn, in plain detail — what birth is, what aging is, what sorrow is. Nothing here is abstract. At the close the chapter turns to the second truth: craving (*taṇhā*), which runs on into another life, delighting now here, now there. The teachers set the four out the way a doctor would: the illness, its cause, its cure, and the medicine. Today you get the first two.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What am I calling by another name because "suffering" sounds too strong?*

**v. 189.** There are four noble truths – the noble truth of suffering, the noble truth of the origin of suffering, the noble truth of the ending of suffering, and the noble truth of the way leading to the ending of suffering.

…

**v. 203.** Therein, what is the noble truth of the origin of suffering? It is this craving that leads to renewed existence, accompanied by delight and desire, delighting now here, now there – namely: craving for sense pleasures, craving for existence, and craving for non-existence. Now where does this craving arise when it arises? Where does it settle when it settles? Whatever in the world is a lovely thing, a pleasing thing – there this craving arises when it arises; there it settles when it settles. *(…)* This is called "the noble truth of the origin of suffering".

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ARIYASACCA — Noble Truth

**Pronunciation:** *a-ri-ya-sac-ca* — "a-ri-ya-SATCH-cha".

**In today's passage:** An *ariyasacca* is a noble truth. Noble because the awakened are the ones who see it; true because it does not shift when you look at it closely. Today all four are named, and then suffering is opened out item by item, from birth all the way to not getting what you want.

**Why it matters:** The teachers compare the four to medicine: suffering is the illness, craving its cause, ending is the cure, and the path is the treatment. That order matters. You are not asked to believe anything here. You are asked to look at what hurts, find what feeds it, and stop feeding it.
