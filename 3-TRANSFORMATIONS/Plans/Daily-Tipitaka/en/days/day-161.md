# Day 161 — Respect and Decline

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The fives end with five wrong ideas about *nibbāna* in this life. Some say the highest peace is enjoying sense pleasures. Others say it is one of the four *jhānas* (meditative absorptions). Each speaker corrects the one before. Sense pleasures change and bring grief, and the first jhāna is still rough with thinking.

Then the sixes begin. The six roots of dispute include anger, contempt, envy, and holding tight to one's own view. Six kinds of disrespect are toward the Teacher, the teaching, the community, the training, heedfulness, and hospitality. Six things lead to decline: delight in busy work, talk, sleep, company, mixing with others, and proliferation (endless mental chatter). Last, the verses show how sights and sounds start joy, grief, or dull indifference tied to home life.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of the six things that lead to decline shows up most in my day?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### AGĀRAVA — Disrespect

**Pronunciation:** *a-gā-ra-va* — four syllables, the second long. Say "a-GAA-ra-va".

**In today’s passage:** *Agārava* is disrespect. The verses name six objects: the Teacher, the teaching, the community, the training, heedfulness, and hospitality. The commentary gives everyday examples: not going to bow at a shrine, chatting through a teaching, pushing past elders.

**Why it matters:** Respect is not about rank. It opens the heart so that we can learn. When we treat the teaching lightly, we take in little of it. Small acts — listening fully, greeting others warmly, bowing with care — keep that door open.
