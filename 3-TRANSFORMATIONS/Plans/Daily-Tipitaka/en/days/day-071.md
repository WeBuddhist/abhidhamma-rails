# Day 71 — From Learner to Adept

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The threes end today, and the twos (*duka*) begin. The very first verse marks the finish line. Which states are "adept" (*asekha*)? The fruit of Arahantship — the mind of one with nothing left to train. A learner is still on the path; an adept has walked the whole of it.

From there the text turns to pairs, opening with the roots (*hetu*). There are three wholesome roots — non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion — and three unwholesome ones — greed, hatred, delusion. Every state of mind is now sorted by whether it carries a root or not, and where that root arises. A root works like the root of a tree: while it holds, the tree stands; cut it, and the tree falls. So this pair points straight at practice — weaken the harmful roots, and the harm they feed cannot last.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which roots am I feeding right now — and which could I begin to let go?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ASEKHA — The Adept (Beyond Training)

**Pronunciation:** *a-SE-kha* — the "kh" is a breathy "k", stress on the middle beat.

**In today's passage:** *Asekha* means "one beyond training." Today's first verse defines it as the mind standing in the fruit of Arahantship. Its partner is *sekha*, the "learner" — one still training in virtue, mind, and wisdom. The adept has finished that training.

**Why it matters:** The goal here is not something superhuman or far away. An adept is simply a mind with no greed, hatred, or delusion left to remove. That marks the direction of every step: each root you loosen today is a little less that ever needs training again.
