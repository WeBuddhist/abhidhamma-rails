# Day 17 — Three Liberations and the Four Divine Abodes

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today the catalogue turns warm. Verses 240–247 close the bases of mastery. The teaching makes one last pass with fair-and-foul colours on wide objects. The long stretch ends. Then verses 248–250 cover the *three liberations* — three more angles on the same jhānas.

Verses 251–262 open the *four divine abodes* (*brahmavihāra*): *mettā* (loving-kindness), *karuṇā* (compassion), *muditā* (joy at others' good), and *upekkhā* (equanimity). Each one is grown through the jhāna grades, in sixteen permutations. The path now turns from holding an object to *radiating a quality* — wide, in every direction.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of the four divine abodes is most missing from how I meet people this week — kindness, compassion, joy at others' good, or steady balance?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### BRAHMAVIHĀRA — Divine Abode

**Pronunciation:** BRAH-ma-vi-HAH-ra — five syllables; "vihāra" means dwelling-place. Stress falls on the first and fourth syllables.

**In today's passage:** *Brahmavihāra* names the four heart-qualities the practitioner now dwells in like a home: *mettā* (loving-kindness, the wish that beings be well), *karuṇā* (compassion, the wish that suffering ease), *muditā* (joy at others' good), and *upekkhā* (equanimity, an even heart). Verses 251–262 walk each through the jhāna grades.

**Why it matters:** These four are called boundless because the more you give them, the more you have. You do not need a meditation cushion to start — you need one person, one moment, and the willingness to wish them well. Today, in any face you meet, you can choose which abode to dwell in. The brahmavihāras are how the wholesome mind shows up in the world.
