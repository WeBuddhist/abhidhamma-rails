# Day 95 — The Object Is Not the Fault

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

This short chapter closes today. Two definitions finish the list — touch, and then mind-objects (*dhammāyatana*), the widest of the twelve bases. That one holds subtle matter, mental factors, and *nibbāna* itself, named here the unconstructed element. Then the Catechism runs all twelve bases through the standard questions.

One answer is worth carrying with you. Ten of the twelve are *abyākata* — indeterminate, meaning neither wholesome nor unwholesome. An eye is not good or bad. A sound is not good or bad. Only two bases, the mind base and mind-objects, can be wholesome or unwholesome. The teaching puts it plainly: the object is not the fault. The mind is what wants, resents, or goes blank. The same sight can meet greed one moment and steady attention the next. So there is nothing to blame in what you see or hear today — and one clear place to do the work.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Was that the object, or was that my mind?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ABYĀKATA — Indeterminate

**Pronunciation:** *a-byā-ka-ta* — "a-BYAA-ka-ta"; the "by" is one sound, as in "abyss".

**In today's passage:** *Abyākata* means indeterminate — neither wholesome nor unwholesome. Today the Catechism asks which of the twelve bases are wholesome, which are unwholesome, and which are indeterminate. Ten of them are indeterminate. Only the mind base and mind-objects can be wholesome or unwholesome at all.

**Why it matters:** This puts the moral weight in one place. Eyes, ears, sights and sounds carry none of it. They are simply what is there. What is wholesome or unwholesome is how the mind takes them up. That is good news. You cannot choose what arrives at your doors, and you do not need to. You can only meet it well, and that is exactly where the choice lives.
