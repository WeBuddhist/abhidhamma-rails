# Day 55 — The Wholesome Roots

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today we begin a new chapter, the Nikkhepakaṇḍaṃ — the "chapter of laying out" (*nikkhepa* means setting each thing in its place). It takes the categories from the very first matrix and sorts every state into them. It opens with the most important question in practice: what is wholesome? The answer is the three wholesome roots (*kusalamūla*) — non-greed, non-hate, and non-delusion. From these grow giving, kindness, and wisdom. Their opposites — greed, hate, and delusion — are the unwholesome roots.

The day ends by naming the three fetters that fall away at the first stage of awakening, "abandoned by seeing." So the chapter starts by handing you the whole map: these roots to grow, these to cut.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which root is moving in me right now — and is it one to grow or to let go?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KUSALAMŪLA — The Three Wholesome Roots

**Pronunciation:** *KU-sa-la-MOO-la* — "ū" held long.

**In today's passage:** The chapter defines the wholesome by its three roots (*kusalamūla*): *alobha* (non-greed), *adosa* (non-hate), and *amoha* (non-delusion). The commentary links each to a fruit — non-greed to giving, non-hate to kindness, non-delusion to wisdom.

**Why it matters:** These are the engine of doing good. When an act is kind or clear, one of these roots is at work. When it grasps or lashes out, an unwholesome root is. Learn to feel which root drives an act, and you can feed the good ones and starve the rest. It is the simplest daily practice, and the deepest.
