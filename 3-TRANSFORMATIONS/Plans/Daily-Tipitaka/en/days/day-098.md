# Day 98 — Only Touch Hurts

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The chapter turns to its question-and-answer close. Eighteen elements are listed, then run through the standard checklist. Are they wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate — that is, neither one? What feeling goes with each?

One answer is worth slowing down for. Ten of the eighteen cannot be said to carry feeling at all: the physical doors and the things they meet, an eye and a sound and a smell. Five carry only neutral feeling — eye-, ear-, nose-, and tongue-consciousness, and the mind-element that takes them in. Only body-consciousness can be joined with pleasure or with pain. Only the mind-consciousness element can carry all three. So among the senses, exactly one hands you pleasure and pain directly, and that one is touch. Seeing and hearing arrive flat. What we call a lovely sight is already the mind's own work, added a moment later.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Did that arrive pleasant, or did I make it pleasant?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### VEDANĀ — Feeling

**Pronunciation:** *ve-da-nā* — "vay-da-NAA"; the last syllable is long.

**In today's passage:** *Vedanā* is feeling — the pleasant, painful, or neutral tone of an experience. It is not emotion. Today the checklist asks which of the eighteen elements carry which tone, and the answer is narrow. Among the five senses, only body-consciousness carries pleasure and pain. The other four arrive neutral.

**Why it matters:** Most of what we call liking and disliking is not in the seeing or the hearing at all. It is added afterwards. That gap is short, but it is real, and it is where practice fits. Catch the flat arrival, and you get a moment before the story starts.
