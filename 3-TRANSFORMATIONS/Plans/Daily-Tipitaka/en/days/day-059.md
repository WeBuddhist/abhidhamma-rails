# Day 59 — The Fetters

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The chapter turns from the taints to the fetters (*saṃyojana*) — the ten bonds that tie beings to the round of rebirth. The image is a rope: each fetter is a strand lashing the mind to more becoming. The list runs from coarse to subtle — the view of a self, doubt, clinging to rites, sensual craving, ill will, and on up to the last faint traces of restlessness and ignorance.

The good news is in how they end. The path cuts them stage by stage. The stream-enterer snaps the first three; an arahant is free of all ten. So awakening is not one impossible leap but a rope untied strand by strand.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which strand binds me most right now — and can I loosen it a little today?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SAṂYOJANA — The Fetters

**Pronunciation:** *sang-YOH-ja-na* — "ṃ" is a soft nasal hum.

**In today's passage:** The chapter lays out the ten *saṃyojana*, "fetters" — the bonds that yoke beings to rebirth. The commentary describes how they arise together, and how the noble path cuts them in stages: the stream-enterer lets go of the first three, and full freedom loosens them all.

**Why it matters:** Naming the fetters turns a vague sense of "being stuck" into something you can work with. Each is a specific bond — a self-view here, a doubt there, a craving, an aversion. And each can be loosened. The path is not one heroic break but a patient untying, strand by strand. Knowing the strands is how you begin.
