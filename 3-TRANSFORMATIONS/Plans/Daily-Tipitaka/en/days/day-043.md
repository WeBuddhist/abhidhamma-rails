# Day 43 — The Tangible

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The close-up study of *derived material form* (matter that leans on the four great elements) keeps going. Today it covers the outer objects of three senses: smell, taste, and touch. Each one is treated the same way. A smell or a taste arises from the four elements, meets its sense for a moment, and is gone.

The day ends with the tangible, and here the text is striking. What you actually touch is hardness, softness, heat, and movement — "earth, fire, and wind" felt right on the skin. There is no solid "thing" behind the touch, just these passing qualities. Seeing this cools the rush to grab the pleasant and push away the unpleasant.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When I touch something, can I feel just hardness, warmth, and movement — arising and passing?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PHOṬṬHABBA — The Tangible

**Pronunciation:** *PHOAT-thab-ba* — "ph" is a breathy "p."

**In today's passage:** The tangible base (*phoṭṭhabbāyatana*) is listed plainly as earth, fire, and wind — felt as hardness, heat, and movement, along with smooth and rough, heavy and light. Notice that water is left out: you cannot feel cohesion by touch, only the other three.

**Why it matters:** This turns ordinary touch into practice. When you sit, walk, or hold something, what is really there is hardness, warmth, and motion — not a solid "thing" you own. Met this way, a pleasant touch pulls less, and an unpleasant one pushes less. The body becomes a field of passing sensations, and the mind grows calm.
