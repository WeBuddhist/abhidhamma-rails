# Day 60 — The Four Floods

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The laying-out keeps grouping the defilements into named bundles, each a different image for how they trap the mind. Today brings three: the knots (*gantha*) that tie the mind to the body and its cravings; the floods (*ogha*) that sweep beings out into the ocean of rebirth; and the yokes (*yoga*) that harness them to the round like an ox to a cart.

Each bundle lists the same deep troubles — sensual craving, wanting to exist, fixed views, and ignorance — seen from a different angle. The point is not to memorize lists but to recognize the feeling of being tied, swept, or yoked, and to know there is a way to untie, to cross, to unyoke.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where do I feel tied or swept along right now — and what would it be to step free?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### OGHA — The Four Floods

**Pronunciation:** *OH-gha* — "gh" is a breathy "g."

**In today's passage:** The chapter lists four *ogha*, "floods": the flood of sensual craving, of wanting to keep existing, of fixed views, and of ignorance. The image is a great current that sweeps beings out to the open sea of rebirth, too strong to swim against by force.

**Why it matters:** Calling these currents "floods" changes how you meet them. You do not thrash against a flood; you find the way across. In the texts, the one who "crosses the flood" does so not by struggling and not by standing still, but by steady, balanced practice. Naming the current you are in — craving, becoming, views, not-knowing — is the first step toward crossing it.
