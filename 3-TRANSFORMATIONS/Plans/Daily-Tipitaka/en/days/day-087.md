# Day 87 — Piling Up, or Taking Down

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The second great round begins, and formations go through the counts again — wholesome, unwholesome, or neither; stained or clean; small, expanded, or measureless.

The day ends on a pair that says something plain about practice. Some states lead to accumulation, some lead to dispersal, and some do neither. Accumulation means adding to the round of becoming — building more of the same. Dispersal means taking it apart. It is a useful test to hold against your own doing: this thing I am about to say, this habit I am feeding — is it piling up, or clearing away? The formations heap is exactly where that gets decided, because this is where intention lives.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Is what I am doing today adding to the pile, or taking from it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ĀCAYAGĀMĪ — Leading to Accumulation

**Pronunciation:** *ā-ca-ya-GĀ-mī* — five beats; "ca" as in "chutney".

**In today's passage:** *Ācayagāmī* means "going toward a heap" — leading to accumulation, adding to the round of becoming. Its partner is *apacayagāmī*, leading to dispersal, taking the heap apart. Today's last verse sorts formations three ways: those that build up, those that dismantle, and those that do neither.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the plainest tests in the whole book. Every intention either adds to what keeps you going round or helps take it down. You do not need to name a state correctly to use this — you only have to ask which direction it points. Asked honestly, the question usually answers itself.
