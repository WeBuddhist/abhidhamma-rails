# Day 12 — Closing the Sense-Sphere, Crossing into Jhāna

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Yesterday we met five of the eight everyday wholesome minds — the kinds of good mind that arise as we walk in the world. Today we finish the other three and then step off that map altogether.

The first half of the day (verses 151–159) closes out the fifth through eighth of those everyday wholesome minds and seals them with the marker *Aṭṭha kāmāvacaramahākusalacittāni* — "the eight great wholesome sense-sphere minds." Then verse 160 opens something new: the *Rūpāvacarakusalaṃ*, the wholesome mind of the fine-material sphere. The opening line — "secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states… one enters and dwells in the first jhāna" — is the canonical formula for meditative absorption, the same one you may have heard in sutta chanting. The Abhidhamma is now turning to look at what is actually present in a mind that has entered jhāna, factor by factor, jhāna by jhāna.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What does it feel like when the same careful attention I've been giving to ordinary good moments is now being given to a mind absorbed in meditation?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### JHĀNA — Meditative Absorption

**Pronunciation:** JHĀ-na — two syllables, stress on the first; the "ā" is long.

**In today's passage:** *Jhāna* is the mind attending steadily to a single meditation object — here, the earth-kasiṇa. From verse 160 the text counts what is present: five factors in the first jhāna, three in the second, two in the third — fewer each time as the practitioner lets one factor go.

**Why it matters:** The practice rail describes jhāna as the mind in which the five hindrances — desire, ill will, sloth, restlessness, doubt — have, for that mind's length, gone quiet. You don't need to sit in jhāna to use this today. Notice once, when a hindrance loosens, what the quieter mind underneath is like. That noticing is the chanting becoming practice.
