# Day 12 — Crossing into Jhāna

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today's verses sit at a hinge. The first stretch closes the eight wholesome minds of the *sense sphere* — *kāmāvacara*, the good minds we use in daily life. Verse 159 caps that catalogue with a chapter seal: *Dutiyabhāṇavāro* (Second Recitation Section). Then a new section opens: the wholesome mind of the *fine-material sphere* — *rūpāvacara*, the mind in deep meditation.

The first half of today (vv. 151–159) finishes the closing four sense-sphere minds, the ones held by *equanimity* rather than joy. The second half (vv. 160–163) steps into the first three *jhānas* (meditative absorption). Each is built by gazing at the *earth kasiṇa* — an earth disc used as a steady focus. What shifts is not the quality of the mind. It is still wholesome. What shifts is how deep the attention runs.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where does my own mind sit right now — in everyday good noticing, or in something deeper?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### JHĀNA — Meditative Absorption

**Pronunciation:** JHAH-na — two syllables, soft breathy "j" and a long "ā". Stress the first.

**In today's passage:** Verses 160–163 open the fine-material sphere section by walking through the first three jhānas. Each jhāna is a state of deep, unified attention — the mind settled on one object (the *earth kasiṇa*) so steadily that the usual restlessness drops away. The first jhāna still has thinking and joy; the second drops the thinking; the third drops the joy and rests in equanimity.

**Why it matters:** Jhāna asks one clean thing: letting go of the five hindrances — sense desire, ill will, sloth, restlessness, and doubt. You do not need to enter jhāna today to get the lesson. Each time you notice one and quietly set it down, you do the same work in a smaller key — making the mind a little more your own.
