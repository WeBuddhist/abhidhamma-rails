# Day 12 — Closing the Sense-Sphere, Crossing into Jhāna

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Yesterday we met five of the eight wholesome minds of the **sense sphere** (*kāmāvacara* — the everyday level where most of our lives happen). Today we meet the last three. Then the text moves to something new.

Verses 151 to 159 close out the eight wholesome sense-sphere minds. They end with a short Pāli line that names them all: *the eight great wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses*.

Verse 160 opens a new door: the *Rūpāvacarakusalaṃ* — **wholesome consciousness of the fine-material sphere** (the mind in deep meditation). The opening line is one many of us know from sutta chanting: *secluded from sense desires, secluded from unwholesome states… one enters and dwells in the first jhāna*. *Jhāna* is **meditative absorption** — the mind settled steadily on one object. The Abhidhamma now looks at what is in the mind in jhāna. Today's verses cover the first, second, and third jhānas, one by one.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I notice, even once today, what a calmer and more settled mind feels like in me?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### JHĀNA — Meditative Absorption

**Pronunciation:** JHĀ-na — two syllables. The "jh" is like a soft "j". The "ā" is long. Stress the first syllable.

**In today's passage:** *Jhāna* is the mind settled steadily on one meditation object — here, the *pathavī-kasiṇa* (an earth disc used as a focus). From verse 160 the text counts the **mental factors** (*cetasika*) present in the mind as it goes deeper. The first jhāna has five. The second has three. The third has two. Each step lets one factor go.

**Why it matters:** Jhāna is the mind in which the five **hindrances** (*nīvaraṇa*) — sense desire, ill will, sloth, restlessness, and doubt — have gone quiet for a while. You don't need to sit in jhāna to use this. Just notice once today, when one of those five eases a little, what your quieter mind feels like.
