# Day 12 — Closing the Sense-Sphere, Crossing into Jhāna

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today's session is the great pivot of Plan 2. Yesterday we walked the spectrum of the eight sense-sphere wholesome minds, beginning to see how the same wholesomeness wears different feeling-tones, different presences or absences of wisdom, different ease or effort of arising. Today we finish that spectrum — and then we step off it.

The first movement (verses 151–158) closes out the fifth, sixth, and seventh of the eight *kāmāvacaramahākusalacittāni* — the great wholesome minds that arise as we walk in the world. Verse 150 yesterday opened the fifth citta: equanimity-accompanied, wisdom-associated, spontaneous. Verses 151–154 give its definitions of contact, feeling, equanimity, and the equanimity-faculty. Verse 155 gives its formations-aggregate. Verses 156–158 add the sixth (the same, with prompting), the seventh (equanimity-accompanied, wisdom-dissociated, spontaneous), and its formations. Verse 159 closes with the eighth citta, then the marker "**Aṭṭha kāmāvacaramahākusalacittāni**" — the eight have been laid out — followed by "**Dutiyabhāṇavāro**," the Second Recitation Section concludes.

The second movement, opening at verse 160, is the *Rūpāvacarakusalaṃ* — wholesome consciousness of the fine-material sphere. The mind that until now has been arising while engaging objects in the everyday world now turns toward a different mode of arising: the jhānas. The opening formula — *vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati* — is the canonical Pāli statement of the first jhāna that anyone who has read a sutta will recognize. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī now picks it up and analyses what is actually present in the mind that has entered. Verse 160 names the first jhāna; verses 161–162 the second (with its formations); verse 163 the third. Tomorrow we will reach the fourth.

The frame is the *Catukkanayo* — the fourfold method, the way the Suttanta speaks of the jhānas: four jhānas, distinguished by which factors have been let go. The earth-kasiṇa is named as the meditation object only because the Abhidhamma needs one to anchor the analysis; the same scheme applies to any of the kasiṇas, and beyond.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What does it feel like when the analysis I have been making of an everyday wholesome mind is the same analysis being made of a mind absorbed in jhāna — and what continuity is the text quietly pointing me toward?*

## 4. Reading for Meaning

### Closing the Fifth Wholesome Mind (vv. 151–155)

**What at that time is contact?** That which at that time is contact, touching, full touching, the state of having fully touched — this at that time is contact. *(v. 151)*

**What at that time is feeling?** That which at that time is mental, born of contact with the appropriate mind-consciousness-element, neither pleasant nor painful, the neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling born of mental contact — this at that time is feeling… *(v. 152)*

**What at that time is equanimity?** That which at that time is mental, neither pleasant nor painful, the neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling born of mental contact — this at that time is equanimity… *(v. 153)*

**What at that time is the equanimity-faculty?** That which at that time is mental, neither pleasant nor painful, the neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling born of mental contact — this at that time is the equanimity-faculty… or whatever other co-arisen immaterial phenomena there are — **these phenomena are wholesome.** At that same moment there are **four aggregates**, two sense-bases, two elements, three nutriments, **eight faculties**, a four-factored jhāna, a five-factored path, seven powers, three roots, one contact… one mind-object-base, one mind-object-element — **these phenomena are wholesome.** *(v. 154)*

**What at that time is the formations-aggregate?** Contact, intention, applied thought, sustained thought, one-pointedness of mind; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, life; right view, right thought, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration; the seven powers; non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion, non-covetousness, non-ill will, right view, moral shame, moral dread; tranquillity, lightness, pliancy, wieldiness, proficiency, uprightness — of body and of mind; mindfulness, clear comprehension, serenity, insight, effort, non-distraction. **The Fifth Wholesome Mind is complete.** *(v. 155)*

The fifth citta, fully laid out: equanimity-accompanied, wisdom-associated, spontaneous. Note the seam: where the joy-accompanied first citta carried *pīti* and *sukha* and the *somanassindriya* (joy-faculty), the fifth carries *upekkhā* (equanimity) and the *upekkhindriya* (equanimity-faculty) in their place. Everything else holds.

---

### The Sixth and Seventh Wholesome Minds (vv. 156–158)

**The Sixth Citta** *(v. 156):* Which phenomena are wholesome? At the time when the sense-sphere wholesome mind has arisen — equanimity-accompanied, wisdom-associated, **with prompting** — at that time there is contact… non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome. The Sixth Wholesome Mind is complete.**

The relation between the sixth and the fifth is exactly the relation between the second and the first: prompted versus spontaneous. The cognitive content is identical; only the manner of arising differs.

**The Seventh Citta** *(v. 157):* Which phenomena are wholesome? At the time when the sense-sphere wholesome mind has arisen — equanimity-accompanied, **wisdom-dissociated**, spontaneous, having any sense-object — at that time there is contact, feeling, perception, intention, mind, applied thought, sustained thought, equanimity, one-pointedness; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, mind, equanimity, life — *(the wisdom-faculty drops away)* — right thought, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration — *(right view drops away)* — six powers — *(the wisdom-power drops away)* — non-greed, non-hatred — *(non-delusion, which is wisdom, drops away)* — moral shame, moral dread; tranquillity, lightness, pliancy, wieldiness, proficiency, uprightness of body and mind; mindfulness, serenity, effort, non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.** Four aggregates, two sense-bases, two elements, three nutriments, **seven faculties**, four-factored jhāna, four-factored path, **six powers, two roots**… **these phenomena are wholesome.**

**The Seventh's Formations-Aggregate** *(v. 158):* Contact, intention, applied thought, sustained thought, one-pointedness of mind; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, life — *(no wisdom-faculty)* — right thought, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration — *(no right view)* — six powers — *(no wisdom-power)* — non-greed, non-hatred — *(no non-delusion)* — moral shame, moral dread; the tranquillity-set; mindfulness, serenity, effort, non-distraction. **The Seventh Wholesome Mind is complete.**

The seventh citta is the equanimity-accompanied counterpart of the third. Wholesome, but without the simultaneous presence of wisdom. Most of our calm, balanced, well-meaning everyday actions arise in this mode.

---

### The Eighth Mind and the Close of the Sense-Sphere (v. 159)

**The Eighth Citta:** Which phenomena are wholesome? At the time when the sense-sphere wholesome mind has arisen — equanimity-accompanied, wisdom-dissociated, **with prompting** — at that time there is contact… non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.**

**The Eighth Wholesome Mind is complete.**

**The Eight Great Wholesome Sense-Sphere Minds.**

**Second Recitation Section.** *(v. 159)*

The eighth citta is the prompted counterpart of the seventh: equanimity-accompanied, wisdom-dissociated, prompted. With it, the eightfold map of sense-sphere wholesome consciousness is complete. Two feeling-tones (joy or equanimity) × two cognitive modes (with or without knowledge) × two modes of arising (spontaneous or prompted) = eight.

---

### Entering the Fine-Material Sphere: The First Jhāna (v. 160)

*Wholesome Consciousness of the Fine-Material Sphere — The Fourfold Method*

**Which phenomena are wholesome?** At the time when, **cultivating the path to rebirth in the fine-material sphere**, *secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states*, **one enters and dwells in the first jhāna** — accompanied by applied thought and sustained thought, born of seclusion, with rapture and pleasure — **focusing on the earth-kasiṇa** — at that time there is contact… non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.** *(v. 160)*

This is the canonical Suttanta formula for the first jhāna, picked up by the Abhidhamma and folded into its analytical framework. The phrase *rūpūpapattiyā maggaṃ bhāveti* — "cultivates the path to rebirth in the fine-material sphere" — does not mean the practitioner is aiming at a future birth; it means the kind of consciousness being analysed is of the type that, were it the last thought of life, would correspond to a fine-material-sphere rebirth. *Pathavīkasiṇa*, the earth-kasiṇa, is named as one of the standard meditation objects; the same analysis applies to any of the ten kasiṇas.

---

### The Second Jhāna and its Formations (vv. 161–162)

**Which phenomena are wholesome?** At the time when, cultivating the path to rebirth in the fine-material sphere, **with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, with internal clarity and unification of mind, without applied thought, without sustained thought, born of concentration, with rapture and pleasure, one enters and dwells in the second jhāna**, focusing on the earth-kasiṇa — at that time there is contact, feeling, perception, intention, mind, rapture, pleasure, one-pointedness of mind; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, mind, joy, life; right view, right effort… effort, non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.** Four aggregates, two sense-bases, two elements, three nutriments, eight faculties, **a three-factored jhāna**, four-factored path, seven powers, three roots… **these phenomena are wholesome.** *(v. 161)*

**The Second Jhāna's Formations-Aggregate** *(v. 162):* Contact, intention, **rapture**, one-pointedness of mind; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, life; right view, right effort… effort, non-distraction; or whatever other co-arisen immaterial phenomena there are at that time, apart from the feeling-, perception-, and consciousness-aggregates. **These phenomena are wholesome.**

Notice what has happened. The first jhāna's five factors — applied thought, sustained thought, rapture, pleasure, one-pointedness — have been reduced. The second jhāna lets go of *vitakka* and *vicāra* and dwells in *samādhi* alone, accompanied by *pīti*, *sukha*, and *ekaggatā*. The jhāna is now "three-factored" rather than "five-factored." The analysis is exact: count the factors named in verse 162 and you will see the absence of applied and sustained thought spoken plainly.

---

### The Third Jhāna (v. 163)

**Which phenomena are wholesome?** At the time when, cultivating the path to rebirth in the fine-material sphere, **with the fading away of rapture**, one dwells in equanimity, mindful and clearly comprehending, and **experiences pleasure with the body**, of which the noble ones declare *"He dwells in equanimity, mindful, dwelling in pleasure,"* **one enters and dwells in the third jhāna**, focusing on the earth-kasiṇa — at that time there is contact, feeling, perception, intention, mind, **pleasure** (without rapture), one-pointedness; the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, mind, joy, life; right view, right effort… effort, non-distraction. **These phenomena are wholesome.** Four aggregates, two sense-bases, two elements, three nutriments, eight faculties, **a two-factored jhāna**, four-factored path, seven powers, three roots… **these phenomena are wholesome.** *(v. 163)*

Rapture is set down. *Pīti* falls away; *sukha* and *ekaggatā* remain. The third jhāna is "two-factored." Each step of the *Catukkanayo* is a step of letting go.

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### JHĀNA — Meditative Absorption

**Pronunciation:** JHĀ-na — two syllables; the "jh" is an aspirated palatal (closer to "jh" in English "judge-house" said quickly than to the "j" in "jam"); the first "ā" is long and stressed; the final "a" short. The whole word lands with steady weight on the first syllable.

**Literal sense:** *Jhāna* comes from the root *jhe*, "to think on, to meditate upon, to attend closely to." The commentaries draw out two complementary senses: *ārammaṇūpanijjhāna*, the close attending to an object (which is the meaning carried in the jhānas of the fine-material sphere), and *lakkhaṇūpanijjhāna*, the close attending to a characteristic (which is the meaning carried in insight). The summary rail glosses the term in today's passage by *ārammaṇūpanijjhāna* — the absorbed attending to a meditation object, the kasiṇa.

**In today's passage:** The Dhammasaṅgaṇī has used the word *jhāna* throughout the *Kāmāvacarakusalaṃ* analysis already, naming the five factors — applied thought, sustained thought, rapture, pleasure, one-pointedness — that are present in any wholesome moment. From verse 160 onwards, the same word is now anchored to the absorbed states of the fine-material sphere. The five factors are still being tracked, but now one factor at a time is set down: the *Catukkanayo* counts them out — five in the first jhāna, three in the second, two in the third, two again in the fourth (with equanimity replacing pleasure). The same word, the same factors, two scales of presence.

**Why it matters:** The practice rail names what each factor does: *vitakka* "lifts the mind onto its object"; *vicāra* "keeps it touching the object"; *pīti* "thrills the mind"; *sukha* "presents the comfort of the mental factors"; *ekaggatā* "establishes concentration." Together, the rail says, they undo each of the five hindrances — sense-desire stilled by one-pointedness, ill-will stilled by rapture, sloth-and-torpor stilled by applied thought, restlessness-and-worry stilled by pleasure, doubt stilled by sustained thought. Jhāna in this technical sense is not an altered state to be chased; it is the inner architecture of a mind in which the hindrances have, for the duration of that mind, gone quiet.
