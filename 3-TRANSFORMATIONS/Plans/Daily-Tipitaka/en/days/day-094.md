# Day 94 — Twelve Doors, All of Them Passing

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

A new chapter opens today. The aggregates asked what we are made of. The *āyatana* — the bases — ask where experience happens. There are twelve. Six are inner doors: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind. Six are what those doors meet: form, sound, smell, taste, touch, mind-objects.

The first verse says one thing about all twelve. Each is impermanent (*anicca*), suffering (*dukkha*), not-self (*anattā*), and subject to change. Then the text slows down and defines each base in turn. The teaching adds a useful note here. Impermanence and pain are easy enough to notice. Not-self is not, and three things hide it. One moment covers the next, so we miss the change. Shifting and settling covers the ache, so we miss the pain. And the feel of one solid lump covers the parts, so we miss the not-self. Break the lump into parts and the hiding stops.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *At which of the twelve doors did my mind get caught today?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ĀYATANA — Base

**Pronunciation:** *ā-ya-ta-na* — "AA-ya-ta-na", four even beats.

**In today's passage:** *Āyatana* means a base — a meeting place, where something happens. Today's verses name twelve of them. Six are the inner doors: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind. Six are what those doors meet: form, sound, smell, taste, touch, mind-objects. The text then defines each one in turn, so nothing is left vague.

**Why it matters:** Nothing happens to you in general. It happens at one of twelve places, one at a time. That makes this practice small enough to actually do. You do not have to watch your whole life — only the door that is open right now. And the teaching says all twelve are passing and not-self, so there is nothing at any door worth holding on to.
