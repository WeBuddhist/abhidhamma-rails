# Day 25 — When the Mind Turns to Hate

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today the chant moves through three more unwholesome minds and then crosses into a different root. Verses 404–408 finish the third greed-mind — accompanied not by joy but by *upekkhā*, equanimity. The same factors as before, but with a *neither-pleasant-nor-unpleasant* feeling in place of *sukha*. Verses 409–412 then run the fourth greed-mind — equanimity, dissociated from wrong view, prompted.

From v. 413 the chant turns to a new field. A mind accompanied not by joy or equanimity but by *domanassa* — displeasure. Joined not to wrong view but to *paṭigha* — aversion. This is the first of the two hatred-rooted minds. The factors of pleasure and joy drop away; in their place come *dukkha* (suffering) and the *displeasure faculty*. The chant ends today on *dosa* and *byāpāda* — hatred and ill will, the new pair of roots.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When my mind tightens against something today, what is it actually rejecting?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### DOSA — Hatred

**Pronunciation:** DO-sa — two syllables; both short. Stress the first.

**In today's passage:** *Dosa* is the second unwholesome root the chant names directly. V. 418 lists its many faces: hating, ill will, opposition, counter-opposition, fierceness, malevolence, *anattamanatā cittassa* — "discontent of mind." V. 419 names the same energy as *byāpāda* — ill will, the wish for another to suffer. The hatred-rooted mind has no joy in it, only displeasure and the heat of pushing away.

**Why it matters:** Hatred shows up smaller than the word suggests. A short answer to a child. A small ruling on someone we don't like. A sigh at traffic. Naming hatred as one root gives a practitioner one practice — see the heat early, and let it cool before it speaks. A little less heat is a little less harm.
