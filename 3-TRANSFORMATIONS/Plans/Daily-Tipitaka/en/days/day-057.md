# Day 57 — The Roots

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the triads finish — sorting states by whether their object is past, future, or present, inside or outside — and the pairs (*dukas*) begin. The pairs open with the most basic one of all: roots (*hetu*). A state is either a root or not, and either has roots or lacks them.

Why start here? Because roots are where change begins. The three unwholesome roots — greed, hate, delusion — are like the root of a tree: cut the root, and the trunk, branches, and leaves all fall. The whole path can be seen as roots-work: starving the unwholesome roots and growing the wholesome ones, until the unwholesome no longer sprout.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What is the root beneath this thought or mood — and what happens if I don't feed it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### HETU — The Roots

**Pronunciation:** *HEH-tu* — two short beats.

**In today's passage:** The pairs begin with *hetu*, "root." States are sorted by whether they are roots, and whether they have roots. There are six roots in all: three unwholesome (greed, hate, delusion) and three wholesome (their opposites). Everything else grows from these.

**Why it matters:** The commentary's image is a tree: cut it at the root and the whole thing falls. Defilements work the same way. You do not have to fight every angry or greedy thought head-on; you can go to the root that feeds them. Starve the unwholesome roots, water the wholesome ones, and the visible "branches" — harsh words, grasping acts — lose their supply.
