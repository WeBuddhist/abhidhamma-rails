# Day 85 — Two Ways Things Are Let Go

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today's first verse names a pair that runs through the whole Abhidhamma: some things are given up by seeing (*dassana*), some by developing, and some by neither. Seeing means the first clear look at how things actually are — the moment that cuts wrong view at the root. Developing means the long work afterwards, wearing down what one look cannot remove.

The rest of the day continues sorting perception, and the range closes the aggregate off entirely: after a long final verse running through set after set, the text says simply, "This is called the aggregate of perception." One of the five heaps is now fully turned. Two remain in this second pass.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Is what I am struggling with something to see through — or something to keep working at?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### DASSANA — Seeing

**Pronunciation:** *DAS-sa-na* — three beats, stress on the first.

**In today's passage:** *Dassana* means "seeing" — not looking at something, but the first real seeing of how things are, the insight that opens the path. Today's verses divide what has to be given up into what goes by seeing, what goes by long development, and what needs neither. Wrong view belongs to the first kind: it does not wear away slowly, it breaks when seen through.

**Why it matters:** This is a practical division, not a doctrinal one. Some knots come undone the moment you look straight at them; others only loosen with patient repetition. Knowing which is which saves effort — you stop grinding away at what wants a clear look, and stop expecting one insight to finish work that takes years.
