# Day 136 — A Heart Without Limits

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

A new chapter opens on the *appamaññā* — the four "measureless" states of heart: loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity. They are called measureless because they reach out to beings without limit or count.

Today's passage begins with *mettā* (loving-kindness). The meditator spreads a mind of good will to one direction, then to all directions, "above, below, across, everywhere", until the whole world is included — a heart "abundant, expanded, measureless, without hostility, without ill will". The commentary notes this practice especially suits an angry temperament, because warmth is the direct answer to ill will. Good will is not a mood that comes and goes; it is a skill that can be widened on purpose.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Who is easy for me to wish well — and could I widen that circle by one person?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### METTĀ — Loving-Kindness

**Pronunciation:** *met-tā* — a short "met", then a long "taa".

**In today’s passage:** *Mettā* is loving-kindness — a warm, open wish for beings to be well. The meditator radiates it in every direction until it is "measureless". It is the first of the four boundless states and, the commentary says, the direct cure for ill will.

**Why it matters:** Loving-kindness is trainable. You start where it is easy — someone you already care for — and gently widen the circle. Done a little each day, it softens the reflex to blame and pull away. A warmer heart does less harm almost without trying, and it is an easier place to live.
