# Day 141 — Four Ways of Understanding

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

A new chapter opens on the four *paṭisambhidā* (analytical knowledges). These are four ways a wise mind takes a teaching apart and sees it fully. They are knowledge of meaning, of principles, of language, and of ready understanding.

Today's verses first set the four out plainly. Then they apply them to the four noble truths, to cause and effect, and to the teaching itself. Each time, the "meaning" is the result. The "principle" is the cause that brings it about. Next the verses show these knowledges at work on every kind of mind, seeing good and bad states for what they are. The commentary says this knowledge grows from five things: reaching the goal, study, careful listening, asking questions, and past practice.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When I hear a teaching, do I ask what it means — or only what it says?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PAṬISAMBHIDĀ — Analytical Knowledge

**Pronunciation:** *pa-ṭi-sam-bhi-dā* — five syllables. Say "pa-ti-sam-BHI-daa".

**In today’s passage:** *Paṭisambhidā* means taking something apart to see it clearly. The commentary says it is always knowledge being divided — knowledge of meaning, of principles, of language, and of knowledge itself. Today’s verses name all four and apply them across the whole teaching.

**Why it matters:** These knowledges grow from ordinary habits: study, careful listening, asking questions, and steady practice. Nobody starts with them. But each time you stop to ask "what does this really mean?", you train the same power. A mind that understands clearly is harder to fool, even by itself.
