# Day 156 — Hard to Tell

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's pairs move from inner faults to how we live with others. Shamelessness and lack of moral dread come first. Being hard to correct means pushing back when someone points out a fault. Evil friendship means spending time with people who lack faith, virtue, and wisdom. Rough speech, careless senses, and eating without moderation are named too. The pairs end with two fetters: one ties us to the sense world, one to higher worlds.

Then the chapter turns to threes. It starts with the three unwholesome roots: greed, hate, and delusion. From these grow wrong thoughts, wrong perceptions, and misconduct of body, speech, and mind. The commentary says these three roots keep the whole round of birth and death turning.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When someone corrects me, do I listen or push back?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### DOVACASSATĀ — Being Hard to Correct

**Pronunciation:** *do-va-cas-sa-tā* — five syllables, the last one long. Say "do-va-CAS-sa-taa".

**In today’s passage:** *Dovacassatā* means being hard to speak to. When someone points out a fault in line with the teaching, this person argues, resists, or shows no respect. The verses pair it with evil friendship, because both cut us off from good advice.

**Why it matters:** Correction rarely feels pleasant. But a fault we cannot hear about is a fault we cannot fix. Being easy to correct is a quiet strength, not a weakness. Next time someone points something out, pause before you answer. Ask yourself: "Is any of this true?"
