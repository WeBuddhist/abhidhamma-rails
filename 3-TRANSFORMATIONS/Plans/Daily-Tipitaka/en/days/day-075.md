# Day 75 — The Fuel of Clinging

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today's pairs move inward, then to what fuels becoming. The verses begin with the internal and external (*ajjhattika*): the six inner bases — eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind — set against the outer things they meet.

From there comes clinging (*upādāna*), the four ways the mind grabs and holds: grasping at sense-pleasures, at views, at rules and rituals, and at a fixed idea of self. Clinging is the fuel that keeps the round of becoming burning. Then the verses turn to the defilements (*kilesa*) — the ten states that stain and torment the mind, led by greed, hatred, and delusion. The day's last pair asks a subtle question: which states are defilements *and* liable to defilement? The defilements themselves.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What am I holding onto so hard that I keep feeding it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### UPĀDĀNA — Clinging (Fuel)

**Pronunciation:** *u-pā-DĀ-na* — long "ā" sounds, stress on the third beat.

**In today's passage:** *Upādāna* means both "clinging" and "fuel" — one word for both. There are four kinds: clinging to sense-pleasures, to views, to rules and rituals, and to a doctrine of self. Today's verses show where each one arises in the mind.

**Why it matters:** The image is fire. A fire burns only while it is fed; the mind keeps grabbing at more becoming only while it clings. You do not put clinging down by force. You see clearly what you are holding — and slowly let the fuel run low, until the grasping has less and less to catch on.
