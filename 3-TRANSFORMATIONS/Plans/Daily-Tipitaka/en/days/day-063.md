# Day 63 — Clinging

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the sense-pairs finish, and the chapter reaches the clingings (*upādāna*). These are the four ways the mind grasps and holds: to sense-pleasures, to views, to rites and rituals, and — deepest of all — to a doctrine of self. Clinging is the fuel that feeds becoming. Where there is grasping, the fire of rebirth keeps burning.

The clinging to self is worth pausing on. Much of our suffering comes from defending an idea of "me." As the day closes, the chapter begins the defilements, opening with a long, vivid definition of greed — craving under two dozen names. The turn is on purpose: name what the mind clings to, and you can begin to open the hand.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What am I gripping most tightly — and what would it feel like to loosen my hold?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### UPĀDĀNA — Clinging

**Pronunciation:** *u-PĀ-dā-na* — "ā" held long.

**In today's passage:** The chapter lists four *upādāna*, "clingings": to sense-pleasures, to views, to rites and rituals, and to a doctrine of self. The word also means "fuel" — clinging is what feeds the fire of continued becoming.

**Why it matters:** Clinging is grasping that has hardened into a hold. The subtlest and strongest is the clinging to self — the constant defense of "me and mine." Seeing this is freeing, because a fire without fuel goes out. You do not have to force the hand open; just noticing where you grip — a pleasure, an opinion, an image of yourself — already begins to loosen it. Less clinging, less burning.
