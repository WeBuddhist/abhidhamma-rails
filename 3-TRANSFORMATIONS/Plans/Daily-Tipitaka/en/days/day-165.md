# Day 165 — One Hundred and Eight

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The chapter closes today. The wanderings of craving now lean on something outside: "By this I am." A person grabs one thing — a sunshade, a sword, a title — and says, "By this I am a noble." Again there are eighteen.

Then the verses add them up. Eighteen inner and eighteen outer make thirty-six. Counted for past, future, and present, there are one hundred and eight wanderings of craving. Last, the chapter points to the sixty-two views the Buddha listed in the Brahmajāla Sutta. The commentary says craving, conceit, and views travel together here. With that, the long list of small faults is complete.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What do I lean on to feel that I am someone?*

**v. 975.** Therein, what are the eighteen wanderings of craving concerning what is external? There is "by this I am"; there is "by this I am thus"; there is "by this I am just so"; there is "by this I am otherwise"; there is "by this I shall be"; there is "by this I shall be thus"; there is "by this I shall be just so"; there is "by this I shall be otherwise"; there is "by this I am lasting"; there is "by this I am perishing"; there is "by this may I be"; there is "by this may I be thus"; there is "by this may I be just so"; there is "by this may I be otherwise"; there is "by this would that I might be"; there is "by this would that I might be thus"; there is "by this would that I might be just so"; there is "by this would that I might be otherwise".

…

**v. 977.** Therein, what are the sixty-two grounds of views spoken of by the Blessed One in the exposition of the Brahmajāla? The four eternalist doctrines, the four partly-eternalist doctrines, the four doctrines of the finite and infinite, the four doctrines of endless equivocation, the two doctrines of chance origination, the sixteen doctrines of the percipient, the eight doctrines of the non-percipient, the eight doctrines of the neither-percipient-nor-non-percipient, the seven annihilationist doctrines, the five doctrines of Nibbāna in this very life — these are the sixty-two grounds of views spoken of by the Blessed One in the exposition of the Brahmajāla. The Analysis of the Minor Topics is finished.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### TAṆHĀVICARITA — Wandering of Craving

**Pronunciation:** *taṇ-hā-vi-ca-ri-ta* — six syllables; the "c" sounds like "ch". Say "TAN-haa-vi-cha-ri-ta".

**In today’s passage:** A *taṇhāvicarita* is a wandering of craving — one way craving moves through the mind. Each one starts from "I am," "I shall be," or "may I be." Some lean on what is inside us, and some on outside things: "By this I am."

**Why it matters:** The well-known number 108 is counted here: one hundred and eight ways craving builds a self. You do not need to remember them all. Just notice the small thought "I am this," or "I need that to be someone." Seeing it is the first step to resting without it.
