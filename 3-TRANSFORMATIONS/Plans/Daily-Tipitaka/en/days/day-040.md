# Day 40 — Earth, Water, Fire, Wind

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the matrix finishes its run — sorting material form by fives, sixes, and so on up to elevens — and then the detailed exposition (*Rūpavibhatti*) begins. The most useful group is the fivefold: the four great elements (*mahābhūta*) — earth, water, fire, and wind — plus the form derived from them. "Earth" means hardness, "water" cohesion, "fire" warmth, "wind" movement; these are felt qualities, not the outer substances.

This is the seed of four-elements meditation. You look at the body and find only these four working qualities, with no owner behind them. The higher groups (sixes through elevens) sort the same material form by the senses and faculties. All of it ends the same way — conditioned, impermanent, worn by aging.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I feel this body as earth, water, fire, and wind — and find no one inside?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### MAHĀBHŪTA — The Four Great Elements

**Pronunciation:** *ma-hā-BHOO-ta* — "bh" is a breathy "b," the "ū" held long.

**In today's passage:** The fivefold group lists the four *mahābhūta* — earth, water, fire, wind — and the form derived from them. The commentary glosses them as qualities you can feel: hardness, cohesion, warmth, and movement. Every piece of matter is built from these four.

**Why it matters:** Sorting the body into four working qualities breaks the sense that it is one solid "me." The commentary says to see "only elements here, no being, no person." Practised gently, this calms the mind and loosens fear and pride about the body — you meet it as nature, not as a self to defend.
