# Day 74 — Wrong Grasping

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The clusters of bonds finish today, and a new kind of pairing begins. First the hindrances close: which states are free of them? The wholesome, results, action, matter, and Nibbāna — the whole clean side of the mind.

Then comes a single, sharp item: misapprehension (*parāmāsa*), the mind's habit of grasping things the wrong way — most of all, holding a view and insisting "this alone is true." After that the verses open the great middle pairs (*mahantara-duka*). These sort states by how they stand to consciousness itself: joined with the mind or not, caused by it, following it or standing apart. The last pair today marks what does *not* simply follow the mind — consciousness itself, matter, and Nibbāna.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where am I gripping a view so tightly that I cannot see past it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PARĀMĀSA — Misapprehension (Wrong Grasping)

**Pronunciation:** *pa-RĀ-mā-sa* — long "ā" sounds, stress on the second beat.

**In today's passage:** *Parāmāsa* means "grasping wrongly" — literally touching a thing and holding it the wrong way round. Its main form is holding a view as the one and only truth. Today's verses show where this grasping arises: in the greed-rooted states that come joined with wrong view.

**Why it matters:** We all meet life through opinions. *Parāmāsa* is when an opinion hardens into "I am right, and that settles it." Loosening that grip does not mean having no views. It means holding them lightly enough to keep looking, keep listening, and keep learning.
