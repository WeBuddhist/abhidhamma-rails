# Day 153 — Drunk on Ourselves

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

A new chapter begins today: the *Khuddakavatthuvibhaṅga*, the Analysis of Minor Topics. "Minor" does not mean unimportant. It means small, everyday faults. The chapter first lays them out in groups of one, two, three, and on up to ten. The teachers count about eight hundred *kilesa* (defilements) in all — the things that stain and trouble the mind.

Then the details begin. The first fault is *mada*, intoxication. It is pride that makes the mind drunk. A person can get drunk on birth, family, health, youth, wealth, looks, or learning — even on virtue or meditation. The inner voice is always the same: "I have this, and others do not." The verses also name heedlessness, a stiff and stubborn mind, the urge to outdo others, and always wanting more.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What am I quietly proud of, and does it make me look down on anyone?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### MADA — Intoxication

**Pronunciation:** *ma-da* — two short syllables. Say "MA-da".

**In today’s passage:** *Mada* is intoxication — pride that makes the mind drunk. The verses list twenty-seven things a person can get drunk on, from birth and health to fame and meditation. The commentary gives the inner voice of each one: "I am healthy; the others are often sick."

**Why it matters:** Good things are not the problem. Health, skill, and learning are worth having. The trouble starts when they become a measuring stick for other people. When you notice the thought "I am better because…", you can enjoy the gift without the poison. That keeps the heart soft.
