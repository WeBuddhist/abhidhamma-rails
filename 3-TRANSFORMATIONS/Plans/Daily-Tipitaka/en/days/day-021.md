# Day 21 — Two Doors to Freedom

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Yesterday the chant rested in calm at the closing word *samatho*. Today the verses cross into something new. The first eight (335–342) finish the long list of factors inside the supramundane mind — insight, exertion, non-distraction — and close the chapter called *Suddhikapaṭipadā*, the "pure mode of progress."

Then two doors open. From v. 343 the verses name the same supramundane jhāna (meditative absorption) again, but this time as *suññataṃ* — *emptiness*. From v. 350 the same moment is named again as *appaṇihitaṃ* — the *undirected*. The teaching says one liberating moment, but a chanter can step toward it from more than one side. One side sees that nothing has a self to defend. The other side lets go of every wish to grasp.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which door feels nearer to my own mind right now — the empty one, or the undirected one?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### SUÑÑATĀ — Emptiness

**Pronunciation:** soon-nya-TAA — three syllables; the "ñ" is a soft "ny" sound. Stress the last.

**In today's passage:** From v. 343 the chant gives the supramundane path a new name: *suññataṃ*. The commentary says it earns this name in three ways — by how the practitioner arrives at it, by what it is empty of, and by what it rests on. Walking the path means watching every formation, seeing it has no self inside, and letting that seeing carry you toward *nibbāna* — which is itself empty of greed, hatred, and delusion.

**Why it matters:** Emptiness here is not nothing. It is freedom from the small self that defends itself all day long. A mind that sees clearly how light its grip can be is a mind that does less harm without trying.
