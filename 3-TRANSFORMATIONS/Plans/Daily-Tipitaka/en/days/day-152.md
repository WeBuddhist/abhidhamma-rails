# Day 152 — Seeing to the Root

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The chapter closes today. The verses finish the Buddha's sixth power, his knowledge of each being's readiness. They name the seven *anusaya* (underlying tendencies): sensual lust, aversion, conceit, views, doubt, the lust for existence, and ignorance. They sort beings by how much "dust" is in their eyes and how sharp their faculties are.

Then come the last four powers. The Buddha knows how deep meditation is spoiled, cleansed, and left behind. He remembers countless past lives. He sees beings pass away and be reborn according to their actions. And finally he knows the ending of the taints — the freedom of mind and of wisdom he has realised for himself. The commentary adds that these ten powers are not the same as the Buddha's all-knowing mind. Each one does its own task.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which underlying tendency shows up most often in me, and can I see it without shame?*

**v. 816.** And what are the underlying tendencies of beings? There are seven underlying tendencies: the underlying tendency to sensual lust, the underlying tendency to aversion, the underlying tendency to conceit, the underlying tendency to views, the underlying tendency to doubt, the underlying tendency to lust for existence, the underlying tendency to ignorance. Whatever in the world is a dear and agreeable form — there the underlying tendency of beings to lust lies latent. Whatever in the world is a disagreeable and unpleasant form — there the underlying tendency of beings to aversion lies latent. So ignorance lies fallen upon these two things; and conceit, views, and doubt, standing together with it, should be seen there too. This is the underlying tendency of beings.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ANUSAYA — Underlying Tendency

**Pronunciation:** *a-nu-sa-ya* — four short syllables. Say "a-NU-sa-ya".

**In today’s passage:** *Anusaya* is an underlying tendency — a habit of the heart that lies low and rises when conditions are right. The verses name seven, from sensual lust and aversion to conceit, views, doubt, lust for existence, and ignorance. The Buddha’s sixth power includes knowing them in each being.

**Why it matters:** Underlying tendencies explain why the same reactions keep coming back. They are not a flaw in who you are; they are patterns that can be weakened. Naming one when it rises — "there is aversion" — takes away some of its force. That small act of seeing is where freedom starts.
