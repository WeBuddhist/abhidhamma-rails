# Day 45 — Bodily Intimation

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the text sorts matter by how it serves consciousness — which material form is the *object* of seeing, hearing, and the rest. It ends on something close to home: bodily intimation (*kāyaviññatti*). This is how your inner state shows in your body.

When the mind moves — to step forward, turn, bend, or reach — the body stiffens and shapes itself, and others can "read" your intention in it. The text calls this a kind of matter, born of mind. The practice is plain. Your posture and movements quietly broadcast your mind all day. Watching them is a doorway to mindfulness of the body, and a check on careless action.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What is my body saying right now — and what mind is behind it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KĀYAVIÑÑATTI — Bodily Intimation

**Pronunciation:** *KAH-ya-viñ-ñat-ti* — "ñ" is like the "ny" in "canyon."

**In today's passage:** *Kāyaviññatti* is the way a wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral mind shows itself in the body — in advancing, turning, bending, or reaching. The mind moves, the body stiffens and gestures, and the intention is "made known." The text counts this as a real kind of matter.

**Why it matters:** Your body is always telling the truth about your mind. Noticing this brings two gifts: mindfulness of the body in motion, and care in how you act. When you see a harsh gesture forming, you can catch the harsh mind behind it. Watched gently, the body becomes a teacher all day long.
