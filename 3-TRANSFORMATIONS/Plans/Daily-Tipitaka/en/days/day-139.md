# Day 139 — The Five Training Rules

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

A new chapter opens on the five *sikkhāpada* (training rules): abstaining from killing, from taking what is not given, from sexual misconduct, from false speech, and from drink and drugs that cloud the mind. These are the five precepts many lay Buddhists take every day.

Today's verses ask a surprising question about each one: what is a training rule, as a state of mind? The answer is that keeping a precept is not only something the body does or does not do. It is a *kusala* (wholesome) state of mind that arises in the moment you hold back from harm. The commentary adds a helpful point. The precepts are the ground the rest of the training stands on. They are the first step, not a side project.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of the five precepts asks the most of me right now?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SIKKHĀPADA — Training Rule

**Pronunciation:** *sik-khā-pa-da* — four syllables. Say "sik-KAA-pa-da".

**In today’s passage:** A *sikkhāpada* is a training rule — literally a "step of training". The commentary explains the name: each precept is a footing on which the higher training stands. Today’s verses show each rule as a wholesome state of mind, not just a list of things to avoid.

**Why it matters:** Seen this way, a precept is not a fence that keeps you out of trouble. It is a step you take, again and again, toward a clearer mind. Each time you hold back from harm, you are not only avoiding a bad act. You are training a good one.
