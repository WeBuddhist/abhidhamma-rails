# Day 22 — All Four Paths

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today the chant takes us across three doorways at once. The first three verses (354–356) close the *undirected* mode. Then v. 357 — *Vīsati mahānayā*, the *Twenty Great Methods* — names the supramundane path in nineteen more ways. It is the same moment, just named from many angles: as path, as mindfulness, as faculty, as power, as truth, as calm, and so on.

From v. 358 the *Adhipati* (Predominance) section names four motors that can carry a practitioner. They are aspiration, energy, mind, and investigation. Then verses 361, 362, 363 each open one more path — second, third, fourth. V. 364 closes with the words *Lokuttaraṃ cittaṃ* — "supramundane consciousness." All four noble paths now stand named.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of the four — aspiration, energy, mind, investigation — most carries my practice today?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### ADHIPATI — Predominance

**Pronunciation:** a-DHI-pa-ti — four syllables; the "dh" is one soft breath. Stress the second.

**In today's passage:** From v. 358 the chant names four *adhipati* — four leaders that can put a practitioner on the path. *Chanda* (aspiration), *vīriya* (energy), *citta* (mind), and *vīmaṃsā* (investigation). The commentary says any one of them, made the head of the work, can lead the supramundane jhāna to grow. Different practitioners are carried by different leaders.

**Why it matters:** A path is not a single engine. Some days aspiration pulls you onto the cushion; some days bare energy does; some days the mind itself wants to settle; some days curiosity about what is actually true does the work. The teaching does not pick one. It asks you to notice which one is closest at hand today, and walk with it.
