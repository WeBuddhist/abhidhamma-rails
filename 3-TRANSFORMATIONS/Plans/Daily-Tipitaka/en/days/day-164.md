# Day 164 — Calling Wrong Right

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The tens arrive today. They start with ten grounds of defilement, from greed and hate to shamelessness. Ten grounds of resentment follow. The commentary adds that people even feel resentment at a stump or a thorn they trip over. Then come the ten unwholesome actions, the ten fetters, and ten-fold wrong views. Two "wrongnesses" are new. Wrong knowledge is doing something bad and then thinking, "I did that well." Wrong liberation is thinking you are free when you are not.

Then the chapter turns to the *taṇhāvicarita*, the wanderings of craving. Craving takes body and mind as a whole and says, "I am." From that come "I am this," "I am like him," "I shall be," and "may I be." There are eighteen of these.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Have I ever called a harmful act "well done"?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### MICCHĀÑĀṆA — Wrong Knowledge

**Pronunciation:** *mic-chā-ñā-ṇa* — the "ñ" sounds like "ny" in "canyon". Say "mic-CHAA-nyaa-na".

**In today’s passage:** *Micchāñāṇa* is wrong knowledge. The commentary explains it as cleverness used for harm. A person plans a bad act, does it, and then looks back thinking, "I did that well." It sits beside wrong liberation — feeling free while still bound.

**Why it matters:** This fault is hard to see from the inside, because it feels like being smart. So the test is not how clever a plan was, but what it did to others. When you feel proud of a result, ask one more question: "Who paid for it?"
