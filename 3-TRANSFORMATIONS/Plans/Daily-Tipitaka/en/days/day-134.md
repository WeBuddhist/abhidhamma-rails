# Day 134 — The Four Jhānas

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Now the same ground is walked a second way. The earlier passage described *jhāna* (deep meditative calm) as something a person lives through. Today's verses set the four jhānas out as the Abhidhamma does — as exact states of mind, listed and then broken down one factor at a time.

The four are named plainly: first, second, third, fourth. Each is shown as a *kusala* (wholesome) state of mind, with the parts that make it up — contact, feeling, one-pointedness, and the rest. The point under the analysis is kind. Deep calm is not magic or a lucky mood. It is a state you can know and build, made of qualities you can grow.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Do I treat calm as luck, or as something I can patiently build?*

**Verses 623–631.** *(Full text rendered in the WeBuddhist app from the verse library; the first and last verses of the range are printed below so the plan-importer can find the right entries.)*

**v. 623.** There are four meditative absorptions: the first meditative absorption, the second meditative absorption, the third meditative absorption, the fourth meditative absorption.

…

**v. 631.** Therein, what is the first meditative absorption? Here a monk, on the occasion when he develops the path for rebirth in the fine-material sphere — secluded from sense pleasures… he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption on the earth device — on that occasion there is contact… there is non-distraction. These things are wholesome. When, because that same fine-material-sphere wholesome action has been done and accumulated, as its result — secluded from sense pleasures… he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption on the earth device — on that occasion there is the five-factored meditative absorption: thought, examination, joy, happiness, one-pointedness of mind. This is called "the first meditative absorption". The remaining things are associated with the meditative absorption. *(…)* Therein, what is the second meditative absorption? Here a monk, on the occasion when he develops the path for rebirth in the fine-material sphere — with the stilling of thought and examination… he enters and dwells in the second meditative absorption on the earth device — on that occasion there is contact… there is non-distraction. These things are wholesome. When, because that same fine-material-sphere wholesome action has been done and accumulated, as its result — with the stilling of thought and examination… the second meditative absorption… the third meditative absorption… the fourth meditative absorption… the first meditative absorption… he enters and dwells in the fifth meditative absorption on the earth device — on that occasion there is the two-factored meditative absorption: equanimity, one-pointedness of mind. This is called "the fifth meditative absorption". The remaining things are associated with the meditative absorption….

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### JHĀNA — Meditative Absorption

**Pronunciation:** *jhā-na* — the "jh" is a soft "j" with a breath behind it.

**In today’s passage:** *Jhāna* is a state of deep meditative absorption — the mind unified and still. Today the four jhānas are listed and then taken apart into the mental factors that make each one. This shows calm as something with real, describable parts.

**Why it matters:** Naming the parts of calm makes it less mysterious and more reachable. You are not waiting for a special mood; you are gathering ordinary qualities — steadiness, one-pointedness, ease — that grow with practice. Seen this way, even a few settled breaths are the same path, just earlier on it.
