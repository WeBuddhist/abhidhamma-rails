# Day 29 — The Eight Great Results

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today the chant finishes the small resultant (*vipāka*) minds and reaches the large ones. Verses 478–483 close the investigating mind that carries joy (*somanassa*). Verses 484–497 give its twin, the one that carries a neutral feeling — equanimity (*upekkhā*).

Then verse 498 names a whole set at once: the *aṭṭhamahāvipākā* — the **eight great resultants**. Unlike the bare senses, these minds have roots: non-greed, non-hate, and sometimes wisdom. And they do a job the small results cannot. They handle *paṭisandhi* — rebirth-linking, the very first mind of a new life — and then keep that life running underneath every waking moment. The commentary says the mind that begins this life, and ours, was one of these eight: the ripened fruit of earlier good deeds.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *If a kind act long ago can shape a whole life, what am I planting today?*

## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### PAṬISANDHI — Rebirth-linking

**Pronunciation:** pa-ti-SAN-dhi — four syllables. Stress the third; the "dh" is a soft, breathy "d."

**In today's passage:** *Paṭisandhi* is the very first mind of a new life — it "links back" to the life before. The commentary says the eight great resultants (*aṭṭhamahāvipākā*) do this job: one of them begins a new sense-sphere life and then keeps it running underneath every moment. It is a result, not a choice — the ripe fruit of earlier good kamma.

**Why it matters:** This is a big claim made plainly: a kind mind cultivated now can shape the start of a whole life later. Whether or not we picture future lives, the lesson lands today — good deeds are not lost; they ripen. Knowing our deeds carry weight is reason enough to do a little more good.
