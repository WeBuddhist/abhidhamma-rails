# Day 58 — The Taints

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today moves through several pairs and arrives at a crucial group: the taints (*āsava*). The word means something that "flows out" or "seeps" — the deep, old habits that ooze through the mind and keep beings turning in the round. There are four: the taint of sensual craving, of wanting to keep existing, of fixed views, and — the deepest — of ignorance.

Along the way the text also pairs the conditioned against the one unconditioned thing, *nibbāna*: everything made of causes is passing; only the unmade is at peace. The taints are exactly what the path drains away. A fully freed person is called *khīṇāsava* — "one whose taints are ended."

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of these deep currents — craving, wanting to be, views, not-knowing — is quietly steering me?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ĀSAVA — The Taints

**Pronunciation:** *AH-sa-va* — "ā" held long, then two light beats.

**In today's passage:** The chapter lays out the four *āsava* — "taints," currents that seep through the mind: the taint of sensual craving, of wanting to keep existing, of fixed views, and of ignorance. The commentary calls them long-fermented, old flows running for ages.

**Why it matters:** The taints are the last things the path removes; full freedom is their ending. You need not tackle all four at once. Just noticing them — the pull to pleasure, the grip on being someone, the clinging to views, the fog of not-knowing — starts to loosen their hold. To see a current is the first step to leaving it.
