# Day 93 — What the Mind Is Holding

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

One verse today, and it closes the chapter. It runs the five heaps through the pairs — root or not, with an object or without — and the answers keep circling one difference. Form has no object (*ārammaṇa*). It does not take anything up. The other four always do: they are always about something.

The commentary draws out what that means in practice. The very same object can be met with greed, with anger, with confusion — or with restraint and clear looking. Craving and composure do not have different objects; they have different responses to the same one. So the practical question is never only "what am I looking at" but "how is my mind holding it." Then the last line: *Khandhavibhaṅgo niṭṭhito* — the Analysis of the Aggregates is finished.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *How is my mind holding what is in front of it right now?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ĀRAMMAṆA — Object

**Pronunciation:** *ā-RAM-ma-ṇa* — four beats; the "ṇa" is a soft "n".

**In today's passage:** *Ārammaṇa* means "object" — whatever the mind takes up and rests on. Today's verse turns on it: form has no object, while the four mental heaps always have one. To feel, to perceive, to intend, to know is always to do so *about* something.

**Why it matters:** The teaching makes a plain point about this. One and the same object can be met with craving, with anger, with confusion — or with restraint and steady looking. The object is not what decides. How the mind takes hold of it is. That is where the work is, and it is available at every object you meet today.
