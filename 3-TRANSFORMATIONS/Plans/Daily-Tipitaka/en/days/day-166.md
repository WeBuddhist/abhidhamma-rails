# Day 166 — The Heart of the Teaching

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today we begin the last chapter of the Vibhaṅga, the Book of Analysis. It is called the heart of the teaching. It sums up the whole book in twelve short questions. How many aggregates (*khandha*, heaps of experience)? How many bases (*āyatana*)? How many elements (*dhātu*), truths, and roots?

The answers come fast: five aggregates, twelve bases, eighteen elements, four truths. The commentary says the Buddha answered just what was asked. That exact fit is a sign of his full knowing. Then the verses ask where these things are found. In the sense-pleasure element (*kāmadhātu*, the world of the five senses), all of them are present. In the material-form element (*rūpadhātu*), there are fewer. Beings there have no nose, tongue, or body sense, so smell, taste, and touch play no part.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *If my whole life fits into twelve groups, which group is busy in me right now?*

**v. 994.** Therein, what are the five aggregates in the material-form element? The aggregate of material form, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of mental formations, the aggregate of consciousness — these are called "the five aggregates in the material-form element". *(…)* Therein, what are the four feelings in the material-form element… the four perceptions… the four intentions… the four minds? Eye-consciousness, ear-consciousness, the mind element, the mind-consciousness element — these are called "the four minds in the material-form element".

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KOṬṬHĀSA — Portion

**Pronunciation:** *koṭ-ṭhā-sa* — three syllables; the "ṭṭh" is a firm "t" with a breath after it. Say "KOT-thaa-sa".

**In today’s passage:** The commentary says this chapter sorts everything into twelve portions (*koṭṭhāsa*). Aggregates, bases, elements, truths — each is one portion of the same whole. From the lowest hell to the highest heaven, nothing falls outside them.

**Why it matters:** When a strong mood comes, try naming its portion. "This is feeling." "This is perception." "This is the mind knowing." A named portion is smaller than a story about me. That small gap gives you room to choose a kind answer over a harsh one.
