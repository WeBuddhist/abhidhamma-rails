# Day 14 — Four Paths, Four Objects, Sixteen Combinations

## 1. Today's Chanting Guide

Today's verses look at the same jhāna under three new lenses. First, the *four modes of progress* (*catasso paṭipadā*): easy or hard, slow or quick. The same first jhāna can come to you through any of these four — and the teaching names each so no one's experience is left out. Verse 180 closes that pass.

Then the *four objects* (*cattāri ārammaṇāni*): small or boundless, holding a small or boundless feel. The kasiṇa disc can be a small patch of earth or a wide field; the mind can sit with it tightly or open out. Verse 185 closes that pass. Finally, verses 186–201 open the *sixteenfold permutation* (*soḷasakkhattukaṃ*) — four paths times four objects, every combination spelled out. Nothing is left to guess.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Of the four ways the path can move — easy, hard, slow, quick — which one feels most like mine right now?*

**v. 201.** What are wholesome states? ## 5. Pāli Word of the Day

### PAṬIPADĀ — Mode of Progress

**Pronunciation:** pa-ti-pa-DAH — four syllables, all short except the final long "ā". Stress the last syllable.

**In today's passage:** Verses 177–180 lay out four modes — *dukkhā* (hard) or *sukhā* (easy), *dandhā* (slow) or *khippā* (quick). The same first jhāna arises through any of these four, depending on the practitioner. Today's verses then mix paṭipadā with object-size, so every real mind finds its own row in the table.

**Why it matters:** The teaching does not say one mode is better. Hard-and-slow is wholesome. Easy-and-quick is wholesome. What matters is that the path is being walked. When practice feels hard today, that is not a failure — it is one of the four named modes. The work is the work, not the speed of the work.
