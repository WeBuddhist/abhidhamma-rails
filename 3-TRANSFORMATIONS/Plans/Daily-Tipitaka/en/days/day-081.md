# Day 81 — Feeling, Counted Many Ways

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the analysis settles on one heap and turns it slowly in the light: feeling (*vedanā*). The Abhidhamma method counts it again and again, each count a different angle.

One way: all feeling comes with contact. Two ways: with a root or without. Three ways: wholesome, unwholesome, or neither. Four ways: by plane of mind. Five ways: by faculty — pleasure, pain, gladness, sadness, equanimity. Six ways: by base. Then ten ways, by the door where the feeling was born — eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind. The commentary asks why so many counts, and answers plainly: different listeners understand in different ways, so the Buddha taught each in the way that would reach them.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I meet one feeling today with real curiosity, instead of just liking or disliking it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### VEDANĀ — Feeling

**Pronunciation:** *ve-da-NĀ* — three beats, stress on the last.

**In today's passage:** *Vedanā* means "feeling" — the pleasant, painful, or neutral tone of an experience, not emotion. Today's verses count it from every side. The commentary adds a useful split: pleasant and painful feeling can be bodily or mental, and bodily feeling always belongs to the sense sphere.

**Why it matters:** Feeling is where practice usually succeeds or fails. Pleasant feeling pulls craving; painful pulls aversion; neutral slides into not-noticing. If you can feel a tone as just a tone — arisen, passing — the pull loses its grip. That is why the text turns this one heap so patiently: it is the hinge.
