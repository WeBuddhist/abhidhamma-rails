# Day 53 — Far and Near

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The fourfold sorting nears its end, sweeping every kind of matter under wide pairs: primary or derived, grasped or not, and — today's key pair — far or near. In the Abhidhamma "near" means the coarse matter that is easy to notice, and "far" means the subtle matter that is hard to see.

The point is that *all* of it, obvious and hidden alike, belongs in the same net. It is conditioned, impermanent, and not a self. Nothing gets a special pass. The practice is to widen the view until it takes in the whole field of matter — the body close at hand and the world far off — and to meet it all as one passing nature.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can my awareness hold all matter — near and far, coarse and subtle — as one passing nature?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### DŪRE & SANTIKE — Far and Near

**Pronunciation:** *DOO-re, SAN-ti-ke* — "ū" held long; light, even beats.

**In today's passage:** The fourfold sorting uses the pair *dūre* (far) and *santike* (near). In the Abhidhamma these are not about distance: "near" is the coarse matter easy to notice, and "far" is the subtle matter hard to see. The sorting keeps placing the two together.

**Why it matters:** It is easy to contemplate the obvious — a strong sensation, a loud sound. The far, subtle matter slips by unseen. Pairing them trains you to include everything: nothing in the body or world is too coarse or too subtle to be seen as impermanent and not-self. A view that leaves nothing out is what ripens into real peace.
