# Day 149 — Wisdom That Moves Forward

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses close the threes and open the groups of four. The fours start with the knowledge that action is one's own, then the knowledge of the path and its four truths. Then they sort wisdom by what it does. Some wisdom builds up more rounds of birth; some takes them apart. Some brings *nibbidā* (disenchantment) with sense pleasures but does not yet see the truths.

The most useful set is about what happens to a meditator's wisdom after deep calm. It can slip back when sense desires creep in. It can get stuck when one clings to the pleasant calm itself. It can move on to finer states. Or it can turn toward letting go and insight. The commentary's warning is gentle but clear. Even good calm, if you cling to it, stops you growing.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Is there something good in my practice that I have started to cling to?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### NIBBIDĀ — Disenchantment

**Pronunciation:** *nib-bi-dā* — three syllables. Say "NIB-bi-daa".

**In today’s passage:** *Nibbidā* is disenchantment — the cooling of fascination with things that cannot truly satisfy. In today’s verses, wisdom that comes with disenchantment is the kind that moves forward, toward letting go, instead of slipping back or standing still.

**Why it matters:** Disenchantment is not gloom. It is the relief of seeing that something was never going to fill you up. When a craving loses its shine, you are a little freer. Noticing even one small pleasure that no longer pulls at you is a sign the mind is growing lighter.
