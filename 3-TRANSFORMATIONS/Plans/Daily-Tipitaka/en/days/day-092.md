# Day 92 — Now Answer the Questions

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The third and last turning begins, and it starts by going back to the beginning. "The five aggregates: form, feeling, perception, formations, consciousness" — the same line the chapter opened with, a hundred and fifty verses ago.

This time the method is question and answer (*pañhāpucchaka*). Of each heap it asks: how many are wholesome? unwholesome? neither? And the answers are exact. Form is always neither — matter carries no moral weight. The other four may be any of the three. Some questions get no clean answer at all, and the text says so plainly: of feeling and perception it "cannot rightly be said" that they are associated with feeling. Where the honest answer is "that question does not apply," the text gives it.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I let a question stand unanswered rather than force a tidy reply?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PAÑHĀPUCCHAKA — The Question Method

**Pronunciation:** *pañ-hā-PUC-cha-ka* — "ñ" as in "canyon"; "cch" like the "tch" in "catch".

**In today's passage:** *Pañhāpucchaka* means "the asking of questions." It is the chapter's third and shortest turning. Instead of listing or counting, it puts a question to each heap — how many are wholesome? what can they take as object? — and answers only what can be answered.

**Why it matters:** Two things are worth taking from this method. First, it asks rather than tells, which is how understanding usually arrives. Second, it is willing to say a question does not apply. Holding a question open, without forcing an answer, is a skill — and the text models it rather than describing it.
