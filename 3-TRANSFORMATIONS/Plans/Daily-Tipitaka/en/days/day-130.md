# Day 130 — Clearing the Ground for Jhāna

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Before the mind can settle into *jhāna* (deep meditative calm), the ground has to be cleared. Today's passage walks through that preparation: living within the training rules, guarding the senses, eating in moderation, staying wakeful, and acting with clear awareness through every ordinary movement.

Then the meditator finds a quiet place, sits, and sets up mindfulness. One by one, five inner obstacles are put down — wanting, ill will, dullness, restlessness, and doubt. The commentary's picture is a builder levelling the land before laying a foundation. Calm is not forced; it is what remains once the clutter is cleared. Virtue and restraint are not a separate project from meditation — they are its first step.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What small restraint would clear a little space in my mind today?*

## 4. 💡 Reading for Meaning

**Verses 508–523.** *(Full text rendered in the WeBuddhist app from the verse library; the first and last verses of the range are printed below so the plan-importer can find the right entries.)*

**v. 508.** Here a monk dwells restrained by the restraint of the monastic code, endowed with proper conduct and proper resort, seeing danger in the slightest faults; he trains himself, having undertaken the rules of training; he guards the doors of the faculties, knows moderation in food, and is devoted to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night, devoted with perseverance and care to the development of the things that side with awakening. He acts with clear comprehension when going forward and returning; he acts with clear comprehension when looking ahead and looking away; he acts with clear comprehension when bending and stretching; he acts with clear comprehension when carrying the outer robe, the bowl, and the robes; he acts with clear comprehension when eating, drinking, chewing, and tasting; he acts with clear comprehension when defecating and urinating; he acts with clear comprehension when walking, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, speaking, and keeping silent. He resorts to a secluded dwelling place — a forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a glen, a mountain cave, a charnel ground, a remote forest, the open air, a heap of straw — with little sound, little noise, without the breath of crowds, hidden from people, suitable for retreat. Gone to the forest, or to the foot of a tree, or to an empty hut, he sits down, having folded his legs crosswise, set his body straight, and established mindfulness in front of him. Having abandoned covetousness with regard to the world, he dwells with a mind free of covetousness; he purifies his mind of covetousness. Having abandoned ill will and hatred, he dwells with a mind free of ill will, compassionate for the welfare of all living beings; he purifies his mind of ill will and hatred. Having abandoned dullness and drowsiness, he dwells free of dullness and drowsiness, percipient of light, mindful and clearly comprehending; he purifies his mind of dullness and drowsiness. Having abandoned restlessness and remorse, he dwells unagitated, with a mind inwardly stilled; he purifies his mind of restlessness and remorse. Having abandoned doubt, he dwells having crossed over doubt, without perplexity about wholesome things; he purifies his mind of doubt. Having abandoned these five hindrances, corruptions of the mind that weaken wisdom — secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome things, he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, which is with thought and with examination, with joy and happiness born of seclusion; with the stilling of thought and examination, he enters and dwells in the second meditative absorption, which has inner confidence and unification of mind, is without thought and without examination, with joy and happiness born of concentration; with the fading away of joy he dwells equanimous, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body — that of which the noble ones declare, "equanimous, mindful, one who dwells happily" — he enters and dwells in the third meditative absorption; with the abandoning of happiness and the abandoning of pain, and with the earlier disappearance of joy and grief, he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant and has purity of mindfulness through equanimity; completely passing beyond perceptions of material form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, not attending to perceptions of diversity, aware that "space is infinite", he enters and dwells in the field of infinite space; completely passing beyond the field of infinite space, aware that "consciousness is infinite", he enters and dwells in the field of infinite consciousness; completely passing beyond the field of infinite consciousness, aware that "there is nothing", he enters and dwells in the field of nothingness; completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, he enters and dwells in the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception.

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SĪLA — Moral Discipline

**Pronunciation:** *sī-la* — a long "ee" sound, then a soft "la".

**In today’s passage:** *Sīla* is moral discipline — the plain work of keeping conduct clean. The passage starts here: restraint within the training rules, guarded senses, moderation in food, and clear awareness in daily acts. All of it comes before a single moment of jhāna.

**Why it matters:** It is easy to treat ethics and meditation as two different things. Today’s text joins them: a settled mind rests on a clean conscience. Fewer regrets to carry means fewer thoughts to fight when you sit. A little more care in how you live today makes the mind easier to quiet tomorrow.
