# Day 133 — Equanimity in the Deeper Calm

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

As the jhānas deepen, even rapture comes to feel a little coarse and is allowed to fade. What remains is *upekkhā* (equanimity) — a balanced, unshaken awareness — together with a quiet happiness and clear presence. Today's verses describe this third stage, where one "dwells equanimous, mindful, and clearly comprehending", and then the fourth, "neither painful nor pleasant", with mindfulness made pure by that balance.

The passage even pauses to explain who "the noble ones" are and what it means simply to "dwell". The path here is one of steady release: each stage lets go of something the last one still held. Calm is not a blank; it is presence without pushing or pulling.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where in my day could I meet things with a little less push and pull?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### UPEKKHĀ — Equanimity

**Pronunciation:** *u-pek-khā* — "khā" is a soft, breathy "k" then a long "aa".

**In today’s passage:** *Upekkhā* is equanimity — an even, balanced awareness that neither grabs at pleasure nor pushes away pain. In the deeper jhānas it becomes the mind’s home base: rapture fades, and this steady balance carries a pure, quiet mindfulness.

**Why it matters:** Equanimity is not coldness or not-caring. It is the strength to stay present without being yanked around by liking and disliking. Practised in small ways — a short pause before reacting — it makes every hard moment a little more workable. A steadier mind harms less and sees more.
