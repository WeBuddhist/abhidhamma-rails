# Day 154 — Keeping Score

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today's verses keep naming small faults, and many are about wanting. Great desire is never having enough. Evil desire is wanting to look better than you are — lazy, but hoping to be seen as hard-working. The commentary says three things can never be filled: a fire with fuel, the ocean with water, and a greedy person with gifts.

Next come the ways people trick others for gain: putting on a show, flattering, hinting, and putting others down. Sleepiness after meals and a dull mind are named too. Then the verses turn to *māna*, conceit. It has three shapes: "I am better," "I am equal," and "I am worse." Each can be held by someone who really is better, equal, or worse. That makes nine kinds.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Where do I try to look better than I really am?*

Here someone is worse on account of birth, or clan, or good family… or on one ground or another, and he regards himself as equal to others; relying on that, he nurses conceit. ## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### MĀNA — Conceit

**Pronunciation:** *mā-na* — a long "aa", then a short "na". Say "MAA-na".

**In today’s passage:** *Māna* is conceit — the habit of measuring yourself against others. The verses show three shapes: "I am better," "I am equal," and "I am worse." Even feeling less than others is conceit, because it still keeps score.

**Why it matters:** We often think pride only means feeling superior. But a low opinion of yourself also holds up a measuring stick. The commentary says even a true sense of "I am better" ends only at full awakening. So be patient. Just notice the scorekeeping when it starts, and set it down.
