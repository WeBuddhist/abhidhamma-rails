# Day 61 — The Hindrances

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today the chapter reaches the group every meditator meets first: the hindrances (*nīvaraṇa*). There are six — sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, doubt, and ignorance. They are called hindrances because they *cover* the mind, like clouds over the moon, and block both calm and clear seeing.

The good news is that each has a known antidote, which the tradition spells out: meet craving with the body's plainness, ill will with kindness, dullness with light and energy, restlessness with calm, and doubt with clear understanding. The day then turns to misapprehension — clinging to "only this is true." Underneath both lies one move: setting down what covers the mind so it can settle and see.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which cloud is over my mind today — and what would clear it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### NĪVARAṆA — The Hindrances

**Pronunciation:** *NEE-va-ra-na* — even, light beats.

**In today's passage:** The chapter lists the *nīvaraṇa*, "hindrances": sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, doubt, and — added here — ignorance. They are named for what they do: cover the mind and block both calm and insight.

**Why it matters:** This is the most practical list for anyone who sits to meditate. When the mind won't settle, it is almost always one of these. The tradition pairs each with an antidote — craving with seeing the body plainly, ill will with kindness, dullness with rousing energy, restlessness with calm, doubt with study. Naming the hindrance in the moment is half the cure; then you apply its remedy and the mind clears.
