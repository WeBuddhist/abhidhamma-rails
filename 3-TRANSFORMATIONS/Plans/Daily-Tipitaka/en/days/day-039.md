# Day 39 — Seen, Heard, Sensed, Cognized

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The *mātikā* (the matrix, the list of sorting categories for material form) continues, and today it sorts by fours. Most of the groups pair up technical opposites — derived and underived, near and far. But the set closes with four plain words that gather the whole sensed world: the *seen*, the *heard*, the *sensed*, and the *cognized* (*diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ*).

Everything that reaches you reaches you as one of these. The teaching here is small but sharp. When you meet a sight or a sound, can you let the seen be just the seen, without piling on liking, disliking, and stories? That guarding of the senses keeps unwholesome states from flooding in. It is a short day in the text — one verse — but a strong one for daily life.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *In the seen, can there be just the seen?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### DIṬṬHA · SUTA · MUTA · VIÑÑĀTA — Seen, Heard, Sensed, Cognized

**Pronunciation:** *DIT-ta, SU-ta, MU-ta, VIÑ-ñā-ta* — "ñ" is like the "ny" in "canyon."

**In today's passage:** These four words close the fourfold sorting of material form. The *seen* is what the eye meets, the *heard* what the ear meets, the *sensed* what nose, tongue, and body meet, and the *cognized* what the mind meets. Together they cover the whole world as it comes to you.

**Why it matters:** This is a doorway to sense-restraint (*indriyasaṃvara* — guarding the senses). When you notice "this is only the seen, only the heard," you stop the rush from bare contact into craving and dislike. Met that way, the senses bring less trouble, and the mind stays clearer through the day.
